Source of Truth, and the Three Things People Get Wrong

The shipped mockups in mockups/ and their styles.css are the source of truth. This document explains them. If a value here disagrees with the stylesheet, the stylesheet wins and this document needs regenerating. Flag it to Curtis rather than guessing.

The three traps on this specific build
  • There are two reds. #a90606 and #d81f27 are not interchangeable and neither one is a mistake. See section 01.
  • Buttons on dark use a WHITE outline, not a red one. This is deliberate and approved. Do not "correct" it. See section 03.
  • Border radius is 0 everywhere, except testimonial photos, which are circles. That single exception is a direct client request. See section 05.

The two-tone red, and why it exists

This register was built for a client whose accent was gold. Gold sits at high luminance, so a single gold value worked on white and on black. Crimson does not have that luxury. The client's brand red, #a90606, is a deep, dark red. It is superb on white and it collapses on black. One value cannot serve both surfaces, so the system carries two, and each one has exactly one job.

The rule, in one line

Light background, use #a90606. Dark background, use #d81f27. Both are Jose's red. The split is not a compromise, it is the only way a dark crimson can carry a high-contrast register. In the CSS this is already wired: --color-accent-dark is the on-light token and --color-accent is the on-dark token. If you are reaching for a red and you are not sure which one, look at what is behind it.

The one shared job: #d81f27 is the hover fill on both surfaces. A primary button on white sits in #a90606 outline and text, then fills bright #d81f27 on hover. That is intentional. The brighter red is what gives the hover its snap.

Color hard rules
  • Body copy on dark is white or rgba(255,255,255,0.85). Never crimson. Crimson on dark is for display type and rules only.
  • Never introduce a second brand color. No blue, no gold, no green. Crimson, black, paper.
  • Never use crimson as a large flat background fill. It appears as outlines, small type, thin rules, and hover states.
  • Hover states shift value, never hue. Nothing in this palette turns orange or pink under interaction.
  • Two dark sections never stack back to back. Break them with section.alt paper or white.
02

Type Scale

Two families. Anton is the display face and it is display only. Inter does everything else, weights 300 through 700. Every size below is a CSS clamp(), so it is fluid between mobile and desktop. Resize this window and the specimens below move with it. That is the intended behavior, do not replace the clamps with fixed pixel sizes in Astra or Spectra.

Two rules people miss
  • H1 and H2 are UPPERCASE by default. This is set in CSS with text-transform: uppercase, not typed into the content. Write headings in normal title case in the WordPress editor and let the stylesheet do the transform, so the copy stays readable in the admin and in search results.
  • Anton is display only. It is a single weight, all caps, tight fitting face. It carries hero H1s, section H2s inside dark bands, card names, stat numbers, and brand moments. It never sets a paragraph, never sets a form label, never sets navigation, and never sets an FAQ answer. Anton has no italic and no other weights, so do not ask it for any.

Typography hard rules
  • Load both faces from Google Fonts on every page: Anton and Inter at weights 300, 400, 500, 600, 700. Nothing else.
  • The client asked for Calibri. It was overridden with Curtis's approval on 2026-07-21 because Calibri is a system UI font with no display weight and it would flatten the whole register. Do not reintroduce it.
  • Never add a third family. No serif accent face, no script, no icon font.
  • No italic serif lift spans inside headings. "Real estate, reimagined" is banned.
  • Keep the clamps. Astra global typography and Spectra block typography both accept clamp values, use them rather than setting separate desktop, tablet, and mobile pixel sizes.
  • No em dashes anywhere in copy. Use commas, periods, colons, or a middle dot.
03

Buttons

Three variants, all sharing the same shell: transparent fill, 1px outline, uppercase Inter 600 at 0.8125rem with 0.12em tracking, padding 1rem 2.5rem, zero radius, transition 400ms cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1) on background, color, and border color. Only the colors change between them. Hover over the live buttons below to see the real transitions.

On white and paper backgrounds
Schedule A Consultation btn btn-primary

Transparent, outline and label in brand red #a90606. Hover fills bright #d81f27 with the label flipping to black #0a0a0a, border matches the fill. This is the default call to action everywhere on a light section.

Browse Listings btn btn-secondary

Transparent, outline and label in black #0a0a0a. Hover fills black with a white label. Use as the second option when two actions carry equal weight, never as the only button in a section.

On dark and deep black backgrounds
Start Your Search btn btn-on-dark

Transparent, white label, outline rgba(255,255,255,0.85). Hover fills bright #d81f27 with a white label and a matching crimson border. Used in the hero, the lead bar, the specialty panel, and every dark conversion band.

Do Not Do This wrong

The same button with a #a90606 outline and label, which is what the universal convention would produce. Look at it against the black. That is the whole argument.

Documented deviation, approved by Curtis 2026-07-21

The AgenticPress universal convention is accent outline on every button, on every surface. This build breaks that rule in exactly one place: .btn-on-dark uses a white outline, not a crimson one.

The reason is the same reason the palette carries two reds. A #a90606 outline on black sits at roughly 2.6 to 1 contrast. Over the hero photograph it effectively disappears, and against the solid black bands it reads as a muddy smudge rather than a button. White outline restores the punch the register depends on, and the accent still owns the interaction because the hover fill is crimson.

A builder who does not know this will "fix" it back to crimson. It is not broken. Leave it white. If it ever needs revisiting, that is a conversation with Curtis, not a silent correction.

Button hard rules
  • Border radius 0. Always. There are no rounded buttons anywhere on this site.
  • :hover and :focus-visible share the same styling. Keyboard users must see the same state mouse users see. Do not drop the focus rule when rebuilding in Spectra.
  • One primary per hero. Two buttons maximum in any hero or band. Never three.
  • Never fill a button crimson at rest. Crimson fill is the hover state and only the hover state.
  • Form submits use the same button classes. The lead bar submit is .btn.btn-primary sitting on black, which is intentional, it is the one place the red-outlined button appears on a dark surface because it is a submit control inside a form group, not a standalone CTA.

Sharp Geometry, and the One Exception

Border radius is 0 across the entire site. Buttons, cards, images, form fields, modals, badges, panels, nav dropdowns, everything. The register depends on it. Rounded corners soften the build into something generic and undo the bold structure the rest of the system is doing.

The single exception: testimonial photos are circular

Jose asked for this explicitly in his Content Snare submission, in his own words, a circular frame. It is the only rounded shape on the site, and it applies to testimonial client photos only. Not headshots, not team member photos, not community cards, not listing images.

JM
Correctborder-radius: 50% with a 2px #d81f27 ring. 72px in the featured testimonial, 56px in the card grid.
JM
Wrong hereSquare is correct everywhere else on the site, but not in a testimonial.

The CSS that carries this is a single rule targeting .testimonial-card__photo and .testimonial-featured__photo. When you rebuild the testimonial block in Spectra, the circular mask and the 2px accent ring both need to survive the port.

Geometry hard rules
  • Radius 0 everywhere except testimonial client photos.
  • No drop shadows as decoration. The only shadows in the system are functional: a subtle lift on testimonial card hover, a nav shadow once the bar goes solid on scroll, and the modal shadows.
  • Accent rules are square: a 3px or 4px crimson top or left border on panels, never a rounded pill or badge.
  • Images fill their frames with object-fit: cover inside a fixed aspect ratio box. No visible image borders except the testimonial ring.

Motion

Slow, deliberate, and sparing. Nothing bounces, nothing springs, nothing spins. Four effects carry the whole site. All four are live below, hover or press replay to run them.

Scroll reveal

First element, no delay
Second element, 90ms
Third element, 180ms

opacity 0 to 1 plus translateY(26px) to 0 over 750ms with cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1). Driven by an IntersectionObserver at a 0.12 threshold, firing once per element. Stagger comes from data-reveal-delay values of 1, 2, and 3, which map to 90ms, 180ms, and 270ms.

Hero Ken Burns

Hero background

The hero background image scales from 1 to 1.09 over 22 seconds, ease-out, forwards, so it settles and holds rather than looping. Home page hero only. It is slow enough that most visitors register it as depth rather than as an animation.

Count-up on credentials

0

Credential and stat numbers animate from 0 to their target over 1400ms, triggered when the strip enters the viewport. There is a setTimeout fallback so a throttled or background tab still lands on the final value instead of freezing partway. Home hero credentials (1,200+ families, 20+ years, 4.9 stars) and the Meet The Team stat bar.

  • No bounce, no spring, no elastic easing. No spinning loaders inside content.
  • Photo hover scale stops at 1.04. Higher reads as a gimmick.
  • Scroll reveals fire once. Do not re-hide an element when it leaves the viewport.
  • The count-up must have a fallback that sets the final value. A visitor who lands on a stat frozen at 437 is worse than no animation at all.
  • No carousels or auto-rotating sliders in the hero.

Hard Rules and Anti-Patterns

Client-specific. Every line below came out of Jose's brief, his Content Snare submission, or a decision Curtis made during this build. These override general habit.

Never, on this build
  • No em dashes. Anywhere. Not in copy, not in headings, not in alt text, not in HTML comments. Use a comma, a period, a colon, or a middle dot. This one has bitten this build twice already.
  • No second accent color. Crimson, black, paper. No blue, no gold, no green, no gradient brand color.
  • No rounded corners, with the single exception of testimonial client photos.
  • No hand-built IDX interface. Jose runs iHomefinder / Optima Express, not Showcase IDX. We do not control that UI. Listing search, listing grids, and map search are shortcode zones only. Never design listing cards.
  • No hand-built home valuation form. The client has his own valuation widget plugin. The valuation page carries a labeled placeholder zone and nothing else.
  • No fabricated statistics. No invented sale counts, no invented average days on market, no invented price data, no invented reviews. Only what Content Snare supplied or research verified.
  • No stats that go stale. Homes sold in the last 12 months is deliberately not published, the numbers on hand disagree. Only evergreen credentials appear: 20+ years, 1,200+ families, Top 1%, 4.9 stars across 91 reviews.
  • No market data on the home page. Market updates belong on the blog where they can be dated.
  • No "Recently Sold Nearby" block on the home valuation page.
  • No superlative claims. No "best rated", no "5 star realtor", no "number one agent". They read as puffery and the keyword research showed no search volume behind them.
  • No filler phrasing. No "dream home", no "your real estate journey", no "every step of the way", no "seamless experience", no "nestled", no "vibrant", no "thriving".
  • No emoji as icons. Inline SVG only, stroke width 1.5, currentColor so it inherits the right red.
  • No employment implication for partners. Matt Hughes at Security National Mortgage and Patti Madone at First American Title are outside partners, not Vantegic staff. Label them as partners.
Always, on this build
  • The footer is identical on all 13 pages. Build it once, reuse it, never fork it per page.
  • No "Home" link in the navigation. The logo is the home link.
  • Contact is the right-side CTA in the nav, not a plain menu item.
  • Terms of Use and Privacy Policy are both live and both linked from the footer.
  • Equal Housing Opportunity and REALTOR logos plus the compliance block appear in the footer, along with HomeSmart, Colorado license ER.100025793, and the PPMLS IDX disclaimer.
  • Minimum three contextual internal links per page. No orphan pages.
  • Eight steps in the buyer and seller process blocks. Six questions in each FAQ.
  • Every image gets descriptive alt text. Never blank, never the word "image", never keyword stuffed.
  • Real photography only. Client-supplied assets first: the crimson shield logo, his headshot, his Colorado Springs listing exterior, and the ten named client photos.
  • Several client testimonial photos are 5 to 6MB. Downsize them before launch.
  • Voice is first person. Jose speaks for himself. Credential-forward without bragging, equity protection as the through line, bilingual service treated as a fact rather than a slogan.
