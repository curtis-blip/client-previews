Buyer’s Guide

Georgia First Time Home Buyer Guide

Eight steps from pre-approval to keys in north metro Atlanta, whether it is your first home or your fifth.

How it works

A Clear Path from Pre-Approval to Keys

Buying in north metro Atlanta moves quickly when the plan is set before the first tour.

Here is how Sarah takes buyers through it, step by step, with the parts of a Georgia purchase that surprise people who have bought elsewhere.

01

Step 1 of 8

Get Pre-Approved

A lender reviews your income, credit and savings and tells you how much you can borrow. Settle on the monthly payment you are comfortable with first, then carry the pre-approval letter with every offer.

02

Step 2 of 8

Sign a Buyer Agreement and Set Your Plan

Before you tour homes, you and Sarah sign a written buyer agreement that spells out her services and how she is paid; the terms are negotiable. Then set your price range, your must-haves and the towns to focus on.

03

Step 3 of 8

Choose Your Towns

Each of Sarah’s ten towns has its own guide with live listings, school districts and what to check before you buy. Start with the communities and narrow it down.

04

Step 4 of 8

Tour Homes

Sarah sets up a saved search that sends new matches as they list, then tours homes with you and tells you what she sees: the condition, the lot, the street.

05

Step 5 of 8

Write the Offer

Georgia offers are usually written on the Georgia REALTORS® purchase and sale agreement: price, earnest money, financing, the due diligence period and the closing date. Sarah helps you set each term to compete without overreaching.

06

Step 6 of 8

Due Diligence and Inspections

During the due diligence period you inspect the home, review the property and negotiate repairs. In a standard Georgia contract you can walk away for any reason before that period ends.

07

Step 7 of 8

Appraisal and Loan Approval

Your lender orders an appraisal and finishes underwriting. If the appraisal comes in low, Sarah walks you through your options under the contract.

08

Step 8 of 8

Close with a Closing Attorney

Georgia closings are run by a closing attorney, who prepares the documents and records the deed. After a final walk-through you sign, the loan funds and you get the keys. Then file for your county’s homestead exemption: Sarah’s guide covers Forsyth County step by step.

Questions

Buyer Questions

Do I need a pre-approval before I tour homes?

It is the best place to start. It sets your price range, and sellers expect to see one with an offer.

What is a buyer agreement, and what does it cost me?

It is a written agreement, signed before you tour homes, that sets out what Sarah will do for you and how she is paid. Compensation is negotiable, and it can be paid by you, by the seller as part of the deal, or a mix.

What is the due diligence period?

A window written into a Georgia offer, a set number of days, for inspections and research. In a standard contract you can terminate for any reason before it ends.

How much earnest money should I offer?

It depends on the price and the competition. Sarah will suggest an amount that shows you are serious without tying up more than you need.

Who runs the closing in Georgia?

A closing attorney. Georgia closings are conducted by an attorney, who prepares the documents and records the deed.

When do I file for the homestead exemption?

After you move in. File with your county by April 1 for it to apply that year; Sarah’s guide covers Forsyth County.
