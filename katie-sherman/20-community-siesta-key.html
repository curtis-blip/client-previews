Siesta Key at a glance

An Eight-Mile Island, Two Bridges From the Mainland

Siesta Key is an eight-mile island off Sarasota, reached by two bridges from the mainland. Siesta Beach sits on its Gulf side, Visit Sarasota describes its sand as nearly pure quartz, and Dr. Beach named it the number one beach in America in 2011 and again in 2017.

Early maps called the island Clam Island, then Sarasota Key. In 1907 three developers, Harry Higel among them, platted a subdivision on the north end and named it Siesta, and the name slowly spread to the whole island. The first bridge opened in 1917; before that, you reached the key by boat.

01

Siesta Beach and the quartz sand

Siesta Beach is the main public beach on the key. Visit Sarasota describes its sand as nearly pure quartz and lists its national rankings: the number one beach in America from Dr. Beach in 2011 and 2017, and Tripadvisor’s number one beach in the U.S. in 2015, 2017, 2020 and 2025. The pavilion has beach volleyball, a playground, restrooms, showers and a concession, and parking there is free. A Mobi-Mat about 400 feet long runs from the pavilion onto the sand, and beach wheelchairs are offered through the concessionaire from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m.

02

Siesta Key Village, the Sunday market and the drum circle

Siesta Key Village is the island’s small downtown, with boutiques, bars and restaurants for beachside dinners and souvenir shopping. The Siesta Key Farmers Market sets up in the Village on Sunday mornings from 8 a.m. to noon, and on Sunday evenings the drum circle gathers on the beach. Visit Sarasota also points to Sunset Point, known as Patriots Pier, for watching the sun go down over the Gulf. The Village merchants first met as the Siesta Village Association in 1972, the year of the first Siesta Fiesta.

03

Crescent Beach, Point of Rocks and Turtle Beach

South of the main beach, Crescent Beach is quieter, and Point of Rocks sits at its south end. Visit Sarasota calls Point of Rocks the largest rock formation on Florida’s Gulf Coast, and people snorkel there for the coral formations and marine life. At the far south end of the key, Turtle Beach has picnic and grilling areas, a pavilion with restrooms and showers, a campground and free parking. Sea turtles nest on these beaches from May 1 to October 31, and visitors are asked to give the nests room.

04

Condos, beach houses and homes on the water

The key has been a beach-house address since the 1920s, when developers promoted what is now Siesta Public Beach and prominent Sarasota families built beach houses there. Today Ryan and Katie see homes on the key bought as full-time homes, second homes and investments, from a condo near the Village to a house on the water. Siesta Key is not a city: it is a census designated place in unincorporated Sarasota County, so county rules apply, and the Sarasota County Property Appraiser has the record for any parcel. Sarasota Condos sets Siesta Key condos beside those downtown and on Lido Key and Longboat Key.

05

Bridges, the free trolley and parking

Two bridges connect the key to the mainland, and the route from U.S. 41 crosses the Stickney Point drawbridge. On the island, Breeze Transit’s Route 77 Siesta Islander is a free, open-air trolley between Siesta Key Village and Turtle Beach; it ran as the Siesta Key Breeze until Sarasota County renamed it in May 2024. Parking is free at the Siesta Beach pavilion and at Turtle Beach, and other lots mix free and metered spaces.

Questions

Siesta Key FAQ

Is Siesta Key its own city?

No. Siesta Key is a census designated place in unincorporated Sarasota County, so the county provides services and sets the rules there. Check any parcel with the Sarasota County Property Appraiser.

Which school district serves Siesta Key?

Sarasota County Schools. No public school campus sits inside the Census boundary of Siesta Key, so confirm the school for any address with the district, since attendance zones change.

How has Siesta Beach been ranked?

Visit Sarasota lists Siesta Beach as Dr. Beach's number one beach in America in 2011 and 2017, and as Tripadvisor's number one beach in the U.S. in 2015, 2017, 2020 and 2025. Dr. Beach is Dr. Stephen Leatherman of Florida International University.

Is there a free trolley on Siesta Key?

Yes. Breeze Transit's Route 77 Siesta Islander is free and open-air, and runs between Siesta Key Village and Turtle Beach. It ran as the Siesta Key Breeze until May 2024.
