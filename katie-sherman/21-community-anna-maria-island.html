Anna Maria Island at a glance

Three Small Cities on a Seven-Mile Island

Anna Maria Island is seven miles long, with seven miles of beaches, by the count of the Bradenton Area Convention and Visitors Bureau. Three cities share it, north to south: Anna Maria, Holmes Beach and Bradenton Beach, with one chamber of commerce and one community center, The Center, serving all three.

Spanish explorers found the island in 1530, and George Emerson Bean built the first permanent homestead on its northern tip in 1893. Sam and Annie Cobb homesteaded in what is now Holmes Beach in 1896, and the island's first post office opened in their home in 1902. Visitors came by boat until 1921, when the first bridge reached Bridge Street from the village of Cortez.

01

Anna Maria, at the north end

The City of Anna Maria holds the island’s northern tip. The City Pier, which looks out over Tampa Bay toward the Sunshine Skyway Bridge, is closed for rebuilding after the 2024 storms, with reopening expected in late 2026; next door Bayfront Park has pavilions, grills, a playground and restrooms with showers beside Anna Maria Public Beach. Bean Point, at the very tip, is named for George Emerson Bean, who homesteaded there in 1893; it has no parking lot, facilities or lifeguards, and the visitors bureau advises against swimming there because of the currents. The Anna Maria Island Historical Society Museum fills a 1920 icehouse, with the Belle Haven Cottage and the 1927 city jail on its grounds.

02

Holmes Beach, the middle of the island

Holmes Beach is the island’s middle city. Manatee Public Beach, at the center of the island, has lifeguards, restrooms and showers, a playground, beach volleyball courts and a beach cafe. Sam and Annie Cobb homesteaded here in 1896, the island’s first post office opened in their home in 1902, and in 1948 Hollywood filmed parts of On an Island with You here. The Anna Maria Island Bridge, which carries Manatee Avenue (State Road 64) over from the mainland, lands in Holmes Beach.

03

Bradenton Beach, Bridge Street and Coquina Beach

In Bradenton Beach, Bridge Street is the center of the Historic Old Town District, where the island’s first bridge from the mainland landed in 1921. It has shops, restaurants and a Sunday market with live music, and the Historic Bridge Street Pier, rebuilt in a project finished in January 2015, has a restaurant, a shop and a floating day dock, with the Richard P. Suhre Pavilion at its bay end. South of town, Coquina Beach is named for the small, colorful mollusks at the water’s edge, and people come for the shells and shark’s teeth. The free Old Town Tram runs from public lots to the beach.

04

Homes in three cities, each with its own rules

Ryan and Katie see homes on the island bought for full-time living, as second homes and as investments, and each part of it has its own personality. Anna Maria, Holmes Beach and Bradenton Beach are separate cities, each with its own government, so the city an address sits in decides which rules apply: look up the parcel first, then that city’s own site, before you plan any work on a home. The City of Anna Maria calls the island the nursery of choice for hundreds of endangered loggerhead turtles each year; Anna Maria Island Turtle Watch and Shorebird Monitoring volunteers protect the nests, and nesting season runs from May 1 to October 31.

05

Bridges, the free trolley and the ferry

Two bridges reach the island from the mainland: the Anna Maria Island Bridge on Manatee Avenue (State Road 64) lands in Holmes Beach, and the Cortez Bridge on Cortez Road (State Road 684) lands in Bradenton Beach. A third bridge at the south end crosses to Longboat Key. The Florida Department of Transportation is replacing the Cortez Bridge in two phases, building the new north half first while traffic keeps moving. On the island, the Anna Maria Island Trolley is free and runs every day of the year from 6 a.m. to 10:30 p.m. along Gulf Drive between the City Pier and Coquina Beach, and the Gulf Islands Ferry runs about 50 minutes each way from the Bradenton Riverwalk to the Bridge Street Pier and Coquina Beach.

Questions

Anna Maria Island FAQ

Which city is an Anna Maria Island address in?

One of three: Anna Maria at the north end, Holmes Beach in the middle or Bradenton Beach to the south. Each is its own city with its own government and rules, so confirm the city for a parcel with the Manatee County Property Appraiser, then check that city's site.

Is the island trolley free?

Yes. The Anna Maria Island Trolley, Manatee County's Route 5, is free and runs every day of the year from 6 a.m. to 10:30 p.m. along Gulf Drive between the Anna Maria City Pier and Coquina Beach, about every 20 minutes.

Which school district serves Anna Maria Island?

The Manatee County School District serves all three island cities. Confirm the school for any address with the district, since attendance zones change.

How do you get onto the island?

By two bridges from the mainland: Manatee Avenue (State Road 64) to Holmes Beach and Cortez Road (State Road 684) to Bradenton Beach. A third bridge at the south end connects to Longboat Key, and the Gulf Islands Ferry runs from the Bradenton Riverwalk to the Bridge Street Pier and Coquina Beach.
