How It Works

Three steps from question to answer

The home valuation process is simple, but the work behind it is not. Here's what happens after you hit submit.

01 The Form

Submit your details

Fill out the short form with your address, basic specs, and a note on any recent renovations. The renovation field matters more than people realize, a refreshed kitchen or a new roof can shift the comp picture by tens of thousands of dollars.

It takes about two minutes. No account to create, no follow-up funnel. Just the information needed to do the work properly.

02 The Work

Michael pulls comps and analyzes

This is where the work happens. Michael pulls active, pending, and sold comparables from the last 90 days inside your subdivision and the surrounding mile, then layers in days-on-market trends and active inventory pressure.

Renovations are weighted against current buyer expectations for your price band. The output is a written valuation range with the reasoning behind it, not a single number from an algorithm.

03 The Handoff

You get a written report and a follow-up call

The report lands in your inbox, usually within 24 hours. It includes the valuation range, the comps that drove it, and a short note on what is happening in your specific market right now.

If you want to talk it through, Michael will book a 20 minute call to walk through the numbers and answer questions. If you do not, no follow-up sequence will chase you. That is the deal.

Algorithm estimates use national models with sparse local data. They miss subdivisions, school district lines, golf course frontage, and any renovation that does not show up in tax records. A local valuation pulls actual sold comps from the last 90 days inside your specific market, then weights them against your home's condition and upgrades. The range is usually tighter and the reasoning is auditable.
