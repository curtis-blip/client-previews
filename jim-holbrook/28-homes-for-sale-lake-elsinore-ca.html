Lake Elsinore at a glance

A town that grew up around its lake

Lake Elsinore surrounds the largest natural freshwater lake in Southern California, with Interstate 15 running through it between Corona and Murrieta. It became a city on April 20, 1888, while the valley was still part of San Diego County.

The name came from Hamlet: Margaret Collier Graham, one of the founders, chose Elsinore for its sound, and voters renamed it the City of Lake Elsinore in 1972. The lake rises and falls with the rain, and its history of drought and flood shapes what a buyer near the water should check.

01

A lake that rises and falls

Lake Elsinore sits at the low end of the 780-square-mile San Jacinto River watershed, so it lives on rain and runoff. It dried out completely in the 1950s and was first filled with Colorado River water in 1964. After floods in the early 1980s, the Lake Elsinore Management Project built an earthen levee, wells and new channels to cut evaporation and flood damage.

The City counts 1,240 feet above sea level as the lake's optimal level, and in dry years the water drops below it. About five million gallons of recycled water from Elsinore Valley Municipal Water District go in each day to offset evaporation. The lake covers about 3,000 acres.

02

Living near the water

California State Parks owned the lake until 1993, when the lake and its water rights passed to the City and Elsinore Valley Municipal Water District. The City posts water-quality advisories on its Lake Watch page, with a link to the lake-level readings. The Lake Elsinore and San Jacinto Watersheds Authority, a joint agency of the City, Canyon Lake, Riverside County and two water agencies, funds water-quality and habitat projects.

Building near the shore has its own rules. Inside the ring of Grand Avenue, Riverside Drive, Lakeshore Drive, Mission Trail and Corydon Road, the City's floodplain code keeps the foundation of a new home or a major addition at 1,264 feet above sea level or higher, plus up to three feet for wave run-up. A shoreline owner who wants to launch a boat from home, or moor it overnight, buys the City's Lakefront Use Pass.

03

Downtown began as a spa town

In 1883 Franklin Heald bought the old Machado rancho, and with Donald Graham, William Collier and Margaret Collier Graham he began selling lots for a town whose mineral springs drew visitors from the start. The Crescent Bath House, built in 1887 and later known as The Chimes, still stands downtown; the City counts it and the Armory Hall among its buildings on the National Register of Historic Places.

The Machado family's adobe, once a relay station on the Butterfield stage route, still stands near Grand Avenue, as does the Santa Fe depot at Graham Avenue and Spring Street. The Downtown Elsinore Specific Plan now governs what is built there.

04

Neighborhoods built to a plan

The City has more than twenty Specific Plans, each setting the rules for what is built in its area. Canyon Hills, Tuscany Hills, East Lake and the Alberhill plans are among them.

Parcels in some neighborhoods also pay special taxes to a Community Facilities District, known as Mello-Roos, on top of the regular property tax. The City's online finder shows, by address or parcel number, which City districts a parcel is in, this year's tax and the year it ends.

What Jim would check in Lake Elsinore

  • Flood and fire maps Look the address up on FEMA's flood maps and on CAL FIRE's fire hazard maps. Near the lake, the City's Engineering Division can tell you whether a parcel is in a special flood hazard area and whether an elevation certificate is on file.
  • Plans for the shoreline Before you plan an addition near the water, check the parcel's elevation against the City's floodplain rules.
  • City or county Lakeland Village, on the southwest shore, is unincorporated Riverside County, as are Meadowbrook and other communities around the city. County zoning and permits apply there, not the City's.
  • Mello-Roos Look the parcel up in the City's special assessment finder. A special tax on the county bill can make two similar houses cost different amounts each month.

Questions

Lake Elsinore FAQ

Who looks after Lake Elsinore?

The City and Elsinore Valley Municipal Water District took over the lake and its water rights from California State Parks in 1993. The City posts lake conditions on its Lake Watch page.

Which school district serves Lake Elsinore?

Lake Elsinore Unified School District, which also serves Wildomar, Canyon Lake and nearby unincorporated communities. Its online locator lists the schools for a street address, so confirm a specific address there.

Is a house near the lake in a flood zone?

It depends on the parcel. FEMA's flood maps show the special flood hazard areas, and the City's Engineering Division can tell you whether a property is in one and whether an elevation certificate is on file.

How far is Lake Elsinore from Orange County and the airports?

By road from downtown, John Wayne Airport is about 51 miles away, Ontario International about 36 miles and downtown Riverside about 34 miles.
