First Time Home Buyer Guide for Arizona

Written for first-time buyers and veterans across Apache Junction, Mesa and the East Valley.

01 Before you start

Buying here is eight steps, and you will know where you stand at each one

Most of the people I represent are buying their first house, or using a VA loan for the first time. Both come with parts nobody explains until you are already in them.

So here is the whole process laid out before you call anyone. Read it, and you will know what happens next at every stage, what it costs, and where the decisions actually get made. Questions about representation and agreements are answered further down this page.

02 How buying works

Eight steps, no surprises

Step 01

Get the financing straight

Talk to a lender before we tour. Pre-approval tells us the real number, and it means an offer can move the same day we find the house.

Step 02

Build a shortlist, not a map

Filtered by commute, school boundary and resale pattern before we drive anywhere. Touring blind wastes weekends.

Step 03

Read the HOA before you fall for the kitchen

Reserves, assessment history and what the minutes actually say. A healthy HOA and a broke one look identical from the street.

Step 04

Check the district, not the rumour

Pinal and Maricopa split this valley, and utility districts change what two identically priced houses cost every year.

Step 05

Write from closed comparables

The offer is priced off what actually sold within half a mile in the last sixty days, not off the list price next door.

Step 06

Inspect like we mean it

Roof, foundation, drainage, and the pool equipment if there is one. The report is leverage, not paperwork.

Step 07

Hold the appraisal together

If the number lands low we renegotiate from evidence. This is where VA and FHA buyers most often lose a house, and where having done it before matters.

Step 08

Close without surprises

Final walkthrough, closing figures reviewed line by line, and keys. You will know what you are signing before you sit down.

06 Questions

Representation, in plain language

The parts of a buyer agreement people are least comfortable asking about. Click any question to open it.

What am I actually signing?

A buyer representation agreement names me as your agent, for how long, and in which area. It is not a mortgage application and it is not an offer on a house.

How do you get paid?

My fee is written into that agreement before we tour anything. Sometimes the seller covers it, sometimes you do, and we settle which before you see a house.

Can I get out of it?

Yes. The term and the cancellation are both in writing, and I will read you both lines before you sign either.

Do I have to sign just to look?

For a private showing, yes, that is the rule now. For an open house, no.

I am a veteran. Does that change anything?

It changes what you can borrow and what you pay at closing. VA financing has no down payment and no monthly mortgage insurance, and there are sellers who do not understand it. Part of my job is making sure that never costs you the house.

This is my first house. Where do I start?

With financing, not with listings. Talk to a lender first so we know the real number, then we look. Touring houses you cannot write on is how people get discouraged.
