Homes for Sale in Placerville and Sawpit, CO

Homes and land down the San Miguel River from Telluride, and what to check before you buy.

Placerville and Sawpit at a glance

The down-valley crossroads, where the mesa roads begin

Placerville and Sawpit sit on CO-145 along the San Miguel River, about 16 and 12 road miles from downtown Telluride. Placerville is an unincorporated community of 362 people at the 2020 Census; Sawpit, about 3 miles upriver, is an incorporated town of 38.

This is where the roads to the mesas leave the highway. CO-62 starts at Placerville and runs about 23 miles to Ridgway by way of the Dallas Divide, with the county roads to the Iron Springs and Horsefly Mesa ranches off it. County Road 58P climbs from Sawpit across Hastings Mesa, Fall Creek Road leaves CO-145 just west of Sawpit for Wilson Mesa, and Beef Trail Road leaves it west of Placerville for Specie Mesa. San Miguel County's 2023 East End survey groups the whole stretch as Down Valley: Placerville, Old Placerville, Juniper Village, Fall Creek and Sawpit.

01

Placer gold and the Rio Grande Southern

Placerville takes its name from the placer gold workings on the San Miguel River and Leopard Creek, and it had a post office from 1878. When the Rio Grande Southern built a depot nearby, the first settlement became known as Old Placerville, and the county still maps an Old Placerville Estate subdivision northwest of today's town.

The same railroad's restored trestle still stands at Trout Lake, on the Galloping Goose Trail. In Placerville Park, the county plaque "Dedication to the Utes (Nuche)" is one of the interpretive sites San Miguel County lists.

02

Placerville, CO, at the highway junction

Placerville is unincorporated, so San Miguel County sets its zoning, and the county has drawn districts just for it: Placerville Residential and Placerville Commercial. The county maps the Placerville subdivision at about 60 acres in 78 parcel records, half of them about a third of an acre or less, bordering BLM land.

The Telluride Fire Protection District keeps Station 3 at 410 Front Street, and its project page covers a remodel of the station and a new training facility. On the east side of town, the county's Angell Lode Natural Area trail climbs about 0.42 mile from Surprise Avenue past an old mining cave toward the vanadium mines (county trails).

03

Sawpit, Colorado, and the river road

About 3 miles upriver, Sawpit is an incorporated town of 38 people at the 2020 Census, on about 19 acres inside its town boundary. Its parcels are under the Town's jurisdiction rather than the county's, and the Town runs its own community water system on groundwater, which the EPA lists as active. County records show 30 parcels under the Sawpit name, from 0.07 to 12.6 acres, half of them about a quarter acre or less. Fall Creek Road, just west of town, leads to Wilson Mesa and the Woods Lake trailhead into the Lizard Head Wilderness.

Between the two, CO-145 follows the San Miguel River, and both subdivisions border public land the Bureau of Land Management manages through its Uncompahgre Field Office. Just east of Sawpit, the county's M59 River Trail runs about 4.3 miles on a closed dirt road, open to hikers, bikers, Class 1 e-bikes, horses, anglers, boaters and cross-country skiers, and groomed for skiing when conditions allow. The county also has a feasibility study for a Down Valley Connector Trail.

What Garrett would check down valley

  • Town or countySawpit parcels fall under the Town of Sawpit. Placerville is county land with its own Placerville Residential and Placerville Commercial districts, though the county's sample point there reads Forestry/Agriculture, so confirm any parcel with the county's Zone District Finder.
  • WaterSawpit's Town system runs on groundwater. The EPA lists no community system for Placerville, which proves nothing either way, so ask where a house's water comes from. A new or replacement well needs a state permit.
  • SepticAsk whether the house is on a shared system or its own septic. Systems under 2,000 gallons a day are permitted by the county's Environmental Health program.
  • Public land next doorBoth subdivisions border BLM land. Find out where the line runs before you plan a fence, a driveway or an addition.
  • Fire and powerStation 3 is in Placerville, and San Miguel Power Association lists both Placerville and Sawpit among the communities it serves.

Questions

Placerville and Sawpit FAQ

Is Placerville a town?

No. Placerville is an unincorporated community, a Census Designated Place of 362 people at the 2020 Census, so San Miguel County handles its zoning. Sawpit, about 3 miles upriver, is an incorporated town with its own jurisdiction.

How far are Placerville and Sawpit from Telluride?

About 16 road miles from downtown Telluride to Placerville and about 12 to Sawpit, on CO-145. Telluride Regional Airport is about 15 and 11 miles away, Montrose Regional about 52 and 56.

Which school and fire districts serve Placerville and Sawpit?

Telluride School District R-1 and the Telluride Fire Protection District, whose Station 3 is in Placerville. Confirm a specific address with the district.

Where does the water come from?

In Sawpit, from the Town's community water system, which draws on groundwater. The EPA lists no community system for Placerville, so ask the seller about the source for any house there. A new well needs a permit from the Colorado Division of Water Resources.
