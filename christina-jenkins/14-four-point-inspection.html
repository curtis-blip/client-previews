Buyer's Guide

4 Point Inspection Florida: What Insurers Ask For

What the 4-point and wind mitigation inspections are, why the age of a roof matters, and what to ask before you buy an older home.

Book a Call

Why insurers ask for it

Citizens Asks for a 4-Point Inspection on Homes Over 20 Years Old

Citizens requires a 4-point inspection for all property owner, dwelling and mobile home applications for properties more than 20 years old. A Florida-licensed inspector checks the electrical system, plumbing, heating and air conditioning, and the roof. Other insurers set their own rules, so ask the insurer for its requirements before you write the offer.

A wind mitigation inspection is a different report. It documents the features that help a home stand up to wind, and Citizens says homes with approved wind features may qualify for premium discounts through it. Both are on Citizens' inspections page (read October 27, 2026).

Christina is a REALTOR®, not an inspector or an insurer. She can tell you which reports to ask for and when to call the professional who can answer.

Selling with an older roof

01

Step 1 of 4

What the 4-point looks at

The inspector reports on the electrical, plumbing, heating and air conditioning, and roof. Citizens updated its forms on March 20, 2025 (read October 27, 2026): the plumbing section now asks the age of the supply and drain piping and the water heater, and a photo of the water heater's pressure relief valve is required. Read the Citizens bulletin to see the current forms.

Ask the seller whether a recent 4-point exists before you pay for another one.

02

Step 2 of 4

The roof rules, and why age matters

Citizens asks for documentation of at least five years of remaining useful life on two kinds of roof. One is a roof older than 25 years with a soft covering such as shingle. The other is a roof older than 50 years with a hard covering such as tile or metal. If less than five years remain, it asks for proof of full roof replacement before a policy is written (read October 27, 2026).

Under s. 627.7011(5), Florida Statutes, an insurer may not refuse to issue or renew a homeowner's policy solely because of the age of a roof under 15 years old. A roof 15 years or older can be inspected by an authorized inspector, at the homeowner's expense. If the inspection shows 5 years or more of useful life remaining, the insurer may not refuse solely because of roof age. Roof age counts from the last date the whole roof surface was built or replaced to code.

03

Step 3 of 4

The wind mitigation form

Form OIR-B1-1802, the Uniform Mitigation Verification Inspection Form, is completed by an authorized inspector and is valid for up to five years if the structure has no material changes. A new version took effect April 1, 2026 (Florida Office of Insurance Regulation, read October 27, 2026).

The discounts are not printed on the form, so ask the insurer or agent. By law (s. 627.0629), rate filings must include actuarially reasonable discounts for fixtures and construction techniques that reduce windstorm loss.

04

Step 4 of 4

My Safe Florida Home

The state's My Safe Florida Home program offers free wind mitigation inspections and grant assistance for approved upgrades such as roof improvements, impact windows and doors, and garage door reinforcement. Its page lists eligibility for up to $10,000 in grant funding (read October 27, 2026).

Under s. 215.5586, Florida Statutes, it is subject to annual legislative appropriations and does not create an entitlement. Inspections are for owner-occupied homes with a homestead exemption, so read the program page before you count on it.

Homeowners insurance

What Florida homeowners insurance cost depends on

The price is set by the insurer for the specific home, so ask your insurance agent which inspection reports they require, and get quotes, before you bid.

The roof, the age of the plumbing and electrical, and the wind features the inspections document all feed the quote. Florida law requires insurers to describe their hurricane mitigation discounts on their websites (s. 627.0629), so you can see what a mitigation report could earn before you buy.

For a number on a specific home, call an insurance agent. Christina can tell you which questions to ask and which reports to bring.

Questions buyers ask

Questions Buyers Ask About 4-Point and Roof Reports

Do I need a 4-point inspection to buy an older home?

Citizens requires one for applications on properties more than 20 years old. Other insurers have their own rules, so ask for the insurer's requirements early. See Citizens' inspections page.

Can an insurer refuse a policy because the roof is old?

Not solely for age under 15 years, under s. 627.7011(5). For a roof 15 years or older, the homeowner may pay for an inspection by an authorized inspector, and the insurer may not refuse solely for age if 5 years or more of useful life remain.

How long is a wind mitigation inspection good for?

Form OIR-B1-1802 is valid for up to five years if the structure has no material changes, per the Office of Insurance Regulation.

Is the My Safe Florida Home program still open?

Its program page is the place to check. The statute says it is subject to annual legislative appropriations, and Christina cannot promise funds or timing (read October 27, 2026).

Is the 4-point the same as a home inspection?

No. A 4-point covers four systems for an insurer, and a general home inspection looks at the whole house for you. A general inspection is for you, and an insurer's 4-point is for the policy.
