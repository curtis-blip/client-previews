The buyer's guide

Buying a home in Virginia's Roanoke Valley

Eight steps from pre-approval to keys, with a buyer's agent who looks past the paint and the staging.

Search Homes Talk to Mitch

Before you start

What a buyer's agent does for you

The seller has an agent working for the seller. A buyer's agent works for you: finding the homes that fit, reading the contracts, and negotiating the price and the repairs.

Mitch looks at houses every week, and because he has renovated homes himself, he can tell you what a repair will cost and what a house will need in five years, before you are under contract.

Step by step

Eight steps to buying a home

01Talk with Mitch and sign a buyer agreement

Start with a conversation about where you want to live, what the house has to have and when you need to move. You sign a written buyer agreement before touring homes, and Mitch explains what it says and how he is paid before you sign. From then on he works for you, not the seller.

02Get your finances ready and get pre-approved

Look at savings, debts and the monthly payment you are comfortable with, not only the most a bank will lend you. A lender's pre-approval letter tells you what you can borrow and shows a seller your offer is real. Mitch works with lender partner Jonathan Sweat of IHMC Loans, who can also tell you whether a VA, USDA or Virginia Housing loan fits.

03Search and tour homes

Mitch sets up a saved search for the towns on your list, from Roanoke and Salem to Botetourt County and Smith Mountain Lake, so new listings reach you as soon as they hit the MLS. When one looks right, he gets you inside quickly. Because he has renovated homes himself, he points out what a house will need in the next few years before you fall for it.

04Write an offer that competes

Price is only one part of an offer. The earnest money deposit, the contingencies, the closing date and how flexible you can be on move-in all count with a seller. Mitch looks at what similar homes nearby actually sold for, then writes an offer that can win the house without giving up the protections you need.

05Inspect the house

Virginia's seller disclosure statement promises very little about a home's condition and points buyers to do their own checking, so the inspection is where you learn the house. Mitch reads the report with you and separates what matters from routine wear: a roof near the end of its life, an older electrical panel in a 1920s Grandin bungalow, or the well and septic on a property out in Botetourt County.

06Negotiate repairs and clear the appraisal

With the report in hand, Mitch asks for what the inspection supports: a repair, a credit at closing or a lower price. Your lender orders an appraisal to confirm the home's value against the loan. If it comes in low, Mitch goes back to the seller's agent with the recent sales.

07Plan the move and do the final walk-through

Book movers and set up the utilities, water and trash service for the city or county you are moving to. Shortly before closing you walk through the house one last time, to confirm the agreed repairs were made and nothing has changed since your offer.

08Close and get the keys

In Virginia, closing is run by a settlement agent (a title company or a real estate attorney), who handles the funds and records the deed. You sign, the money moves and the keys are yours. Mitch is still a call away after closing, for the contractor, the handyman or the question about the furnace.

First-time buyers

First-time home buyer programs in Virginia

  • Virginia Housing

    The state's housing finance agency offers first-time buyer loans and down payment help through approved lenders. Virginia Housing

  • VA home loans

    Veterans, service members and eligible spouses can buy with a VA-backed loan. VA.gov

  • USDA loans

    Some homes outside the cities may qualify for USDA's rural home loans. USDA Rural Development

  • Start with a lender

    Mitch works with lender partner Jonathan Sweat of IHMC Loans, who can tell you which programs you qualify for.

How buyer's agents are paid

Since 2024, buyers sign a written agreement with their agent before touring homes, and that agreement says how the agent is paid. The fee is negotiated between you and your agent.

In many sales the seller agrees to cover some or all of it, or it is asked for in the offer as a seller concession. Mitch explains the options in your first meeting, before you sign anything.

Questions

Questions buyers ask Mitch

How much do I need for a down payment?+

It depends on the loan. VA and USDA loans can mean no down payment for those who qualify, and Virginia Housing has programs that help with it. A lender can tell you your options.

Should I get pre-approved before I look at homes?+

Yes. It tells you what you can borrow, and sellers take an offer with a pre-approval letter more seriously.

What does a home inspection cover?+

The structure, the roof, the systems and the visible condition. Mitch reads the report with you and tells you which items matter and which are routine.

How long does it take to close?+

Often 30 to 45 days from an accepted offer, depending on the loan, the inspection and the appraisal.
