Oceanside at a glance

A railroad town with a harbor

Oceanside sits on the coast of northern San Diego County, with Camp Pendleton along its northern edge and Interstate 5 running about a mile inland from the ocean. It became a city on July 3, 1888, and covers about 42 square miles.

The pier, the harbor and 3.5 miles of public beach are on the west side, under the City's coastal zone rules. The city runs inland along State Routes 76 and 78, and a large portion of eastern Oceanside is in the Vista Unified School District.

01

A mission, then a railroad

Mission San Luis Rey de Francia was founded in 1798, the 18th of California's 21 missions, and came to be known as the King of the Missions. It is a National Historic Landmark with its own museum.

The town came with the railroad. The California Southern line was completed in 1883, a storekeeper named Andrew Jackson Meyers homesteaded the 160 acres that became the heart of town, and weekend trips to the "ocean side" gave the place its name. Oceanside incorporated in 1888 with about 1,000 people.

02

The pier and the harbor

Oceanside has had six piers since the first went up in 1888 at the end of what is now Wisconsin Avenue. The current one, at the foot of Pier View Way, opened in 1987, and at 1,954 feet it is one of the longest wooden piers on the West Coast.

The City built the small craft harbor at the north end of town in the 1960s. About two miles of sidewalk run around it, and Harbor Beach lies between the harbor and the Camp Pendleton boundary.

03

Camp Pendleton next door

The land north of the city was Rancho Santa Margarita y Las Flores, 133,441 acres granted to Pio Pico and his brother in 1841. The Navy bought it in 1942, the Marine Corps built Camp Pendleton there during World War II, and Oceanside grew from 4,652 people in 1940 to 12,888 in 1950.

The base covers more than 125,000 acres and, by its own account, has been the largest employer in North San Diego County for more than 60 years. It also holds the largest undeveloped stretch of coastline in Southern California.

04

Where the trains meet

The City built the downtown transit center in 1983. It is the one station where the COASTER, the SPRINTER, Amtrak's Pacific Surfliner and Metrolink all stop. The COASTER serves eight stations between Oceanside and downtown San Diego, and the SPRINTER runs 22 miles east to Escondido through Vista and San Marcos (NCTD's stations).

State Route 76 heads east to Interstate 15, and State Route 78 meets Interstate 15 in Escondido.

05

What Jim would check in Oceanside

  • Coastal zone Everything from the inland side of Coast Highway to the ocean is in the City's coastal zone, and so is land near parts of the San Luis Rey River, Loma Alta Creek and Buena Vista Lagoon. Many projects there need a coastal permit (the City's Local Coastal Program), so ask Jim before you plan a remodel.
  • Which school district Oceanside Unified or Vista Unified, which covers a large portion of eastern Oceanside. Check the address in the district's own locator.
  • Which water provider The City runs its own water utility, but Vista Irrigation District and Rainbow Municipal Water District each serve parts of Oceanside. It goes by address.
  • Flood and fire maps Look the address up on FEMA's flood maps, especially near the river, the creeks and the lagoon, and on CAL FIRE's fire hazard maps, which can affect insurance and building rules.

Questions

Oceanside FAQ

Is Camp Pendleton part of Oceanside?

No. Marine Corps Base Camp Pendleton borders the city to the north, on land the Navy bought in 1942, and Harbor Beach runs up to its boundary.

Which school district serves Oceanside?

Oceanside Unified School District, and Vista Unified for a large portion of eastern Oceanside. Each district's locator gives the schools for a street address.

What is the coastal zone in Oceanside?

Everything from the inland side of Coast Highway to the ocean, plus land near parts of the San Luis Rey River, Loma Alta Creek and Buena Vista Lagoon. Many projects there need a coastal permit.

How far is Oceanside from San Diego and Temecula?

By road from the civic center, downtown San Diego is about 38 miles away, Old Town Temecula about 30 miles and John Wayne Airport in Orange County about 49 miles.
