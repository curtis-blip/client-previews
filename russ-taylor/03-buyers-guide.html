The Buying Process

Buying a Home in Champaign County, Step by Step

Eight clear steps. No guesswork, no surprises at the table. Here is exactly how a purchase comes together when you buy with The Russ Taylor Team.

01

First Conversation

We sit down, in person or by phone, and talk through what you need, your timeline, and your budget. I want to understand the move before we ever look at a listing, so the search stays focused on homes that actually fit.

02

Get Pre-Approved

Before you tour, talk to a lender and get a real pre-approval, not a quick online estimate. It tells you your true price range and signals to sellers that your offer is solid. I can point you to local lenders I have closed with for years.

03

Define Your Search

We set the criteria that matter: location, school district, commute, must-haves, and deal-breakers. I set up a live MLS search so new Champaign County listings reach you the moment they hit the market, often before they are everywhere else.

04

Tour Homes

We walk through the homes that fit, and I tell you what I see: the bones, the roof, the resale outlook, the things photos hide. You will hear the honest read on each one, including the homes I think you should pass on.

05

Make a Strong Offer

When you find the one, we move. I read the comps, gauge how the seller is likely to respond, and structure an offer that competes without overpaying. Price is only part of it: terms, contingencies, and timing often decide who wins.

06

Inspection

Once your offer is accepted, a licensed inspector goes through the home top to bottom. We review the report together, separate the real concerns from the cosmetic ones, and negotiate repairs or credits where they are warranted.

07

Appraisal and Financing

Your lender orders an appraisal to confirm the home supports the loan, then works through underwriting. I stay in close contact with the lender and the title company so paperwork keeps moving and nothing stalls your closing date.

08

Closing Day

You review the figures, sign with the attorney and title company, and the keys are yours. I am there to make sure the final numbers match what we agreed to, and that you walk out a homeowner with no loose ends.

Earnest money is a good-faith deposit you submit with your offer to show the seller you are serious. In our market it commonly runs around one to two percent of the purchase price, though a stronger deposit can help in a competitive situation. It is not an extra cost: the funds are held by the title company or attorney and applied toward your down payment and closing costs at closing. As long as you stay within the contract's contingencies, that money stays protected.
