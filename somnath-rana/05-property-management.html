Property management

Property management in the Las Vegas Valley

Tenant placement, leasing, rent collection, maintenance and inspections across Las Vegas, Henderson and North Las Vegas. A good share of these owners live somewhere else.

Who this is for

Owners who would rather not run it themselves

Some of these owners bought through me and kept me on afterwards. Others arrived with a property they had been managing alone, or with a manager who stopped returning calls. The work is the same either way: keep it tenanted, keep it maintained, and keep the numbers honest.

What I handle

All of it, not the easy parts

Management is a full scope or it is not worth paying for. Half a service leaves the owner doing the half nobody wanted.

  • Tenant placement and screening
  • Leasing, renewals and rent reviews
  • Rent collection and owner disbursement
  • Maintenance coordination
  • Move-in, move-out and periodic inspections
  • Long-term portfolio review
Talk about your property

If you live somewhere else, someone still has to go and look.

A good share of the owners I manage for have never stood inside the property. Inspections, turnovers and anything that needs a set of eyes happen here in the valley, in person.

How it runs

Four things, every month

01

Onboarding and make-ready

I walk the property, list what has to happen before it can be marketed, and take over the file: keys, warranties, HOA contacts and any lease already in place.

02

Marketing and leasing

Photographs, listing, showings, applications. Every applicant goes through the same written criteria applied the same way, because consistency is what Fair Housing requires.

03

Maintenance

One channel for requests, then triage. Water, gas and cooling in a Las Vegas summer are urgent. Everything else is scheduled with a contractor I have used before, and reported to you.

04

Rent and reporting

Rent collected, owner funds disbursed on a set schedule, and a statement each month showing income, expenses and anything still outstanding.

Buying to rent

Or I find you the next one

The sales side of what I do buys rental property for people who already own some. The practical difference is that the rent assumptions get tested before you commit rather than after, and the property arrives already managed.

It also means one office knows both the purchase and the lease, which matters the day you decide to sell.

How buying works

Questions owners ask

Before you hand it over

What does management cost?

Fees are quoted per property, once I have seen it and know what it should rent for. Ask for the full schedule in writing before you sign with anyone, this office included. The management percentage is only part of it, and leasing fees, renewal fees and maintenance mark-ups are where the real differences between managers show up.

How do you screen tenants?

Income verification, rental history, credit and a background check, against written criteria that are applied identically to every applicant. That consistency is a Fair Housing requirement, not a preference, and it is also what keeps an owner out of trouble.

What happens when something breaks?

Requests come through one channel and get triaged rather than queued. Anything involving water, gas, or cooling during a Las Vegas summer is treated as urgent. The rest is scheduled with a contractor I have used before, and you see it on the monthly statement.

When do I get paid, and what do I see?

Owner funds are disbursed on a set schedule each month, with a statement showing rent collected, expenses paid and anything outstanding. If a repair is going to be large, you hear about it before it happens, not on the statement afterwards.

Do you manage properties you did not sell me?

Yes, and plenty of them. Some owners came to me with a property they had been running themselves, or with a manager who stopped returning calls. Taking over an existing lease mid-term is routine.

What if I want to sell it later?

Then it stays in the same office. I can tell you whether to sell with the tenant in place or wait for the lease to end, because I hold the lease and know what is in it. Nothing gets handed to a stranger at the point it matters most.
