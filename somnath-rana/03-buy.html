Buying

Buying in the Las Vegas Valley

Resale, new construction and investment purchases across Las Vegas, Henderson and North Las Vegas. In English, Nepali, Hindi and Urdu.

How this goes

Six steps, in the order they happen

Most people I work with are doing one of three things: buying a first or next home here, buying new construction, or buying something that has to produce a return. The sequence below is the same for all three. What changes is what gets checked before you sign.

01

A conversation before a search

I start with what you are buying and why. A home you plan to live in and a rental that has to carry itself point at different parts of the valley, and it is easier to say that out loud at the start than three months in.

02

Financing, before the looking

Get pre-approved before you tour anything. Sellers here rarely take an offer seriously without it, and knowing your real number saves you from walking through houses you would have to stretch for.

03

Resale and new build, side by side

I look at resale and at builder inventory in the same week. A lot of the new construction in the valley never reaches the MLS, and you cannot compare the two if you are only seeing one of them.

04

Offer, and the window after it

Offer, counter, then the contingency period: inspection, appraisal, HOA documents and title. This is where most of the real work sits, and where anything unpleasant tends to surface.

If it is an investment purchase, this is also where I check the rent assumptions against what is actually leasing nearby.

05

Closing

Final walkthrough, signing, funding, recording. In Nevada it is the recording that transfers the property, and it usually lands the same day.

06

After the keys

If the property is a rental, the management side picks it up from here: tenant placement, the lease, and the years after it. If it is your home, you have my number.

Questions buyers ask

Before you start

Do I need to be pre-approved before we start looking?

It is strongly advised. Most sellers in the valley will not consider an offer without a verified pre-approval attached, and touring without one tends to waste everybody's time. If you do not have a lender yet, I can point you at a few and you choose.

How much of the new construction is really off the MLS?

Enough that it changes what you should be looking at. Builders hold standing inventory and quick move-in homes that are never listed on the systems most buyers search from. I keep a live view of that inventory and check it alongside resale.

Can you help if I am buying from out of state?

Yes, and a good share of the people I work with are. I can tour on video, handle documents electronically, and if the purchase is an investment, the same office manages it afterwards, so you are not assembling a team of strangers from another time zone.

What does using a buyer's agent cost me?

Compensation is set out in a written buyer agreement before we tour anything, and who pays it is negotiable on each transaction. I will go through that agreement with you line by line before you sign it, and nothing is assumed.

Do you work with investors, or only people buying a home to live in?

Both, and the process above is the same either way. What changes is what gets checked before you sign: an investment purchase gets its rent assumptions tested against what is actually leasing nearby, and its ongoing costs looked at properly.

Which languages do you work in?

English, Nepali, Hindi and Urdu. If the biggest purchase of your life is easier to think through in the language you grew up with, it is available here.
