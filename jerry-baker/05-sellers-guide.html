Seller's guide

Selling a home in Gilpin County

Pricing land and cabins is not pulling three neighbours off a subdivision street. Here is how I do it, and what it costs.

What is it worth
Why me

Showing the home is the beginning. We sell lifestyles, school districts and neighbourhood amenities, and selling your property should not feel like a second job. Our service is hands on.

Twenty years of thin markets

Negotiation as a discipline

The process

Eight steps from first visit to closing

Photo: Jerry walking a property

Walk it and read it

I come out and look at what you actually have, including the things a listing sheet never captures: how the access reads in bad weather, what the well and septic are doing, whether the outbuildings help or hurt. That visit sets the price more than any spreadsheet.

Build the comparables by hand

Another firm told the Beavers there were no usable comparables in Gilpin County. I built a full analysis over that weekend. When the data is thin you widen carefully, adjust honestly, and show your work rather than pretending an algorithm did it.

Photo: pricing discussion, desk or documents

Set a strategy, not just a number

There is a price that gets showings, a price that gets offers, and a price that sits. On a market this thin, going out too high costs more than it does in the metro, because there is no volume of traffic to correct you quickly.

Photo: property prep, exterior or defensible space

Prepare what actually matters up here

Not staging in the metro sense. Clearing defensible space, making the access legible, getting the well and septic paperwork in order, fixing the things an inspector will find anyway. Buyers up here are practical and they look at different things.

Photo: camera or photographer on site

Photograph it properly

Mountain property photographs badly if you shoot it like a suburban house. It needs the setting, the approach and the light, plus honest interior work. This is where most listings in the corridor leave money behind.

Market to Denver, because that is who buys

Most of your buyers are forty five minutes east and looking for something specific. The listing has to answer their questions before they drive up, or they do not drive up. Syndication is the easy part, the copy is the part that works.

Photo: contract review

Negotiate the whole contract

Master Certified Negotiation Expert, designated by Oliver Frascona. Price is one term. Who carries a failed septic, how long the inspection window runs and what happens if the appraisal falls short are often worth more.

Photo: closing or handshake

Close, and stay reachable

Appraisal and insurance are where mountain closings slip. I stay on both. My reviews mention communication more than any other single thing, and that is mostly what they mean.

Marketing

How I market your property

01

Written for the buyer who is forty five minutes away

The listing answers access, water, waste and insurance before a Denver buyer decides whether the drive is worth it. Most listings in this corridor make them guess, and they do not guess, they scroll on.

02

Photography that shows the setting

The approach, the light and the outbuildings, not just four walls. Mountain property sells on place, and place has to be in the first three frames.

03

Full syndication, then actual follow up

The MLS feeds the portals automatically. What is not automatic is calling the agents whose buyers match, which is where showings on a thin market actually come from.

04

Reporting you do not have to ask for

What activity looks like, what feedback is saying, and whether the strategy needs to change. My reviews mention communication more than anything else.

The hard case

When comparables do not exist

The Beavers were told by another firm that there were no usable comparables in Gilpin County, after waiting a week for the answer. I built a full analysis that weekend, and the property went under contract on the third day it was live.

That is not a sales story, it is the actual job. Land, cabins and mining claims rarely have three clean neighbours that sold last quarter. You widen the search carefully, adjust for access and utilities honestly, and then defend the number to an appraiser who has the same problem you did.
