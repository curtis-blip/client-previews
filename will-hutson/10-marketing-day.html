Prep & Marketing Day

How to Prepare Your Home for Sale

Knowing how to prepare your home for sale is what separates a listing that sits from one that draws offers in the first weekend. Before our photographer ever arrives in Woodstock or Marietta, your home goes through a focused prep, and these home staging tips are the exact playbook we hand every seller.

Room-by-Room Checklist See the Seller's Guide
Start Here

Three goals behind every prep list

Preparing your home to sell comes down to three jobs. Once you understand what each one is really for, every task on the checklist makes sense.

Goal One

Emotionally Connect

A buyer decides in seconds whether a home feels like theirs. Warm light, fresh air and tidy spaces help a North Georgia family picture Sunday mornings here, not just square footage.

Goal Two

Declutter

Clear counters and half-empty closets make rooms read larger and let the architecture do the selling. Pack the extras early. You are moving anyway, so this is a head start, not busywork.

Goal Three

Depersonalize

Family photos, collections and bold personal touches pull a buyer's attention onto your life instead of their future. Neutralize the space so the next owner can imagine their own.

The Checklist

Room by room, before the camera comes

Work through one room at a time. Tap any room to open its list. None of it is expensive, and all of it shows up in the photos and on the first walkthrough.

  • Mow, edge and trim the lawn; pull weeds and clear leaves from beds and walkways.
  • Pressure-wash the driveway, walkway and siding where grime has built up.
  • Repair gutters and eaves, and touch up exterior and trim paint that has faded.
  • Add a clean doormat and a couple of potted plants by the entry for a warm welcome.
  • Wash the front door, polish the hardware, and replace a dim or mismatched porch bulb.
  • Clear coffee tables and shelves down to a few simple pieces, and pack personal collections.
  • Open every drape and blind to let in natural light, and turn on lamps before showings.
  • Touch up scuffed paint, fill nail holes, and repair any cracked plaster or trim.
  • Shampoo carpets, vacuum, and clean the windows inside and out for clear photos.
  • Remove an oversized or extra piece of furniture so the room feels open and easy to walk.
  • Clear the counters down to one or two items so the workspace looks generous.
  • Deep-clean the stove, oven and refrigerator, including the front and the handles.
  • Fix leaking taps, tighten loose cabinet knobs, and oil any squeaky doors or hinges.
  • Empty the trash, run the dishwasher, and keep sinks spotless before every showing.
  • Organize the pantry and a few cabinets; buyers do open them, and order signals care.
  • Make the beds with clean, neutral linens and remove anything stored underneath.
  • Thin out closets so they look roomy; pack off-season clothes you will not need before the move.
  • Take down family photos and personal art so buyers focus on the space, not your life.
  • Give a spare room one clear job, a guest room or an office, rather than a catch-all.
  • Replace burned-out bulbs, dust the blinds, and open shades to brighten each room.
  • Scrub tubs, showers, tile and grout until they look fresh and bright.
  • Fix leaking or running toilets and taps, and re-caulk where it has yellowed.
  • Clear the counters, hang fresh neutral towels, and stow personal toiletries out of sight.
  • Polish mirrors and fixtures, and make sure the room smells clean, never heavily perfumed.
  • Replace a worn shower curtain or bath mat; small swaps read as a well-kept home.
  • Cut the grass, weed the beds, and clear the patio or deck of clutter and stray toys.
  • Stage outdoor seating simply so buyers picture North Georgia evenings on the porch.
  • Sweep walkways, tidy the garden, and store hoses, tools and trash bins out of view.
  • Clean and arrange the grill and patio furniture, and add a few potted plants for color.
  • Repair loose deck boards or railings, and touch up exterior paint where it is peeling.

For more on what buyers respond to, the National Association of REALTORS publishes its annual Profile of Home Staging, an unbiased look at how prep affects sales.

Why Prep Pays

Staged homes sell for more

Prep and staging are not vanity. They move the number on the contract. Across the industry, staged homes sell for roughly 1 to 5 percent more than comparable homes left as-is.

Good to Know

Prep questions, answered

Most North Georgia sellers need one to two weeks for the prep on this checklist, depending on how much decluttering and small repair the home needs. We walk through with you early, prioritize what actually moves the needle, and schedule the photographer once the home is camera-ready so the listing launches strong.

Deep cleaning and decluttering are the non-negotiables and get you most of the way there. Staging then layers on the emotional pull that helps a home sell for 1 to 5 percent more. Sometimes that means rearranging what you already own; sometimes it means bringing pieces in. We tell you honestly which rooms are worth the effort.

Fix the small, visible things first: leaky taps, scuffed paint, loose hardware, burned-out bulbs. They cost little and signal a well-kept home. Big renovations rarely return their full cost, so check with us before spending on a remodel. Often a fresh, clean, decluttered home outperforms an expensive update a buyer may not even want.

Plenty of sellers do. The trick is keeping it showing-ready: surfaces clear, beds made, lights on, drapes open, pets out for showings, and a few bins for quick tidy-ups before a buyer arrives. We give you a short pre-showing routine so the home looks its best without turning daily life upside down.

Our photographer and in-house designer are part of how we market every home we list, so the listing photos, flyers, brochures and online ads are produced by our team. You focus on the prep and keeping the home showing-ready, and we handle the visuals and the campaign that get it in front of buyers.

Buyers weigh the kitchen, the primary bedroom and bathrooms, and curb appeal most heavily, so those earn your time first. We do a prep walkthrough with you, flag the highest-impact items for your specific home, and skip the busywork that will not change an offer. That keeps your effort focused where it pays.
