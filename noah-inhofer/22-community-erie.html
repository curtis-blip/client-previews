Get Your Bearings in Erie

Erie began as a coal town. Its plat was filed in 1871 after the Briggs Mine opened, the first commercial coal mine in Weld County, and the town was incorporated on November 16, 1874, named for Erie, Pennsylvania. Today it spans two counties, Weld and Boulder, and two school districts.

Stop 1

Old Town

Start where the town started. When Erie was laid out and incorporated in 1874, its streets were named for prominent residents, among them Kattell, Briggs, Holbrook, Pierce, Moffat and Cheesman. A few facts from the town’s own history page:

  • Plat filed in 1871 after the Briggs Mine opened
  • Briggs Mine was the first commercial coal mine in Weld County
  • Incorporated November 16, 1874
  • Old city hall building renovated in 2005

Stop 2

Coal Creek Park

Coal Creek Park is at 575 Kattell Street, a short walk from Old Town. The town lists these as part of the park:

  • Playground and seasonal splash pad
  • Seasonal ice rink
  • Memorial brick garden
  • A community room and a picnic shelter
  • Multi-purpose field and walking trails

Stop 3

Erie Community Park

Erie Community Park is at 450 Powers Street, next to the Erie Community Center, which opened on January 5, 2008. Between them, the town lists:

  • Ballfield complex, pump track and skate park
  • Sledding hill, three playgrounds and walking trails
  • Community center gym and racquetball courts
  • Indoor pool with a lazy river

Stop 4

Columbine Mine Park

Columbine Mine Park is at 1936 Skyline Drive. It is named for the Columbine Mine, which ran from 1920 to 1946 and employed about 350 men, so the coal story reaches this side of town too. The town lists what is in the park today:

  • Four outdoor pickleball courts
  • A ballfield, a shelter and a walking trail
  • No playground
  • The mine produced 7,316,275 tons of coal

Before you buy

What to Check on Any Erie Address

Four things that change from one side of town to the other. Each takes a few minutes to look up and is worth doing before you write an offer.

Five Hidden Costs Before Moving to Northern Colorado

12:37 · Costs

  1. Which county is it in?

    Erie sits in two counties, about 72 percent of its area in Weld and 28 percent in Boulder. The county of the address decides the assessor and the tax bill, so look the parcel up in that county’s own property search.

  2. Which schools go with the address?

    Two districts serve Erie: St. Vrain Valley Schools, about 84 percent of the town, and Boulder Valley School District, about 16 percent. Enter the street address in the St. Vrain Valley boundary lookup, and confirm with the district before you write an offer.

  3. Is the address in a floodplain?

    The town requires a floodplain development permit before building, fencing or home construction inside a floodplain. Its floodplain information page explains the permit and who to contact.

  4. Is the home in a metro district?

    A metro district is a separate government with taxing authority that pays for infrastructure, which makes it different from an HOA. The Town of Erie keeps a Metro Districts page and a policy for new ones. The town also runs its own water treatment and wastewater reclamation.

Quick answers

Erie Questions Buyers Ask

Which county is Erie in?

Both. Most of the town, about 72 percent by area, is in Weld County and about 28 percent is in Boulder County, so check the county for the address you are considering.

Which school district serves Erie?

Two do. St. Vrain Valley Schools covers about 84 percent of the town and Boulder Valley School District about 16 percent. Confirm the schools for a specific address with the district.

Where did Erie get its name?

The town’s history page says founder Richard Van Valkenburg named it for his birthplace, Erie, Pennsylvania. The town was incorporated on November 16, 1874.

What is a metro district and how is it different from an HOA?

The town describes a metro district as a separate special district with taxing authority that finances public infrastructure and services, while an HOA is usually funded by fees for shared spaces. Ask whether an address is inside one.
