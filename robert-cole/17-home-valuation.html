Know the Number That Matters

In Texas, nobody publishes what your neighbor sold for.

Texas is a non-disclosure state. Sale prices are not part of the public record, which means every online estimate you have seen was produced by a model working without the single most important input. The portals do not tell you that, and in smaller North Texas markets the gap between their number and reality can be wide enough to cost you real money.

Licensed agents can pull the actual closed figures out of the NTREIS MLS. That is what we price from, and we show you the comparable sales so you can check our work rather than trust a number on faith.

Get the price right and the home sells with momentum. Get it wrong and it sits, collects reductions, and teaches buyers to assume something is wrong with it. Starting at a defensible number is most of the job.

Our Method

How we arrive at your number

A defensible figure comes from real sales and real local knowledge, not an algorithm reading tax records off a server. Here is what goes into yours.

Real closed NTREIS sales

We price against homes that actually closed, pulled from the NTREIS MLS. Texas does not publish sale prices, so this data is not available to the portals estimating your value. Recent sales on your street and in your submarket set the baseline.

The things an algorithm cannot see

Condition and the age of the updates. Whether the lot backs onto a road or a greenbelt. Outbuildings, shop space, and on acreage the well, the septic system and the road frontage. On Cedar Creek Lake, the lakebed lease, the dock permit and the state of the bulkhead.

The local tax layer

A MUD or PID assessment changes what a buyer can afford to pay for your home, which means it changes what your home is worth. If your neighborhood carries one, it belongs in the pricing conversation from the start, not at the negotiating table.

Priced to sell, not to sit

The goal is a number that brings qualified buyers and protects your equity. A home that launches too high sits, collects price cuts and teaches buyers to wonder what is wrong with it. We would rather start at the right number.

Common Questions

Home value, answered

The questions that come up most before someone asks for a valuation.

Less accurate here than almost anywhere else, and the reason is structural. Texas is one of a handful of non-disclosure states, which means sale prices are not part of the public record. An automated valuation model works by comparing your home to what similar homes sold for. In Texas it cannot see those numbers.

So it estimates from what it can see: tax assessments, square footage, listing history. In a large uniform subdivision that gets close. In Terrell, Kaufman or on Cedar Creek Lake, where two homes on the same road can be genuinely different properties, it can be off by a margin large enough to change your decision in either direction.

Three numbers get used interchangeably and they rarely agree.

Assessed value is what the county appraisal district uses to calculate your property taxes. It often trails the market, and it is not what a buyer will pay.

An online estimate is an algorithm's guess from public data, working without sale prices in this state.

Market value is what a qualified buyer will actually pay in today's market. It is the only one of the three that matters when you sell, and it is the number we give you.

A real one. You get a written opinion of value with the comparable sales we used attached, so you can check the reasoning rather than take a number on faith. There is no obligation to list with us, and we will not pass your details to a third party.

The address, and anything the MLS will not know: updates and roughly when they were done, roof and HVAC age, any foundation work, and whether the lot has a feature that affects value. On acreage we will ask about the well and the septic system. On the lake we will ask about the dock and the bulkhead.
