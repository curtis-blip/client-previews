Frequently Asked

Buying and selling lake homes on Lanier

Practical questions, honest answers. If you have one that is not here, the contact page reaches Louis and Rhonda directly.

Lakefront homes sit directly on the water, often with a permitted dock or the right to apply for one. Lake-access homes are set back from the shoreline but include shared or deeded access, such as a community dock, ramp, or park. The price gap between the two can be significant, so it pays to know exactly what you are buying. Our buyer's guide walks through both, and you can browse current options on the search homes page.

Lake Lanier is a U.S. Army Corps of Engineers lake, so docks are permitted through the Corps, not simply built at will. Permits are tied to the property, have specific shoreline and size rules, and do not transfer automatically in every situation. Before you fall in love with a home, we confirm the dock status, the permit, and whether the slip size fits your boat. It is one of the first things we check on any waterfront listing.

Water depth at the dock determines whether you can keep a boat in the water year-round or end up sitting on mud when lake levels drop. Deep-water coves hold their value because they stay usable through seasonal fluctuations. Two homes that look identical from the street can be very different at the shoreline, so we evaluate depth, cove protection, and exposure as part of every lakefront showing.

Carefully, and with data. Lake and luxury homes do not have a tidy stack of identical comps the way a subdivision does, so pricing is part analysis and part judgment. Rhonda holds a Data Science in Real Estate certification from MIT, which means your price comes from real local sales, dock and water-depth adjustments, and current market context, not a guess.
