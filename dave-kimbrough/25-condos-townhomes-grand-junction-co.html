Condos, townhomes and patio homes

What you buy with a condo or a townhome

With a condo or a townhome you buy your home and join a homeowners association, and the association's documents decide what it maintains, what it insures and what it charges.

Colorado's Common Interest Ownership Act, CCIOA, governs how those associations are formed, managed and run. Some of its provisions apply only to communities created on or after July 1, 1992; older communities are still subject to the older law.

Once you are under contract, the Division of Real Estate says to ask your broker to get the association documents from the seller. Dave can request them as soon as you are under contract and go through them with you.

What the HOA covers

Insurance, upkeep, dues and management

Insurance

Under CCIOA section 38-33.3-313, an association must keep property insurance on the common elements against broad-form covered causes of loss, and commercial general liability insurance for them.

Ask what the master policy leaves to you: some associations require owners to carry more, such as flood insurance, and after a hail storm a condo or townhome association will almost certainly need to file a claim.

Maintenance and landscaping

Colorado law says little about when an association must do maintenance or landscaping; that is the executive board's call.

The documents define what counts as a common element, which tells you what the association looks after and what is yours. The Division of Real Estate notes maintenance questions come up most in attached homes such as condos and townhomes.

Search patio homes

Dues and special assessments

Most mandatory associations charge regular dues to run the community, and special assessments outside those dues are possible.

Read the last year of board meeting minutes for any special assessment under discussion before you close.

Search townhomes

Who runs it

Many associations hire a community association manager to run day-to-day operations for the board.

The seller has to give you enough to tell whether the association is professionally managed, so find out who the manager is, and read what other owners say about the company, before you close.

Before you sign

What to read in the HOA documents

The contract

  • Once you are under contract, section 7 of the Colorado Contract to Buy and Sell Real Estate (Residential) entitles you to the association's documents.
  • They should include both the governing documents and the financial ones.
  • Buying a home in a HOA, Colorado DRE

The finances

  • Colorado law sets some standards for HOA finances, but no agency oversees them, so the budget and the financial statements are yours to read.
  • Check the minutes for special assessments under discussion.

The insurance

  • Confirm the association insures the common elements, as CCIOA requires.
  • Find out what it expects owners to insure themselves.

The law

  • CCIOA governs how associations are formed, managed and run; communities created before July 1, 1992 are still subject to the older law.
  • The state's HOA Information and Resource Center registers associations and explains owners' rights; it does not give legal advice or settle disputes.
  • CCIOA and other laws, Colorado DRE
  • HOA Information and Resource Center

Dave can go through the association documents with you. The HOA Center does not give legal advice, so a legal question about them is one for an attorney.

Questions

Condo and townhome questions

What law covers condo and townhome associations in Colorado?

The Colorado Common Interest Ownership Act, CCIOA, which governs how associations are formed, managed and run. Communities created before July 1, 1992 are still subject to the older law for some provisions, so the answer can depend on when the community was created.

When do I get the HOA documents?

Once you are under contract, section 7 of the Colorado Contract to Buy and Sell Real Estate (Residential) entitles you to the association's governing and financial documents. Your broker gets them from the seller.

Does the HOA's insurance cover my unit?

CCIOA requires the association to insure the common elements and carry general liability coverage for them. What that leaves to you depends on the documents, and some associations require owners to carry their own policies, so read the insurance section before you close.

Can an HOA raise what I pay?

Yes. Besides regular dues, associations can levy special assessments, and the Division of Real Estate suggests reading the last year of meeting minutes for any under consideration.
