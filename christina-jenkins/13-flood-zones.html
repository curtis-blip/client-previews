Sarasota County Flood Zone Map: What to Check Before You Buy

Look up the home's zone on the county's own map, learn what the letters mean, and ask the right questions before you write an offer.

Book a Call

What a flood zone means for a purchase

A flood zone changes what a home costs to own

A flood zone is a label on a federal flood map, and it can decide whether your lender requires flood insurance and what an insurer asks of the home. The county explains that the special flood hazard area is land with a 1 percent chance of flooding in any year. Its zones start with A and V, and land outside it is zone X.

Sarasota County has new flood maps. FEMA issued them on March 27, 2024, and the county's interactive map shows whether a property was affected by the change (read October 27, 2026). The county's flood maps page is where it explains the maps and links that tool.

Look up the zone before you tour, so the cost of flood coverage is in the budget from the start.

Selling a home in Sarasota

01

Step 1 of 4

Look the address up on the county's map

Open the county's interactive flood map and search the address. Because the maps changed on March 27, 2024, check the current map rather than an older listing description or a past owner's memory.

The county page behind it, Sarasota County flood maps, explains the maps. Development in the special flood hazard area must comply with the Sarasota County Floodprone Areas Ordinance, so ask what that means for any work you plan.

02

Step 2 of 4

Read the letters in plain words

FloodSmart groups zones A, AE, AH, AO, AR and A99 as high-risk flood areas, and zones V and VE as high-risk coastal areas with an additional hazard from storm waves. Zone X shaded is moderate risk and zone X unshaded is lower risk, as FloodSmart's map and zone guide describes them.

Where a federally backed mortgage is involved, flood insurance is mandatory in the high-risk A and V zones, in communities that take part in the National Flood Insurance Program. In lower-risk zones it is not required, and FloodSmart still recommends it.

03

Step 3 of 4

Ask about the elevation certificate

When maps change, the county notes that an insurer may ask for a new FEMA Elevation Certificate. Ask the seller whether the home has one, and ask your insurance agent whether the insurer will want a new one, before the inspection period runs out.

How high the house sits matters as much as the zone letter. Your insurance agent and lender, not Christina, decide what coverage you need.

04

Step 4 of 4

Read the seller's flood disclosure, and get insurance quotes before the offer

Under s. 689.302, Florida Statutes, a seller must complete and provide a flood disclosure at or before the time the sales contract is executed. It asks whether the seller knows of flooding that damaged the property during their ownership, whether they filed a flood claim, and whether they received assistance for flood damage. The law was amended effective October 1, 2025 (chapter 2025-166, read October 27, 2026). The form also states that homeowners' policies do not include flood coverage.

Ask for homeowners and flood quotes before you write the offer, when the price can still change with what you learn.

If the home is insured by Citizens

Citizens can require flood coverage too

For personal lines residential property, s. 627.351, Florida Statutes says Citizens shall require flood insurance as a condition of coverage.

The statute phases it in by policy effective date and replacement cost: January 1, 2026 for a dwelling replacement cost of $400,000 or more, and January 1, 2027 for all other personal lines residential property insured by Citizens. For homes in FEMA's special flood hazard area, flood coverage is required at initial issuance for new policies on or after April 1, 2023 (statute read October 27, 2026).

Policies without wind coverage and condominium unit-owner forms are excepted. Whether this applies to a particular home is a question for an insurance agent, and Christina can tell you when it is time to ask.

Questions buyers ask

Questions Buyers Ask About Flood Zones

Which map should I use to find a flood zone in Sarasota County?

Start with the county's interactive map, linked from its flood maps page. It shows whether a property was affected by the new maps FEMA issued on March 27, 2024.

Do I have to buy flood insurance?

If the home is in the special flood hazard area and you use a federally backed mortgage, yes: the county's page calls it the Mandatory Purchase Requirement, and FloodSmart says the same for A and V zones. In lower-risk zones it is not required, though FloodSmart recommends it. Citizens has its own flood rule, described above.

Doesn't homeowners insurance cover flood?

No. The flood disclosure form under s. 689.302 states that homeowners' policies do not include flood coverage, which is why a separate quote matters.

Did the new maps change my hurricane evacuation level?

The county says the new map changes do not affect hurricane evacuation levels. Details are on the county's flood maps page.

Who decides what flood coverage I need?

Your lender and your insurance agent. Christina is a REALTOR®, not an insurer or an inspector. She can look up the zone with you and tell you when to bring them in, and they answer what coverage costs and what you must carry.
