Before you list

Selling Is One Option.
Make Sure It Is The Right One

For most families the house is the largest thing they own, and a sale is only one of the things it can do. Trading down and freeing up cash, trading up while you can, or holding it and letting a tenant cover the note are all real options, and which one wins depends on numbers specific to you.

I am unusual in Teller County in that I can price all of them properly, because American Eagle Property Management is the same office. If renting it out is the better answer, I will say so, and then I can run it for you.

Where you are selling changes the calculus too. A parcel in Florissant and a home in Briargate face different buyer pools and different timelines. The community pages cover all nine, and past sellers describe how it went.

Step 01

Establish the real number

The online estimate is a starting point. I walk the house, look at what has actually closed nearby, and account for the things an algorithm cannot see, which up here means the driveway, the well, the views and how much of the lot is usable.

Step 02

Decide whether selling is the right move

Sometimes it is not. If the numbers favour holding the property and renting it out, I will tell you, and I can price that scenario properly because I run the management side too. Getting an honest answer here is worth more than a fast listing.

Step 03

Prepare the house

Not a renovation. Decluttering, paint where it reads tired, and the small repairs that a buyer's inspector will find anyway. Up here I also want the defensible space cleared and the well and septic paperwork gathered before we list, not after.

Step 04

Photograph it properly

Professional photography, and shot at the right time of day for the aspect of your lot. If the view is the reason someone will pay a premium, the photographs have to carry it. Drone imagery earns its keep on acreage where the parcel shape is the story.

Step 05

Price it and go live

Priced correctly, the first two weeks do most of the work. Priced optimistically, you spend those two weeks teaching the market that your house is overpriced, and the price reduction that follows almost always nets less than the correct number would have.

Step 06

Showings and honest feedback

You get the feedback as it comes in, including the parts you will not enjoy. If the same objection appears three times in the first ten showings, that is data, and we adjust rather than waiting the market out.

Step 07

Offers and negotiation

We read the whole offer, not just the number at the top. Financing type, appraisal gap language, inspection objection deadlines and closing date all change what an offer is really worth to you. The highest number is not reliably the best one.

Step 08

Under contract to closing

Inspection objections, the appraisal, and the buyer's loan are the three places deals wobble. I stay on all three with the other agent and the title company so that problems surface early, while there is still time to solve them.

Net proceeds

The Number That
Actually Matters

Sale price is the headline. What lands in your account is the sale price minus the loan payoff, commission, title and closing costs, any concessions you agreed, and prorated property taxes. On a Teller County property there can also be a well or septic repair negotiated late in the deal.

You get all of that in writing before we list, and again with every offer, so you are never comparing two offers on price alone. If you want to run the numbers before we ever speak, ask me for a net sheet and I will put one together for your address.

Your current assessed value and tax history are public record, held by Teller County for Teller County properties and the El Paso County Assessor for anything on the north side of the Springs. Assessed value is not market value, but it is a useful sanity check.
