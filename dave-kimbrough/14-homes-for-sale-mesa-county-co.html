Living in Mesa County

A county of towns strung along I-70

Mesa County counted 155,703 people in the 2020 Census. Grand Junction holds 65,560 of them, and Clifton, Fruita, the Redlands, Orchard Mesa and Palisade hold most of the rest.

I-70 crosses the county from east to west, with Palisade at Exit 42, Clifton at Exit 37, Grand Junction at Exit 31, Fruita at Exit 19 and Loma at Exit 15. The county also takes in the towns of Collbran and De Beque and a handful of unincorporated communities, and four school districts serve it.

01

Where the county's towns sit

Grand Junction is the center, with Colorado Mesa University, the regional airport and Dave's office on Main Street. Clifton, home to 20,413 people, and Palisade lie east along I-70; Fruita and Loma lie west, out to Mack, where US 6 and 50 join the interstate.

South of the Colorado River are the Redlands, below the monument, and Orchard Mesa, between the Colorado and the Gunnison. Whitewater sits on US 50, about eight miles from Grand Junction.

02

Wine grapes and peaches

The Grand Valley American Viticultural Area, set by federal rule in 1991, lies entirely within Mesa County. Its boundary begins where I-70 crosses the Colorado River near Palisade and wraps west to Fruita.

Fruit came first. The Redlands had peach orchards by 1907, a few years after irrigation water arrived, and Palisade holds the Palisade Peach Festival at Riverbend Park.

03

Trails and public land around the valley

Colorado National Monument, set aside in 1911, has an east entrance on Monument Road in Grand Junction and a west entrance about three miles from Fruita. North of Fruita, the BLM's 18 Road trails run up toward the Book Cliffs, and McInnis Canyons National Conservation Area spreads west of town.

Kokopelli's Trail starts at the Loma boat launch and runs about 142 miles to Moab, Utah. Near Palisade, Mount Garfield's trails climb about 2,000 feet, and the Palisade Plunge comes down off Grand Mesa into town.

04

Mesa County land for sale, and what to check

Land in Mesa County runs from lots inside Grand Junction to rural parcels in the unincorporated county, where the county, not a city, sets the rules. Look a parcel up with the Mesa County Assessor before you make an offer, and ask whether irrigation water comes with it: in the Redlands, for one, that water comes from a company its shareholders own.

The current listings are on Dave's land and lots page.

05

Property taxes and county offices

The county's administration is at the Old Courthouse, 544 Rood Avenue in Grand Junction. The Assessor's office has a property value lookup and an exemptions page; the Treasurer sends the tax statements and collects the taxes, and the Assessor's office takes applications for the senior and disabled veteran exemption.

Mesa County Valley School District 51 covers most of the county. Plateau Valley School District 50, De Beque School District 49-JT and part of Delta County School District 50J cover the rest, so confirm the district for any address.

Questions

Mesa County FAQ

Which towns are in Mesa County?

The cities of Grand Junction and Fruita; the towns of Palisade, Collbran and De Beque; and unincorporated places including Clifton, the Redlands, Orchard Mesa, Loma, Mack, Whitewater, Gateway and Glade Park.

Which school district serves Mesa County?

Four do. Mesa County Valley School District 51 covers most of the county, with Plateau Valley School District 50, De Beque School District 49-JT and part of Delta County School District 50J. Confirm the district and school for a specific address.

Where do I look up a property's assessed value?

On the Mesa County Assessor's property lookup. The Treasurer's office handles tax payments; applications for the senior and disabled veteran exemption go to the Assessor's office.

Is Mesa County wine country?

The Grand Valley AVA, a federal wine-grape area set in 1991, lies entirely within the county, from where I-70 crosses the Colorado River near Palisade around to Fruita.
