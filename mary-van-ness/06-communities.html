The Map I Work From

Five counties, one realtor who actually knows them

Most agents pick a corner and stay there. Mary K. Van Ness works the whole map: Somerset, Middlesex, Mercer, Burlington, and Union.

That breadth matters whether you are buying your first home, moving up, downsizing, or sorting out an estate. You get one person who can compare a Hillsborough cul-de-sac to a Princeton condo to a Moorestown colonial and tell you, in plain English, where your money goes furthest and where your life fits best. Mary's Home Base

Somerset County

At a Glance

The suburban anchor of Central Jersey

Somerset is the county Mary lives in and works in the most. Strong school districts, leafy family neighborhoods, downtown Somerville for a night out, plus the open horse country up in the Somerset Hills. The market here rewards prepared buyers and well-priced sellers.

Towns Mary Covers

Across the whole county

BridgewaterHillsboroughSomervilleBasking RidgeBernardsvilleBedminsterBranchburgWatchungWarrenBound BrookRaritanPeapack-GladstoneFar HillsFranklin TwpMontgomeryNorth PlainfieldManville
Lifestyle & Character

Family suburbs in the south, horse country in the north

The southern half is built around family life: parks, neighborhood pools, weekend soccer, downtown Somerville for dinner. Move north and the county opens up. Duke Farms in Hillsborough, the Somerset Hills around Bernardsville and Far Hills, equestrian estates, hiking at Sourland Mountain and Schiff Nature Preserve.

Schools

Several of the strongest districts in Central Jersey

Bernards Township, Hillsborough, Watchung Hills Regional, and Montgomery anchor the county's most-requested districts. Bridgewater-Raritan, Franklin, and Branchburg are solid in their own right. Mary helps families weigh the trade-offs between district strength, commute, and house budget.

Commute & Connectivity

Highway spine plus two train lines

I-287, I-78, and Route 22 cover the county for drivers. NJ Transit Raritan Valley Line runs through Somerville, Bridgewater, and Bound Brook for the NYC commute. The Gladstone Branch handles the Somerset Hills. Newark Airport is about 35 minutes off-peak.

Who Lands Here

Who Somerset tends to fit

  • Families trading up who need more space, a yard, and a school district that holds its reputation.
  • NYC professionals who want suburban quiet but still need a workable train commute.
  • Empty nesters staying close to grown kids and grandkids without moving into a condo just yet.
  • Estate executors selling a family home that has been in the same hands for decades.

Looking in Somerset?

Mary lives here. Ask her anything: a specific town, a specific street, a specific school zone.

NYC Commuter Belt

Middlesex County

At a Glance

The county with the most range

Middlesex covers more ground than any of Mary's other counties, in every sense. NYC commuter towns on the Northeast Corridor in the north. Princeton-adjacent suburbs in the south. Denser, more urban pockets around New Brunswick. A long stretch of family neighborhoods in between.

Towns Mary Covers

Across the whole county

EdisonWoodbridgeNew BrunswickOld BridgePiscatawayEast BrunswickSouth BrunswickMonroePlainsboroNorth BrunswickSayrevilleMetuchenSouth PlainfieldHighland ParkCranburySpotswoodCarteretJamesburg
Lifestyle & Character

A community for every kind of buyer

Walkable downtown Metuchen and Highland Park sit a few miles from the cul-de-sacs of Monroe and Plainsboro. New Brunswick brings restaurants, theater, and Rutgers. Edison and Iselin anchor one of the strongest international food scenes in the state. Cranbury feels like a postcard.

Schools

Several strong districts, plus magnet options

South Brunswick, East Brunswick, Edison, Monroe, and West Windsor-Plainsboro (Plainsboro side) all carry strong district reputations. Cranbury is a quiet standout. Middlesex also has good magnet and county-vocational options for families who want a different kind of fit.

Commute & Connectivity

One of the best-connected counties in the state

NJ Transit Northeast Corridor through Edison, Metuchen, New Brunswick, Jersey Avenue, and Princeton Junction. NJ Turnpike, Garden State Parkway, Route 1, and Route 18 all run through. Newark Airport is roughly 25 minutes off-peak. Driving to Philadelphia is realistic from the southern half.

Who Lands Here

Who Middlesex tends to fit

  • First-time buyers stretching for the best school district they can afford.
  • Pharma, healthcare, and tech professionals working along the Route 1 corridor.
  • Multigenerational households looking for in-law suites and bigger floor plans.
  • Princeton-adjacent buyers who want the schools without the Princeton price tag.

Looking in Middlesex?

Tell Mary your commute and your budget. She can narrow 25 towns down to the three that actually fit.

Princeton Anchor

Mercer County

At a Glance

Princeton sets the tone, the rest of the county fills it in

Princeton anchors the top of the market and pulls the cultural energy with it. Hopewell, Pennington, and the surrounding villages bring quiet historic charm. Hamilton, Lawrence, and Ewing handle the workhorse market. Robbinsville and East Windsor draw families looking for newer builds.

Towns Mary Covers

Across the whole county

PrincetonWest WindsorEast WindsorHopewell BoroughHopewell TwpPenningtonLawrenceHamiltonEwingRobbinsvilleHightstownTrenton
Lifestyle & Character

University-town energy plus real countryside

Walkable Nassau Street, the Princeton Battlefield, the Princeton University campus, and the D&R Canal towpath all sit within a short drive. Sourland Mountain trails up in Hopewell. Farm stands and equestrian properties out toward Pennington. Then quieter family neighborhoods as you push east toward Robbinsville and East Windsor.

Schools

One of the deepest school benches in the state

West Windsor-Plainsboro Regional, Princeton Public Schools, Hopewell Valley Regional, and Robbinsville all hold strong reputations. Lawrence and Hamilton mix solid districts with respected magnet and charter options. Choices here are about fit, not whether there's a good fit.

Commute & Connectivity

Direct train to both cities

Princeton Junction is the Northeast Corridor stop, with direct service to New York Penn and Philadelphia 30th Street. Hamilton also has its own NEC station. Route 1 is the everyday spine. I-295 and I-95 are right there. Trenton-Mercer Airport handles short flights to Florida and the Carolinas.

Who Lands Here

Who Mercer tends to fit

  • Academics, hospital staff, pharma scientists, and the policy and government world.
  • Long-term holders who want a town they will not need to leave again.
  • Move-up families stepping into a school district they have been waiting for.
  • Downsizers selling a large Princeton home and moving into something easier.

Looking in Mercer?

Princeton, Hopewell, Hamilton, Robbinsville. They are very different markets. Mary can talk you through which one fits.

South Jersey Value

Burlington County

At a Glance

Where your dollar goes further

Burlington gives you South Jersey value with a Philadelphia commute. Generally lower property taxes than the northern counties, more land for the dollar, and a wide range of housing from historic colonials to newer suburban builds. The Pinelands stretch fills the eastern half.

Towns Mary Covers

Across the whole county

MoorestownMount LaurelMedfordMarltonMount HollyWillingboroBordentownBurlington CityBurlington TwpCinnaminsonDelranMaple ShadePembertonTabernacleRiversideWestamptonEastamptonLumberton
Lifestyle & Character

Historic main streets and real backyards

Walk historic main streets in Moorestown, Mount Holly, and Bordentown. Lakes and pine forests around Medford. Family-oriented swim clubs, soccer fields, and weekend farmers' markets across the western half. Quieter, more rural pockets the farther east you go toward the Pinelands. Plenty of room to actually own land.

Schools

Several strong districts across the western half

Moorestown and Lenape Regional (covering Medford, Mount Laurel, Marlton, and Shamong) carry the strongest reputations. Cinnaminson, Delran, Burlington Township, and Northern Burlington also reliable. Mary can match a target district to the kind of house and lot you actually want.

Commute & Connectivity

Philadelphia is the everyday city

NJ Transit River Line light rail runs Camden to Trenton along the river towns. PATCO via Camden gets you into Center City Philadelphia quickly. NJ Turnpike Exit 5, I-295, and Route 38 spine the county. Philadelphia International Airport is roughly 30 minutes off-peak.

Who Lands Here

Who Burlington tends to fit

  • Philadelphia commuters who want more house, more yard, and lower taxes than the city or close-in suburbs.
  • Families priced out of North Jersey who still need the school-district story.
  • Retirees and downsizers, including a strong active-adult community market.
  • Estate executors handling family homes settled by previous generations. Mary's probate background fits this part of the county especially well.

Looking in Burlington?

South Jersey gets less attention from North Jersey realtors. Mary works it like it deserves.

Walkable Downtowns

Union County

At a Glance

The NYC commuter sweet spot

Union is smaller than Mary's other counties in land area, but dense with the kind of towns buyers ask for by name. Direct-train commutes to Penn Station, real downtowns you can walk, top-ranked schools, and Saturday mornings spent on Main Street rather than in the car.

Towns Mary Covers

Across the whole county

WestfieldSummitCranfordBerkeley HeightsNew ProvidenceMountainsideScotch PlainsFanwoodGarwoodSpringfieldKenilworthRoselle ParkClarkLindenRahwayRoselleHillsideUnionElizabethPlainfield
Lifestyle & Character

Real downtowns and real community

Saturday morning is downtown Westfield, downtown Cranford, downtown Summit. Coffee shops, indie bookstores, parades, real Main Streets. Family-oriented but with grown-up restaurants and live music. Older housing stock with character. Rahway has the arts scene, Plainfield has the architecture.

Schools

Heavy concentration of strong districts

Westfield, Summit, New Providence, Berkeley Heights, Mountainside, and Scotch Plains-Fanwood all hold strong reputations. Cranford and Springfield round out the list. School lines drive a real share of the buyer decision in this county, sometimes more than the house itself.

Commute & Connectivity

Direct train to Penn Station from most towns

NJ Transit Raritan Valley Line runs through Westfield, Cranford, Garwood, Fanwood, and Plainfield. Morris & Essex Line covers Summit, New Providence, and Berkeley Heights. Northeast Corridor handles Rahway and Linden. Garden State Parkway, Route 22, and I-78 cover the rest. Newark Airport is right next door.

Who Lands Here

Who Union tends to fit

  • NYC professionals who want a walkable downtown and a real community, not just a commute pad.
  • Brooklyn, Hoboken, and Jersey City transplants trading apartment life for a house and a yard.
  • Move-up buyers chasing a top-ranked school district.
  • Sellers in the strongest direct-train towns who can run a competitive process when priced right.

Looking in Union?

These towns each have their own personality. Mary can match yours to one in 20 minutes on the phone.
