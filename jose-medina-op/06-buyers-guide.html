Buying A Home In Colorado Springs

Most first time buyers arrive with the same two worries: that they cannot afford it, and that someone is going to take advantage of them. Both are fixable, and both get fixed in the first conversation, before you tour a single house.

Buying a home in Colorado Springs is its own thing. This is nine distinct markets wearing one name, from the historic Broadmoor district to master-planned Briargate to acreage in Black Forest, and the right answer depends on your commute, your school district, and your number. Narrow the Colorado Springs communities first, then look at homes.

The Process

How Buying Works, Step By Step

Eight steps, in order, from the first phone call to the day you get keys. None of it should surprise you, because you will know what is coming before it happens.

photo Buyer reviewing a pre-approval letter with a lender, 4:5
01

Get Pre-Approved Before You Tour

Before we look at a single home, you talk to a lender and get a real pre-approval, not an online estimate. That letter tells you the actual number you can spend, tells me how to structure your offer, and tells a seller you are serious. Matt Hughes at Security National Mortgage, our outside mortgage partner, handles this for many of our buyers, and you are always free to use your own lender.

photo Map of Colorado Springs neighborhoods on a table, 4:5
02

Map Your Budget To The Right Areas

Colorado Springs is not one market. Briargate lives and breathes District 20 schools. The Broadmoor is a different world entirely. Fountain sits close to Fort Carson. Monument and Black Forest trade commute minutes for space and trees. We take your number, your drive, and your priorities and narrow the map before we start burning weekends on the wrong side of town.

03

Set Up A Live MLS Search

You get a saved search wired straight into the Pikes Peak REALTOR® Services Corp MLS, so new listings reach you the same day they go active instead of whenever a national portal gets around to syncing. When the right house can go under contract in a weekend, being early is most of the advantage.

Start Your Home Search
photo Agent walking buyers through a home showing, 4:5
04

Tour Homes, In Person Or On Video

We walk the homes that survived the filter. I point out what listing photos hide: drainage, roof age, how the house sits to the sun, road noise, the HOA reality, and the resale ceiling. If you are still out of state or deployed, we do the identical walkthrough on video and you get my honest read, not a highlight reel.

photo Purchase contract and pen on a desk, 4:5
05

Write An Offer That Holds Up

Price is one line of the contract. Financing type, inspection deadlines, appraisal terms, concessions, possession, and how the earnest money is structured all move the odds. I write offers built to win the home without stripping out the protections that keep you safe if something goes sideways after you sign.

photo Home inspector checking a furnace and roofline, 4:5
06

Inspection And Due Diligence

Once you are under contract we inspect. A general inspection, plus whatever the property calls for: radon, a sewer scope, a closer look at the roof. Roofs matter in this part of Colorado. Then we sit down with the report and decide together what is worth asking for, what is worth walking away over, and what is simply the age of the house.

photo Transaction paperwork and calendar deadlines, 4:5
07

Appraisal, Loan, And Title

Your lender orders the appraisal and pushes the file through underwriting while title work runs alongside it. This is the stretch where transactions quietly die from missing paperwork and blown deadlines. Tosh Sweat, our transaction coordinator, tracks every date, and Patti Madone at First American Title, our outside title partner, keeps the title side moving.

Meet The Team Behind Your File
photo Keys handed to new homeowners at closing, 4:5
08

Final Walkthrough And Closing

We walk the home one more time to confirm nothing changed and that agreed repairs actually got done. Then you sign, funding clears, and the house is yours. You keep my number afterward. The relationship does not end at the closing table, and neither does the advice.

Out Of State

Moving To Colorado Springs

If you are moving to Colorado Springs from another state, the first real question is never the house. It is where in the region you actually belong.

The Springs stretches from the historic Broadmoor district on the southwest side to master-planned Briargate on the north, out to acreage in Black Forest and Peyton, and south to Fountain near Fort Carson. Every one of them carries a different price point, a different school district, a different commute, and a different feel on a Tuesday morning. Pick the wrong one and you will feel it every single day.

So we do it in that order. Narrow the region, then run a live search filtered to the areas that survived, then tour. And if you are selling a home somewhere else to make this move, get a real number on that one first, because your equity there sets your options here. Start with the Colorado Springs home search, and if you have a home to sell, run a home valuation before you commit to anything.
