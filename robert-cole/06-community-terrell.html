Overview

Living in Terrell

Terrell is the market this team knows best, and it is where most of their inventory sits. The town grew up along the railroad and US-80, and that history is still legible in the housing stock: wide-porch homes on Griffith Avenue and the surrounding historic blocks, mid-century ranches closer to the schools, and new construction pushing north and west toward FM 148 and the I-20 corridor.

The other half of the market is land. Kaufman County acreage does not price like a subdivision, and buyers who assume it does tend to be surprised twice: once by what a well and septic system add to the number, and again by how much the road frontage and the shape of a tract move the value. Terrell also absorbed a large Amazon fulfillment facility, which changed the rental picture and the pace of the entry-level market.

The Basics

Kaufman County. 30 miles east of downtown dallas on us-80. Served by Terrell ISD.

Who Buys Here

Commuters priced out of Forney and Mesquite, buyers who want acreage inside a reasonable drive, and investors following the industrial growth.

Around Town

What is nearby

Historic downtown

The square and the surrounding blocks hold most of the local retail, the restaurants and the seasonal events that run through the year.

No. 1 British Flying Training School Museum

A working museum on the site where RAF cadets trained during the Second World War, and one of the more unusual pieces of local history.

Terrell State Park area and lakes

Fishing and day-use water within a short drive, with Cedar Creek Lake about forty minutes south.

US-80 and I-20 access

The reason the commuter market works. Downtown Dallas is a straight run west, and I-20 picks up traffic heading toward Tyler and Shreveport.
