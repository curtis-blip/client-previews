Sarasota at a glance

A Downtown on Sarasota Bay, With the Arts a Few Blocks Away

Downtown Sarasota sits right on the bay. The Bay park, Bayfront Park and Marina Jack line the water, and the Sarasota Opera House, Florida Studio Theatre and the Van Wezel are a short walk inland.

Scottish colonists arrived in 1885 after a British company platted the town, and John Hamilton Gillespie became the first mayor when it incorporated in 1902. From 1927 to 1959 the Ringling circus spent its winters here, and John Ringling left his museum and his bayfront home, Ca' d'Zan, to the state.

01

The Bay and the bayfront

The Bay is the city’s first signature waterfront park, where the Boulevard of the Arts meets Sarasota Bay, with free classes and events from kayaking and yoga to live music. Just south, Bayfront Park has paved paths among the palms, benches facing the boats, fountains and the Sailor and Nurse sculpture, and Marina Jack sits on the water beside it.

02

Theaters, the Opera House and the Van Wezel

The Sarasota Opera House opened in 1926 as the A.B. Edwards Theater, closed in 1973 and reopened in 1984 after the Asolo Opera Guild bought it. Florida Studio Theatre and Urbanite Theatre are within steps of it, and on the bay the Van Wezel calls itself the world’s only purple, seashell-shaped theater.

03

The Ringling and Selby Gardens

The Ringling is the State Art Museum of Florida and part of Florida State University: the Museum of Art, Ca’ d’Zan, the Circus Museum, the Historic Asolo Theater and bayfront gardens. John Ringling left it to the state, and it opened to the public in 1946. Downtown, Marie Selby Botanical Gardens grows orchids, bromeliads and other tropical plants on the bay at 1534 Mound Street.

04

Mid-century homes and older neighborhoods

In 1952 the architectural historian Henry-Russell Hitchcock wrote that a group of young architects in Sarasota was producing some of the most exciting new architecture anywhere, and their mid-century houses became known as the Sarasota School. Katie points buyers to neighborhoods like West of the Trail and Gulf Gate, and to the Sarasota Condos page for the high-rises downtown.

05

Saturdays downtown, and getting around

The Sarasota Farmers Market runs every Saturday morning around Main Street and Lemon Avenue, rain or shine. The free open-air Bay Runner trolley links downtown with St. Armands Circle and Lido Key seven days a week, and Sarasota Bradenton International Airport is about four miles north of downtown.

Questions

Sarasota FAQ

Which school district serves Sarasota?

Sarasota County Schools serves the city. Confirm the school for any address with the district, since attendance zones change.

Is a Sarasota address always inside the city?

No. Many homes with a Sarasota mailing address are in unincorporated Sarasota County, where county rules apply instead of the city's. Check the parcel with the Sarasota County Property Appraiser.

Is there a free trolley downtown?

Yes. The Bay Runner is a free, open-air trolley that runs seven days a week between downtown Sarasota, St. Armands Circle and Lido Key.

How far is the airport?

Sarasota Bradenton International Airport is on the Manatee and Sarasota county line, about four miles north of downtown.
