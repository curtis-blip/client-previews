Coming Out to Look at Property in Telluride: How to Plan the Trip

October 2026 · 4 min read

By Garrett Simon, broker associate, Telluride Properties

Most of the people I work with live somewhere else. They know Telluride from a ski trip or a festival, and now they want to look at property seriously. A well-planned trip makes that easy: the right flight, a place to stay in the area you are considering, and showings lined up so that two towns and the mesas fit into a few days. This is how I would plan it.

Getting here

Telluride Regional Airport is about six miles from town, with a runway at 9,070 feet. It has scheduled commuter flights and takes private aircraft. There is no Uber or Lyft at the airport, so book a shuttle or a rental car before you land.

Montrose Regional Airport is about 65 miles away and has more choice, including seasonal nonstops from major hubs. The drive takes about an hour and a quarter, through Ridgway and over the Dallas Divide. Authorized shuttles run from Montrose to Telluride; book one ahead.

Grand Junction and Durango are each about two and a half hours by car, and Denver is about six. Mountain weather can move a flight or close a pass, so leave a little room at each end of the trip.

Where to stay while you look

Stay in the area you are thinking about buying in. If it is Mountain Village, a few nights there will tell you more about ski-in/ski-out, the gondola and the village core than any listing can. If it is the town, stay in town and walk it after dark. If it is a mesa, stay somewhere you can drive out from in the morning and come back to for dinner.

I can help you find a rental that fits the plan. Luxury Vacation Rentals explains how that works.

Let me handle the logistics

While you look at property, the rest of the trip should take care of itself. Through my concierge help I can arrange the pieces in between: the airport shuttle, dinner reservations for the night you arrive, a caterer or private chef for a night in. Tell me your dates when we set up the showings and I will fold the rest into the same plan.

If you ski, bring your gear. A few runs between showings are part of seeing how you would use the place.

Seeing property in Telluride in a few days

Three days covers the ground if the showings are booked before you land.

Day one: the two towns

Walk the Town of Telluride first: the historic grid, the condos and the east end. Then ride the free gondola up to Mountain Village, about twelve minutes, and see the slope-side homes and the village core. The gondola closes for maintenance in the spring and fall shoulder seasons, and a free bus runs between the towns while it is closed.

Day two: the mesas

Spend day two on Deep Creek Mesa, with Aldasoro Ranch, Sunnyside and Gray Head, and on Turkey Creek Mesa, with the Telluride Ski Ranches, The Preserve and West Meadows. You will want a car from here on.

Day three: farther out

If you want land or more room, keep going: Wilson Mesa and Hastings Mesa, the ranches of Iron Springs and Horsefly Mesa, Placerville and Sawpit down the San Miguel River, Specie Mesa toward Norwood, or Ophir and Trout Lake south of town. Last Dollar Road runs from CO-62 near the Dallas Divide across Hastings Mesa and over Last Dollar Pass to Telluride. It is unpaved beyond the Telluride end, and the pass is closed to vehicles from December 1 to May 15, so ask me about conditions before you drive it.

When to come

Every season shows you something different about a property.

  • Winter, from late November into early April, shows you what an owner lives with: snow on the driveway, the plowing, which rooms get the sun. You can ski between showings.
  • Summer and early fall are the easiest time to walk land and see the mesas, and they bring the festivals: Telluride Bluegrass in June and the film festival over Labor Day weekend. If your dates land on a festival weekend, book early.
  • The shoulder seasons, in spring after the lifts close and in late fall before they open, are quiet. The gondola closes, along with many restaurants, shops and hotels, so plan meals and transport ahead. A quiet week can still be a good week to look.

For what is on in each season, see Things to Do.

What to ask me before you fly in

A few answers before the trip make the showings far more useful:

  • What is for sale in your range right now. I can set up a saved search so new listings reach you before you arrive.
  • For a condo: the HOA documents, the rental rules and the parking.
  • For a house or land on a mesa: water, septic, road maintenance and winter access, and what the county will let you build.
  • If you plan to rent the place out, how the town or the county licenses short-term rentals.
  • How your financing or proof of funds will be ready, so an offer can go in while you are here.
