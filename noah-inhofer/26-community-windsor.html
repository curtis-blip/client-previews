Get Your Bearings in Windsor

Windsor grew up halfway between Greeley and Fort Collins, around a roadside stop on the Overland Trail and then the 1882 railroad. A sugar factory that ran from 1905 until 1966 shaped the town and drew many Germans from Russia. Today the town spans two counties, Weld and Larimer, with Weld holding about 78 percent of it.

Stop 1

Boardwalk Park and the History Museum

Boardwalk Community Park, at 100 N. 5th Street, is a large park around Windsor Lake, and the Windsor History Museum sits on its grounds. What the town lists:

  • A playground, a stage and beach access
  • Three shelters, including a Market Pavilion, plus fishing, boating and a dog park
  • History Museum: five historic buildings moved onto the grounds
  • The Train Depot was built in 1882 and moved to Boardwalk Park in 1975; museum admission is free

Stop 2

Art and Heritage Center

The Art and Heritage Center is on the same street as the museum, at 116 5th Street. The town lists:

  • At 116 5th Street
  • Admission is free

Stop 3

Brunner Farm

Brunner Farm is a town park at 305 Prospector Street. The town lists:

  • A playground
  • A shelter and tables
  • Hard and soft surface trails

Before you buy

What to Check on Any Windsor Address

Four things that change from one side of town to the other. Each takes a few minutes to look up and is worth doing before you write an offer.

Five Hidden Costs Before Moving to Northern Colorado

12:37 · Costs

  1. Which county is it in?

    Windsor straddles two counties: about 78 percent of the town by area is in Weld County and about 22 percent is in Larimer County. The county decides the assessor and the tax bill, so look the parcel up in the one that holds it, in the Weld County property portal or the Larimer County assessor’s search.

  2. Which schools go with the address?

    The town says three school districts lie within its limits: Weld RE-4, Poudre School District and Thompson School District. Weld RE-4 has a Boundary Locator. Confirm the campus with the district before you write an offer.

  3. Is it in the floodplain?

    The Town of Windsor requires a Floodplain Development Permit for any work in a floodplain, but its floodplain page shows no map. FEMA’s Flood Map Service Center is the map to search by address, and the town engineering office handles questions.

  4. Who provides the water, and is there a metro district?

    The town says three providers serve Windsor: North Weld County Water District, the City of Greeley and Fort Collins-Loveland Water District. Metro districts are Title 32 special districts, usually set up by developers to finance subdivision infrastructure, with the power to tax. The town links the state’s district listing.

Quick answers

Windsor Questions Buyers Ask

What county is Windsor in?

Both. About 78 percent of the town by area is in Weld County and about 22 percent is in Larimer County, so the parcel decides.

Which school district serves Windsor?

Three districts lie within town limits: Weld RE-4, Poudre School District and Thompson School District. Weld RE-4 covers about 78 percent of the town by area.

Who supplies water in Windsor?

Three separate providers serve the town: North Weld County Water District, the City of Greeley and Fort Collins-Loveland Water District, so it depends on the address.

What is a metro district?

The town describes a metro district as a Title 32 special district, usually organized by a developer to finance infrastructure for a new subdivision, with the power to tax or charge fees.
