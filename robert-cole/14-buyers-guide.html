Buyer’s Guide

Buying a Home in North Texas

How buying actually works in North Texas, including the two or three things that catch people out here and nowhere else.

Home/Buy/Buyer's Guide
Start Here

Buying in this market is not the national playbook.

Most buyer guides are written to be reusable in any state. This one is not. Texas is a non-disclosure state, a large share of new construction around Forney and Terrell sits inside a MUD or PID, much of the Cedar Creek Lake shoreline is leased rather than owned, and county acreage runs on wells and septic systems. Each of those changes the number you actually pay.

We closed 88 homes in the last twelve months across this exact geography. What follows is the process we run, in order.

The Process

Eight steps, in order

Skip step four and you can lose more money than every other step saves you.

Get pre-approved before you look

Not pre-qualified, pre-approved. A pre-qualification is a conversation. A pre-approval means a lender has actually pulled your credit and verified income. In these markets a listing agent reads the two very differently, and it decides whether your offer gets taken seriously.

Set the search up properly

We build a saved search on the NTREIS MLS against your real criteria and price band. You see matches when they hit the MLS, which is ahead of the national portals. That head start is often the whole game on the homes worth having.

Tour with someone who knows the street

Photographs hide grade, drainage, road noise and what is planned on the empty lot behind the fence. We walk it with you and tell you what we would want to know if we were writing the check.

Check the tax layer before you write

This is the North Texas step most buyers skip. A MUD or PID assessment can add hundreds of dollars a month to an otherwise identical payment. We confirm the district, the rate and how long it runs before you commit to a number.

Write an offer that fits the situation

Price is one term of several. Option period, financing and appraisal terms, closing date, what conveys, who pays what at closing. On a competitive listing the structure of the offer often matters more than the top line figure.

Use the option period

Texas gives you a negotiated option period, an unrestricted right to terminate. That is when the general inspection happens, plus anything the general inspection flags: foundation, septic, well, roof, HVAC. On acreage and lake property this is where the real risk sits.

Handle the appraisal and the loan

The lender orders the appraisal and underwriting runs in parallel. If the appraisal comes in under contract price we renegotiate, cover the gap or walk, and the right answer depends on terms agreed weeks earlier.

Close

Final walkthrough, funding, keys. In Texas the title company runs closing rather than an attorney. We are there for it, and we stay reachable afterwards.

Questions

What buyers ask us

A Municipal Utility District or Public Improvement District is a taxing entity created to fund the infrastructure for a new neighborhood: water, sewer, roads, drainage. The cost is repaid through an assessment on top of your ordinary property taxes, and it can run for decades.

Two houses with the same list price, one inside a MUD and one outside it, can differ by several hundred dollars a month. It is disclosed, but it is easy to miss. We confirm the district and the current rate before you write an offer, not during the option period.

Sale prices are not part of the public record in Texas, so the estimates you see on national portals are modeled, not observed, and in smaller markets like Terrell and Gun Barrel City they are frequently well off.

Licensed agents can pull actual closed prices from the NTREIS MLS. That is the data we use to tell you whether a list price is defensible, and it is the main practical reason to have an agent in this state at all.

Verify what you actually own. Much of the Cedar Creek shoreline is Tarrant Regional Water District property, which means the lakebed and often the strip in front of the house are leased rather than deeded. Docks, bulkheads and retaining walls carry permit obligations that transfer to you at closing.

We confirm the lease status, the permit and the condition of the bulkhead before closing. A failing retaining wall is a five-figure problem that does not show up in listing photos.

Water and waste are yours. A well and a septic system need to be inspected on their own, separately from the general inspection, and replacement is expensive. Road frontage, access easements and the shape of a tract move value more than buyers expect, and lenders treat land differently from a standard mortgage.

Compensation is negotiable and is agreed in writing before we start showing you homes. We will walk you through the buyer representation agreement, what it commits you to, and what the seller side may or may not be offering on any given listing, before you sign anything.

From accepted contract to closing, a financed purchase typically runs about 30 to 45 days, driven mostly by the lender. Cash closes faster. The search itself varies enormously by price band and market, which is why getting the financing sorted first matters so much.
