The seller's guide

Selling your Roanoke Valley home

Pricing, preparation and marketing, with professional photography included and a plan for every showing.

Get My Home's Value Talk to Mitch

What sells a house

Three things that sell a home

  • Strategic pricing

    A price set from recent sales and the home's real condition draws buyers in the first weeks, when interest is highest.

  • Presentation

    Repairs that pay for themselves, a clean, bright house, and photographs that show it at its best.

  • Targeted marketing

    The MLS and the sites buyers use, agents across the valley, signage, email and social, and open houses when they help.

Step by step

Eight steps to selling your home

01Meet with Mitch at the house

Mitch walks through the house with you, hears your timing and what you need from the sale, and notes what has been updated and what has not. He then prices it against recent sales of similar homes nearby and explains the number before you decide anything.

02Prepare the home

Mitch gives you a short list: the repairs a buyer's inspector is sure to flag, and the touch-ups that will show in the photographs. He has renovated enough houses to know which fixes pay for themselves, and he can point you to vendors around the valley to do the work.

03Photography and media

Professional photography is included with every listing, with video, aerials and a virtual tour when the house and the lot call for them. The shoot waits until the house is ready and is timed for the light, and a home at Smith Mountain Lake gets its dock and its water view as well as its rooms.

04Launch the listing

The listing goes live on the MLS and the sites buyers search, with a sign in the yard and a note to the agents across the valley most likely to have a buyer for it. The first weeks bring the most attention, which is why the price and the photographs are ready before the house goes live.

05Showings and feedback

Showings are booked through a lockbox and scheduling system, so you know who is coming and when. Mitch passes on every agent's feedback, and if the same comment keeps coming back, he talks it through with you, whether the answer is a price change, a repair or more time.

06Review offers and go under contract

Mitch lays out each offer side by side: the price, the financing, the contingencies, the closing date and what you would take home from each. Once you accept one, he tracks every deadline in the contract from signing to closing, so nothing slips.

07Inspection, appraisal and repairs

The buyer's inspector goes through the house and the buyer's lender orders an appraisal. Mitch answers the repair requests and negotiates what is fair, then makes sure the agreed repairs are done and documented before closing. If the appraisal comes in under the price, he works through the options with the buyer's agent.

08Close and hand over the keys

In Virginia, closing is run by a settlement agent (a title company or a real estate attorney), who pays off your mortgage and sends you the proceeds. You sign, hand over the keys and move on to what is next, whether that is across town or out to the lake.

Mitch's take

Before you list, fix what a buyer's inspector will find and skip what a buyer will repaint anyway. Mitch has renovated enough houses to know which is which.

Questions

Questions sellers ask Mitch

How is my asking price set?+

From recent sales of similar homes nearby, adjusted for your home's condition, updates and lot. Mitch walks you through the numbers before you decide.

Should I make repairs before I list?+

Some, not all. Mitch will tell you which repairs a buyer's inspector will flag and which updates are worth the money.

What if I am buying and selling at the same time?+

Mitch plans the timing of both sales with you, so you are not carrying two homes longer than you need to.

What does it cost to sell?+

Mitch explains every cost in your first meeting, from commission to closing costs, so the number you take home is clear.
