Iron Springs and Horsefly at a glance

Ranches north and west of CO-62

The ranch subdivisions on this page sit north and west of CO-62, between Placerville and Dallas Divide, about 24 to 33 road miles from downtown Telluride. County records list every parcel in them at 35 acres or more, and a few at several hundred.

The names need care. Federal maps have no Horsefly Mesa; their Horsefly Peak, Ridge and Creek lie to the north in Ouray and Montrose counties, so here it is a local name. The federally named Iron Springs Mesa lies farther west, in the Norwood School District R-2J and the Norwood Fire Protection District. Every subdivision on this page that the county maps falls in the Telluride R-1 school district and the Telluride Fire Protection District.

01

Where Iron Springs Mesa is on the map

On federal maps, Iron Springs Mesa is the ground west of these subdivisions, north of the San Miguel River, in the Norwood fire and school districts. The Telluride Fire Protection District is pursuing a boundary inclusion of about 130 square miles that the county maps over it; check its status with the district.

Horsefly Mesa is a local name that brokerages use for the ranch country west of Dallas Divide. Confirm the school and fire district for any parcel here by its address.

02

Ranches north of CO-62

North of the highway, toward the Ouray and Montrose county lines. County Road 60X, which the county maintains but leaves to private plowing, reaches San Juan Ranch and Panorama Acres.

  • San Juan RanchA common-interest community with filings recorded in 1995: eight parcels of about 41 to 165 acres plus open space, on about 728 acres at the Ouray and Montrose county lines. Not the unplatted San Juan Ranch tracts on Hastings Mesa.
  • Dancing Bear RanchNot found under that name in county records. A listing routes it through the San Juan Ranch gates; ask Garrett for the recorded plat name.
  • Panorama AcresTen tracts of about 40 acres near the county's northern edge, on County Roads Z60 and 60X.
  • Frontier RanchFive parcels of about 55 to 722 acres on about 1,372 acres, recorded by the county as Frontier.
  • Brown Dog RanchFifteen parcels of about 35 acres on about 531 acres, beside a 574-acre Nature Conservancy easement.

03

Ranches west of CO-62

West of the highway, The Bluffs sits across CO-62 from Finnegan Ranch on Hastings Mesa. Brown Ranch Road (County Road X48) runs west toward the federally named Iron Springs Mesa: county-plowed for 1.8 miles, privately plowed for about 5.2, then not plowed for the last 4.8.

  • Brown RanchFive county filings along Brown Ranch Road: Brown Ranch itself, 40 to 170 acres, and the East, Estates, North and West filings, mostly 74 acres and up.
  • McKenzie Spring RanchTwenty-six parcels of 35 to 232 acres on National Forest edge, with a Black Canyon Regional Land Trust easement recorded as Red Dog Ranch. Garrett's essay spells it McKenzie Springs.
  • The BluffsSeventeen parcels of about 35 to 49 acres beside BLM land, where Colorado Parks and Wildlife maps elk winter range and a migration corridor.
  • The Lakes at Carstens RanchJust west of The Bluffs: eleven parcels of about 35 to 86 acres beside a 486-acre Trust for Land Restoration easement.
  • Big Valley RanchFive lots of about 35 to 38 acres, at the lowest sample point in the area, about 8,420 feet.

What Garrett would check off CO-62

  • School and fire districtConfirm both by address. These subdivisions map to Telluride R-1 and the Telluride Fire Protection District; the mesa to the west maps to Norwood's school and fire districts.
  • Who plows the roadPast the first 1.8 miles of Brown Ranch Road, plowing is private or none, and the county does not plow County Roads 60X, Z60 or 59Z. Ask who plows the road to a lot.
  • Water and septicEPA lists no community water system here, which proves nothing about a small shared one. Ask how a parcel gets water; a new well needs a state permit, and septic a county permit.
  • CovenantsRead each subdivision's recorded covenants, which can set minimum parcel sizes and building rules, before you plan to divide a parcel or build.

Questions

Iron Springs and Horsefly Mesa FAQ

Is Horsefly Mesa a real place name?

Not on federal maps. The USGS place-name database has no Horsefly Mesa in Colorado; its Horsefly Peak, Ridge and Creek lie to the north in Ouray and Montrose counties. Brokerages use the name for the ranch country west of Dallas Divide.

Which school district serves Iron Springs Mesa?

It depends on the parcel. The subdivisions on this page that the county maps are in Telluride School District R-1; the federally named Iron Springs Mesa, farther west, is in Norwood School District R-2J. Confirm any address with the district.

How far are these ranches from Telluride?

About 24 to 33 miles by road to downtown Telluride, depending on the subdivision, and about 46 to 56 to Montrose Regional Airport.

Is Dancing Bear Ranch a recorded subdivision?

Not under that name in the county's subdivision or parcel records. A listing routes it through the San Juan Ranch gates off County Road 60X; ask Garrett for the recorded plat name.
