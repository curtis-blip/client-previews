Foreign Buyers and Investors

I Work With Many Anna Maria Island Foreign Buyers and Investors That Need Advice and Ask My Advice on How to Purchase Real Estate Here in the United States

Ways to hold title in the U.S.

Foreign nationals may take title to U.S. real estate through several structures: personal name, domestic corporation, foreign corporation, limited liability company (LLC), joint venture, REIT, or foreign pension plan. The right one depends on your tax residency, investment size, liability preferences, and estate planning goals.

Most of my international clients form an LLC. It combines the liability protection of a corporation with the tax flexibility of a partnership, plus privacy: your personal name does not appear on the deed, only the LLC's.

The LLC advantage, in short:

  • Lawsuit protection and asset shielding
  • Credibility with U.S. banks and title companies
  • Pass-through taxation, no double tax
  • Deductible business expenses and depreciation
  • Anonymity on the public deed
  • Easy to bring in additional members as partners
  • Perpetual duration, continues regardless of member changes

There Are 3 Issues Foreign Investors Must Comply With When Owning Property in the United States.

Visas

Owning U.S. real estate does not grant residency. If you plan to spend significant time here, a visa is required. Common types for international investors:

  • B-1 Business Visitor
  • L-1 Intra-Company Transferee
  • E-1 / E-2 Treaty Trader & Investor
  • EB-5 Immigrant Investor
  • O / P Extraordinary Ability

Federal Taxation

Non-residents must pay U.S. federal tax on income generated from their investments, including rental income and capital gains on sale.

FIRPTA requires 15% of the gross sales price be withheld at closing when a foreign seller sells U.S. real estate, unless an exemption applies. More at the IRS FIRPTA page.

Reporting & Compliance

If you have income from any U.S. source, including real estate, you must file a U.S. federal income tax return, typically Form 1040-NR for non-residents. Foreign individuals need an ITIN to file.

Real estate professionals are also required to report cash transactions exceeding $10,000 to the federal government.
