Seller's Guide

How to Sell Your House in Sarasota, FL

Pricing, photos, Florida's disclosures and the closing, with Ryan on the market and the negotiating and Katie on the details.

How it works

What Selling Looks Like in Sarasota and Manatee Counties

Selling here means a written listing agreement, the disclosures Florida's laws and courts expect, a contract with its own deadlines and a deed the county clerk records. Each step below takes one of them in turn.

Your buyer may live down the street or several states away. Many of Ryan and Katie's own buyers have shopped from out of state, so they know how a home reads on a screen before anyone walks through it.

Step 1 of 8

Price It From an Estimate and Real Sales

Start with the home value estimator: enter your address and it returns a value, a likely range and a confidence score, all from public records. Nobody has seen inside your house yet, so treat it as a first look.

For a price you can list at, Ryan and Katie walk the house and compare it with recent sales of similar homes. The list price is yours to set.

02

Step 2 of 8

Get the House Ready for Buyers and Their Inspectors

Fix what an inspector would flag, clear the counters and closets, and trim the landscaping back from the windows and the roofline. Gather your paperwork too: the roof's age and permits, any wind mitigation report and your insurance history.

Your buyer's insurer will ask about the roof and the windows. A wind mitigation report documents qualifying features, and Florida requires insurers to offer discounts for them, so a current report can matter to your buyer's quote.

03

Step 3 of 8

Photos and Video for Buyers Who See It First on a Screen

Most buyers meet your home in the listing photos, and a buyer from out of state may tour it only on video before writing an offer. Shoot in the brightest part of the day, open the shutters, and show the lanai, the pool and the water if you have them.

Ryan and Katie walk homes with buyers who can't be here on video calls, so expect buyers to ask about the roof and the flood zone on yours.

04

Step 4 of 8

Sign the Listing Agreement and Go Live in the MLS

The listing agreement authorizes Ryan and Katie's brokerage to market and sell your home, and sets the price, the commission and how long the listing runs. Commission is negotiable and set in the listing agreement. Florida requires a definite expiration date and a signed copy in your hands within 24 hours.

Then the listing goes into the MLS, where agents can show it to their buyers. Through IDX Broker it also appears on this site's Home Search and on Ryan and Katie's listings.

Sources for Step 4

In Florida, a seller has to disclose the defects they know about that a buyer can't see.

05

Step 5 of 8

Showings, in Person and on Video

Agents book showings with you ahead of time. Leave for them if you can, put pets and valuables away, and leave the lights on and the shutters open.

A buyer who lives out of state may have their agent walk your home on a video call, the way Ryan and Katie tour homes for their own buyers. Treat it like any other showing.

06

Step 6 of 8

Weigh Every Offer, Then Negotiate

Each offer arrives through the buyer's agent, and each gets one of three answers from you: yes, a counteroffer or no. The price is only part of it; weigh the deposit, the financing, the inspection period, the appraisal and the closing date too.

Ryan, whose bio pairs strong market knowledge with smart negotiation, handles the back and forth. Katie, with her eye for the details, keeps you informed as each deadline comes up.

07

Step 7 of 8

Disclose What You Know

In Johnson v. Davis, Florida's Supreme Court held that a home seller who knows of facts that materially affect its value, which the buyer cannot readily see and does not know, has a duty to disclose them. Your disclosure is where that goes, and a real estate attorney can tell you what applies to your home.

Florida also requires a written flood disclosure at or before the contract: whether you know of flooding that damaged the home while you owned it, and whether you filed a flood claim or received federal flood assistance. A home built before 1978 needs the federal lead-based paint disclosure, and the contract carries the radon and property tax notices.

Sources for Step 7

08

Step 8 of 8

Sign, Record and Collect Your Proceeds

The closing agent, often a title company or a real estate attorney, pays off your mortgage from the buyer's funds and records the deed with the Clerk of the Circuit Court, the county's recorder. Florida charges documentary stamp tax on deeds, and your contract says who pays it.

Already moved away? A Florida online notary can notarize your signature over a video call wherever you are. Confirm wiring instructions for your proceeds by phone, at a number you already have. If you are a foreign person for U.S. tax purposes, the buyer generally has to withhold tax under FIRPTA.

Questions

Before You List: Common Questions

Who decides the commission?

Commission is negotiable and set in the listing agreement. Florida requires every written listing agreement to carry a definite expiration date.

  • Listing agreements, Florida Statutes section 475.25(1)(r)
What if I want to sell the house as is?

You can, and an as-is listing tells buyers you do not plan to make repairs. Florida's disclosure rules still apply, so tell the buyer what you know, and ask a real estate attorney about your contract.

  • Johnson v. Davis, 480 So. 2d 625, as The Florida Bar explains it
Do I have to tell the buyer the house flooded?

Florida's flood disclosure asks whether you know of flooding that damaged the home while you owned it, whether you filed a flood insurance claim and whether you received federal flood assistance. You give it to the buyer at or before the contract.

  • Seller's flood disclosure, Florida Statutes section 689.302
Will my buyer pay the same property taxes I do?

Usually not. A sale resets the assessment to just value on January 1 after closing, and your homestead exemption and Save Our Homes benefit do not pass to the buyer. That is why Florida contracts carry a property tax disclosure.

  • Property taxes for first-time Florida homebuyers, from the Florida Department of Revenue
  • Property tax disclosure, Florida Statutes section 689.261
Can I take my Save Our Homes benefit to my next Florida home?

Most Florida homestead owners can transfer all or part of it, up to $500,000, if they establish the new homestead within three years of January 1 of the year they left the old one. File the transfer form with the homestead application by March 1.

  • Save Our Homes and portability, from the Florida Department of Revenue
  • Portability, from the Manatee County Property Appraiser (2026)
Do I owe tax on the sale?

Florida does not impose a personal income tax. For federal tax, IRS Publication 523 explains the exclusion for the sale of a main home and who qualifies; your tax adviser can tell you what applies to you.

  • Personal income tax, from the Florida Department of Revenue
  • Selling your home, IRS Publication 523
