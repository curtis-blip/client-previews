Behind the Number

So, What Actually Counts as a Real Valuation?

Plenty of websites will spit out a number for your address in three seconds. That number is a useful starting point, an orientation, a rough north star. It is built from public records, recent sales in your zip code, and an algorithm doing its best with limited information.

A real valuation layers human judgment on top. It pulls comparable sales street by street, adjusts for finishes, views, acreage, and the dozen things county data does not see, and reads the current pace of buyer interest in your specific micro-market. The result is a price range you can actually plan around.

The valuation here gives you both. The widget above is the instant on-screen orientation. The personal CMA Christian sends back within 24 hours is the layer of real local judgment on top. Same form, both outputs, no extra step.

The Methodology

How Christian Determines Value

01
Step One

Pull Comparable Sales

I start by pulling every closed sale in your immediate area over the last six to nine months that genuinely compares to your home. Same square footage band, same lot type, same neighborhood feel. Not just whatever the algorithm chose because the zip code matched. The comp set is the foundation of every number that follows, and getting it right is half the work.

02
Step Two

Adjust for Property Specifics

Then I adjust each comp to match your home line by line. A finished basement, a remodeled kitchen, a deeper lot, a south-facing view, a new roof, a detached shop. Every meaningful difference between your property and the comp gets a dollar-value adjustment, up or down. This is the part the automated tools cannot do, because it requires walking the property and reading the listing photos with a trained eye.

03
Step Three

Layer in Active Competition and Days on Market

Sold comps tell you what buyers paid yesterday. Active comps tell you what they have to choose from tomorrow. I overlay the homes currently listed against you, read the days-on-market for similar properties, and adjust the recommended price range to reflect real-time demand. A clean sold-comp number priced into a soft active-market gets you no showings; one priced into a hot active-market leaves money on the table.

04
Step Four

Walk the Comps in Person

For any serious valuation I drive the comps myself. Photos hide a lot. A comp that looks identical online might back to a busy road, sit on a sloped lot you cannot terrace, or have a view your home does not. Walking the comps is how the final number gets tightened from a probable range into a specific recommended listing price you can defend to a buyer's appraiser six weeks later.

Local Value Drivers

What Drives Value Around Buena Vista

Central Colorado homes are not Front Range homes. The factors that move the price up or down in a mountain market are different, and weighting them right is the difference between a strong list price and a stale one.

Views and Acreage

An unobstructed Collegiate Peaks view or a deeper buildable lot can shift a list price by a meaningful margin. View easements and adjacent open space matter too.

Water Rights and Well

Permit type, well yield, water-quality test history, and whether the property has senior or junior water rights all factor into the value, especially on land or rural parcels.

Build Quality and Finishes

Mountain construction has its own checklist. Roof material, insulation, heat source, window quality, and finish level all read differently up here than on the Front Range.

Proximity to Downtown and Schools

Walkability to Main Street Buena Vista, drive time to schools, and access to the river corridor are real value levers. Premium narrows the further you sit from town.

Outdoor Recreation Access

Trailhead proximity, river access, ski drive time to Monarch, and BLM-adjacent parcels carry a premium with the lifestyle-driven buyer pool we have here.

Year-Round Drive-Time Accessibility

Pavement-to-the-door versus seasonal road, snowplow priority, and Highway 285 or 24 corridor access all factor into how buyers value daily livability.
