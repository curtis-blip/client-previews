Buying a condo in Florida

What to Read Before You Buy a Florida Condo

The association's records tell you what the building needs and what it will cost. Ask for them before you sign, and read them before the time to cancel runs out.

  • The condo documents

    Florida law entitles you to a current copy of the declaration, the articles, the bylaws and rules, the yearly financial statement and budget, and the association's frequently asked questions sheet (section 718.503, Florida Statutes). Unless you had them more than seven days before you signed, you can cancel a resale contract in writing within seven days, not counting weekends and legal holidays, of signing it and receiving the documents you asked for.

  • Milestone inspections

    Since the 2022 condo safety law (SB 4-D), buildings three or more habitable stories tall need a milestone inspection at 30 years of age and every 10 years after, or at 25 years where the local building department requires it (Florida DBPR). The inspector's summary is part of the documents you receive.

  • Structural integrity reserve studies

    An association must have a reserve study for each building three stories or higher at least every 10 years, done or checked by a licensed engineer or architect or a credentialed reserve specialist. It covers the roof, the structure, plumbing, electrical systems, windows and the other items the law lists (section 718.112), and for budgets adopted from January 1, 2025, those reserves cannot be waived. Ask for the latest study, and check the Division's reserve study reports.

  • Rental rules

    How soon and how often you can rent a unit is set in the declaration and the rules, and the town or county may add limits of its own. If the association later amends the rules to restrict rentals, the change applies only to owners who agreed to it and owners who buy after it takes effect (section 718.110). The Investment Property page covers the local rental rules.

Questions

Sarasota Condo Questions

Can I buy a Sarasota condo before I move here?

Yes. Florida allows online notarization even when the signer is outside the state (section 117.265), so many buyers sign from where they live now. Ryan or Katie can walk a unit with you on a video call before you make an offer.

Do I need flood insurance for a condo?

Look the address up on FEMA’s flood maps before you make an offer, then ask the association what its master policy covers and what your own unit policy needs to cover. Your lender will tell you what it requires.

How do I know if a building has finished its reserve study?

Ask the association for the latest study: Florida requires it, or a statement that it has not been done, in the documents a resale buyer receives. The Division of Florida Condominiums also lists the reserve study reports associations file with it.

What do condo fees cover?

It depends on the building. The yearly budget in the condo documents shows what the fee pays for, from insurance and upkeep to the reserves. A special assessment for a large repair is billed separately, so ask whether one is planned.
