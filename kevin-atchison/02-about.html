01 The broker

I would rather lose a sale than put someone in a house I know is wrong for them.

I grew up in the Roaring Fork Valley and I never left. I have been a Broker Associate with Property Professionals in Rifle since December 2016, which means I have now sold through a boom, a rate shock and the long flat stretch after it. That matters more than it sounds. I have watched what holds value here and what does not.

The part of this job that decides whether a purchase goes well is almost never the part people expect. It is the well share nobody read. It is the metro district that adds four hundred dollars a month in Battlement Mesa. It is the septic field that needs replacing in two years, which the seller knows and the buyer will not unless someone asks. I ask, and I explain what the answer means before you sign rather than after.

I answer my own phone. There is no team, no assistant and no call screening. If you text me on a Sunday about a house you drove past, you get me, and you get a straight answer about whether it is worth your Monday.

02 How this usually starts

Three ways people end up working with me

Most of my clients arrive in one of these three ways. Whichever one you are, the first conversation is the same: what you are trying to do, and whether this valley can do it.

01

You are buying here for the first time

Usually moving up from the Front Range or down from Aspen, and nobody has explained what a well, a septic field or a metro district does to a monthly payment. The buying process walks through all eight steps, and the programs page covers CHFA and down payment help if this is your first house anywhere.

02

You are selling a house you have owned a long time

You know roughly what the neighbours are asking and you want to know what will actually close. Those are different numbers. How I sell explains how I price, and you can start with an instant estimate before we speak.

03

You are doing both at once, in the same valley

Selling in Rifle and buying in Glenwood, or the reverse. The timing is the whole problem and it is solvable, but only if the two sides are planned together from the start rather than sequenced by accident.

I am the one who answers the phone.

Every call, every showing, every set of closing figures. That is the whole model, and it is why I work one corridor instead of half a state.
