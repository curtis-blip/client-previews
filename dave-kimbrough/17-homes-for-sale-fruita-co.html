Living in Fruita

Between the Book Cliffs trails and the monument's west entrance

William E. Pabor founded Fruita in 1884, and it is a city of 13,395 people today (2020 Census), about 12 road miles west of downtown Grand Junction on I-70.

North of town the BLM's 18 Road trails run up toward the Book Cliffs; to the south, Highway 340 reaches the Colorado National Monument's west entrance in about three miles. In between are downtown, where the Mike the Headless Chicken Festival runs each May, the Fruita Community Center and Dinosaur Journey.

01

The 18 Road trails, north of town

The BLM's 18 Road trails are single-track mountain bike trails at the base of the Book Cliffs, north of Fruita, managed by its Grand Junction Field Office. From I-70 Exit 19 you head north on Cherry Street and follow the county roads to 18 Road; the trailhead is about seven miles up it.

Riding is free. The campground there charges a fee and is booked through Recreation.gov.

02

Kokopelli's Trail and McInnis Canyons

Kokopelli's Trail, a 142-mile multi-use trail to Moab, Utah, starts at the boat launch in Loma, at I-70 Exit 15. Closer in, the BLM's McInnis Canyons National Conservation Area has the Devil's Canyon and Pollock Bench trailheads off Kingsview Road just outside Fruita.

The same conservation area takes in the Mack Ridge mountain bike trails, off-road routes at Rabbit Valley and hiking in the Black Ridge Canyons Wilderness.

03

The monument's west entrance

From I-70 Exit 19, Highway 340 runs south about three miles to Colorado National Monument's west entrance, and the Saddlehorn Visitor Center and campground are four miles past it. The monument, set aside in 1911, keeps its mailing address in Fruita.

Highway 340 crosses the Colorado River at Fruita and carries on east through the Redlands toward Grand Junction.

04

Dinosaur Journey and a headless chicken

Dinosaur Journey, a dinosaur museum run by the Museums of Western Colorado, is in town. So is the story Fruita celebrates every spring: in 1945 a chicken on Lloyd Olsen's farm lived on without its head, and Mike toured with Olsen for about 18 months.

The Mike the Headless Chicken Festival fills downtown toward the end of May with a 5K, a car show, vendors and music. In 2026 it ran May 29 and 30.

05

The town Pabor founded in 1884

William E. Pabor founded Fruita in 1884, seeing a place to grow fruit. James Albert and Alberta Lapham were its first permanent homesteaders, and the Lapham Pioneer Memorial Gazebo stands in Circle Park.

City Hall is on East Aspen Avenue. The Fruita Community Center on North Coulson Street opened in 2011 and holds the Mesa County Libraries branch, a senior center, a gym with a track, and an indoor pool with a lazy river and a water slide.

Questions

Fruita FAQ

How far is Fruita from Grand Junction?

About 12 miles by road from the Civic Center to downtown Grand Junction, roughly 19 minutes on I-70 without traffic.

Where are Fruita's mountain bike trails?

North of town, the BLM's 18 Road trails sit at the base of the Book Cliffs, about seven miles up 18 Road. West of town, McInnis Canyons National Conservation Area has trailheads off Kingsview Road, and Kokopelli's Trail starts at Loma.

Which entrance of the Colorado National Monument is near Fruita?

The west entrance. Take I-70 Exit 19 and head south on Highway 340 for about three miles; the Saddlehorn Visitor Center is four miles past the entrance.

Which school district serves Fruita?

Mesa County Valley School District 51 serves the whole city, including Fruita Monument High School and Fruita Middle School. Confirm the campus for a specific address with the district.
