What we would tell you

Living in Whitney

Whitney is small, and it does not take long to see all of it. There is a downtown, a few streets around it, and then the lots keep getting bigger until you are looking at working land.

The lake sits three miles west and it still sets the pace of the year here. It filled in 1951, and people have been coming out for weekends ever since. Summer feels different from January. If you are looking near the water, ask us which places were built for weekends and which were built to live in, because it is not always obvious from a listing.

Day to day, you drive. Most people who move here already know that and pick it on purpose. Everyday shopping is in town. For anything bigger it is Hillsboro, twelve miles east at the interstate, or Waco, about thirty miles south.

01

In town

The older streets around Main Street. Square lots, close to the schools and to everyday shopping. If being able to walk somewhere matters to you, this is the part of Whitney where you can.

02

Toward the water

Head west out of town and you are into lake country, White Bluff included. Out here the utilities, the road upkeep and the associations can change from one street to the next, so we look into those before you get attached to a place.

03

Past the edge of town

Every other direction is open country. Once you are out here the questions change from square footage to water, septic and how you get in and out. Buying land is a different transaction from buying a house, and we treat it like one.

Joanna's take

"I came into this from design, so I walk into a room and see what it could be. Some of the older places out by the water need work, and that does not put me off at all. What I want is for you to know exactly what you are taking on while there is still time to act on it, so the work stays a project and never becomes a surprise."

What the housing stock actually is

Listing photos make every house look about the same. In person, Whitney is really three kinds of building, and they do not inspect or insure the same way.

01

The original town

Older homes on the original streets. You get what older Texas houses come with: earlier wiring, rooms that were added on over the years, and foundations worth a careful look. Michael came up around building, so this is the part he enjoys.

02

The lake era build-out

The area grew after the lake filled in 1951, and a lot of what went up near the water started life as a weekend place. Some of those have been made into full-time homes and some have not. Ask us which is which before you write an offer.

03

New building and acreage

Newer homes on bigger parcels outside the city limits, and the ongoing building inside White Bluff. The questions out here are water, septic, who maintains the road and how far the utilities reach. We check all four.

What we check for you

None of this is special to Whitney, but it comes up here more than it does in a city. We would rather find it early than have you find it late.

Who controls the shoreline

Lake Whitney belongs to the Corps of Engineers, so the strip between a lake property and the water is very often federal land. Docks, retaining walls and even the vegetation are permitted through the Corps, not the county. We find out what is permitted, what is grandfathered and what actually transfers to you.

Where the water line actually sits

The lake is run for flood control first, so the level moves, sometimes by a lot. If we can, we will show you a place in more than one season. If we cannot, we will at least tell you what the shoreline looks like at both ends.

Water and wastewater

In town you are probably on city service. Outside it, expect a well or rural water and a septic system. We get the septic inspected and the well tested on their own, never folded into the home inspection.

The road to the door

We ask who maintains it. A county road, a private easement and an association street are three different answers, and they cost three different amounts across the years you own the place.

Association dues, and what they buy

White Bluff and several of the lake developments have property owners associations. We get you the current dues, the transfer fees, the amenity rules and the governing documents in writing while you can still act on them.

Insurance, before you fall in love

Older construction, distance from a fire station and closeness to the water all move the premium. We price insurance early, because on some places out here it decides whether the house works for you at all.

Michael's take

"I worked in construction before I ever sold a house, so I still walk through one like a builder. Near the lake, the first thing I want to know is whether a place was built for weekends or built to live in, because that changes the inspection, the insurance and what you ought to offer. It is worth having someone look at it with you."

Whitney is small, so the honest answer is that you can handle most days here and you drive for the rest. Here is roughly how that breaks down.

Groceries and everyday

Brookshire's and Dollar General cover the everyday run, and David's is the locally owned option. For a big shop it is Hillsboro, twelve miles east, or Waco, about thirty miles south.

Eating out

There are a handful of places downtown that have been around a long time, Texas Great Country Cafe and Pie Pantry and Tres Hermanos among them, plus a few spots out by the water.

Parks and outdoors

Lake Whitney State Park is on the east shore with campsites, trails and a boat ramp. McCown Valley Park, also on the lake, has camping and equestrian trails. Most of what people do here happens on or beside the water.

Lake Whitney State Park

Downtown and events

A Main Street with shops and places to eat. Pioneer Days has been running for around fifty years and brings a parade, a pie bake-off and a costume contest through town.

Whitney ISD

There is no bus route through Whitney. You will want a vehicle, though there is a dial-a-ride service for people who need it.

The roads

State Highway 22 runs through town and meets I-35 at Hillsboro, twelve miles east. That is the county seat, and the interstate access for this whole area.

Driving times

About thirty miles south to Waco and about sixty-five miles north to Fort Worth. That drive is why so much of the lake market is people coming down from the Metroplex for the weekend.

Dial-a-ride

The Heart of Texas Rural Transit District runs a weekday service across Hill County and four counties around it. You book a ride ahead rather than catching it at a stop.

Rural transportation

Airports

Hillsboro Municipal is the closest field. Waco Regional is the nearest commercial service, and Dallas Love Field and DFW are the bigger options north.

Common questions

Whitney, answered

Is Whitney actually on Lake Whitney?

Not quite. The city sits about three miles east of the lake. Most of what people call lake property is outside the city limits and closer to the shoreline, which usually means a different situation for utilities, roads and associations than an address in town.

Do I need a permit to build a dock?

Very likely, and from the US Army Corps of Engineers rather than the county, because the Corps controls the Lake Whitney shoreline. If a dock is already there, confirm what it is permitted for and whether that permit transfers to you.

Is White Bluff a separate town?

No. It is a gated community of roughly 3,450 acres in Hill County holding about four miles of Lake Whitney shoreline. It has its own association, dues and rules, but it is not a municipality and it is not a separate school district.

Is there public transportation in Whitney?

No fixed-route service. The Heart of Texas Rural Transit District operates a weekday dial-a-ride across Hill County and four neighboring counties, booked ahead of time. For everything else, plan on driving.

Where do people do their shopping?

Day to day, in town. Anything bigger tends to mean Hillsboro, 12 miles east on State Highway 22 at I-35, or Waco, about 30 miles south.

Can I buy acreage near Whitney?

Yes. Once you are outside the city limits, lots give way to acreage and then to working land. Those purchases turn on water, septic, road access and fencing far more than on square footage. Our farm and ranch page covers what changes when you buy land instead of a lot.
