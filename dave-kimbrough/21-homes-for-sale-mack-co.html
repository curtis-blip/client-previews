A railroad town turned farm community four miles west of Loma, where U.S. Highways 6 and 50 join Interstate 70.

Living in Mack

A railroad camp that became a farming community

Mack grew out of a railroad terminal and construction camp around 1900, and it is named for John Mack, president of the Uintah Railway Company in 1903. It is an unincorporated rural community, so Mesa County is its local government.

Mesa County's 2012 plan for Mack covers about 5 square miles, from Ruby Canyon Estates and Salt Creek on the west to 11 1/2 Road on the east, with Interstate 70 along its southern edge and its northern line near P Road. Fruita is 8 1/2 miles east, the plan says.

01

Named for the Uintah Railway's president

Mack grew out of a railroad terminal, construction camp and settlement around 1900. Mesa County's plan records that it takes its name from John Mack, president of the General Asphalt Company and the Uintah Railway Company in 1903.

The Uintah was a narrow-gauge line built to haul gilsonite from mines in northeast Utah to the Denver and Rio Grande in the Grand Valley. It reached Dragon City, Utah, by late 1904 and closed in 1939. Colorado Parks and Wildlife credits the railway at Mack with a major part in carrying dinosaur fossils to the Smithsonian Institution in the early 1900s.

02

From a railway camp to a farm community

Around 1918 the federal government held a homestead drawing for irrigated or irrigable land around Mack. By 1930, the county's plan says, Mack looked less like a railway town and more like a farming community that had a railroad, with a general store called the Mack Mercantile, a school, a post office and a hotel.

03

Who governs Mack, and where its students go to school

Mack is an unincorporated rural community, so Mesa County plans and governs it under the 2012 Mack Community Plan. The plan names the Mesa County Sheriff and the Colorado State Patrol for law enforcement and the Lower Valley Fire District for fire and EMS.

Elementary students go to school in Loma, and the plan says middle and high school students ride the bus to Fruita. Confirm the campus for any address with District 51.

04

Where Highways 6 and 50 meet I-70

U.S. Highways 6 and 50 come in from the east and join Interstate 70 at Mack. Mesa County maintains the old highway as M.8 Road, and it once carried the county's traffic west into Utah.

The Mack Mesa Airport, at 10 Road and R Road about 2.75 miles north, serves private and general aviation. Grand Junction Regional Airport is 18 miles east, by the plan's count.

05

Canyons and dinosaur fossils to the west

BLM land in the McInnis Canyons National Conservation Area lies to the south and west, with mountain biking at Mack Ridge and on the Kokopelli trails, off-highway trails at Rabbit Valley and hiking in the Black Ridge Canyons Wilderness.

At Rabbit Valley, off I-70 Exit 2, the Trail Through Time is a 1.5-mile loop that begins at the Mygatt-Moore Quarry, where the BLM lists sauropod, armored dinosaur and Allosaurus fossils. Day use has no fee.

Questions

Mack FAQ

Is Mack an incorporated town?

No. Mack is an unincorporated rural community governed by Mesa County. The county's 2012 Mack Community Plan covers about 5 square miles between Salt Creek and 11 1/2 Road, north of Interstate 70.

Which schools serve Mack?

Mesa County Valley School District 51. The county's 2012 plan says the elementary school for the Mack area is in Loma, and middle and high school students ride the bus to Fruita. Confirm the campus for any address with the district.

Who provides fire protection in Mack?

The Lower Valley Fire District covers fire and EMS for the Mack area, according to Mesa County's plan. A homeowner's insurance quote will ask for it, so it helps to know before you make an offer.

How far is Mack from Grand Junction?

About 21 miles by road to downtown Grand Junction, roughly 26 minutes without traffic by OpenStreetMap routing. Loma is about 4 miles east.
