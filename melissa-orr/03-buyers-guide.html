Home/Buyer's Guide

For Buyers

Buying a home in Victoria County

What the process actually looks like here, what the disclosure will not tell you, and where the money really goes.

Start Here

Get the number before you fall in love

The most expensive mistake we see is touring homes before talking to a lender. You lose the house you wanted because someone with a pre-approval moved faster, or you find out at underwriting that the number was never real.

Talk to a lender first. If you do not have one, our local vendor directory lists seven we have closed with, and we take no referral fee from any of them.

The Process

Eight steps, start to keys

01

Talk to a lender

Pre-approval, not pre-qualification. One is a credit-checked commitment, the other is a guess.

  • Ask for the payment with taxes and insurance in it, not principal and interest
  • Rural buyers need a lender comfortable with septic, well and acreage
  • USDA financing covers much of the county outside the city limits
02

Set the real budget

Principal and interest is the small part. Taxes and insurance in this county move the monthly number more than the rate does.

  • Windstorm coverage near the coast can dwarf the inland equivalent
  • Prior foundation work affects insurability as well as price
  • Your ceiling is the monthly payment, not the purchase price
03

Search with a filter that matches

We set up a saved search so new listings reach you the morning they hit the MLS, not three days later.

  • Decide between town, the surrounding towns and acreage first
  • Each one carries a different utility, insurance and lender picture
  • Compare all ten towns in our community guide
04

Tour, and take notes

After four houses they blur. We photograph the things you will forget: the panel, the water heater date, the roof line.

  • Walk the perimeter and watch how water leaves the lot
  • Sticking doors and cracks above frames say something about movement
  • On rural property, ask where the septic field and the well sit
05

Write an offer that holds

Price is one term of seven. Option period, financing deadline and possession often matter more to a seller.

  • Size the option period for the inspections you actually intend to run
  • Decide your response to a low appraisal before you need it
  • On acreage, ask what conveys and who holds the minerals
06

Use the option period properly

This is your window to inspect and walk. Spend the money here, it is the cheapest information you will ever buy.

  • Foundation, because the clay here moves and prior repair is common
  • Septic and water well, which Texas inspects and permits at transfer
  • Termite and roof, usually as separate specialist visits
  • Every trade above sits in our vendor directory
07

Negotiate repairs on facts

Not everything in an inspection report is a defect. We separate the real items from the noise before we ask.

  • Safety, structure and anything blocking the loan come first
  • Cosmetic findings are usually better handled in price than in repairs
  • Documented, warrantied foundation work is an asset, not a red flag
08

Close

Final walkthrough, funds wired to a verified account, keys. We confirm wire instructions by phone, every time.

  • Walk it after the seller is out, not before
  • Confirm wiring instructions by phone, never from an email alone
  • Start utilities the day of closing, not the day after
Local Reality

Six things that surprise buyers here

Foundations move

South Texas clay expands and contracts. Movement is common and not automatically a deal breaker, but you want an engineer's read, not a guess.

Wind insurance on the coast

Toward the bay you may need windstorm coverage through TWIA plus a WPI-8 certificate. Price it before you write the offer.

Well and septic country

Outside city limits you own your water and your wastewater. Both need testing and permit history, and both are real numbers if they fail.

Taxes are the swing factor

Rates vary by district. Check the Victoria County Appraisal District before you assume a monthly payment.

Flood zones are not obvious

A house can sit blocks from water and still be in a mapped zone. Pull the parcel on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

Title work matters on land

Mineral rights, easements and legal access are routine questions out here. Ask them in week one, not at closing.
