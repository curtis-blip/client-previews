Get Your Bearings in Loveland

Loveland was founded in 1877 along the Colorado Central Railroad and incorporated in 1881. The city says it grew from agriculture, with cherry orchards and the sugar beet industry in the early 1900s, and later added employers such as Hewlett-Packard, Teledyne and Hach. Today the whole city is in Larimer County.

Stop 1

Downtown Loveland

Start downtown. The historic district is centered on East 4th Street and roughly bounded by Railroad Avenue and Jefferson Avenue, and the city says it has been the center of economic activity in Loveland since the late 19th century.

  • Listed as a National Historic District in the National Register of Historic Places in 2015
  • Centered on East 4th Street
  • Roughly bounded by Railroad Avenue and Jefferson Avenue

Stop 2

The Loveland Museum

The Loveland Museum is at 503 N. Lincoln Avenue. Its own site tells the story this way:

  • Founded in 1937 by local author, collector, curator and mountain guide Harold Dunning
  • Dunning began collecting pioneer artifacts and stories as early as 1919
  • An accredited art and history museum

Stop 3

Benson Sculpture Garden

The garden is at 1125 W. 29th Street, at the corner of 29th Street and Beech Drive, across from Loveland High School. The city’s page lists:

  • Free to walk through and view the sculptures
  • More than 150 pieces of sculpture
  • Walking paths, benches and ponds

Stop 4

Boyd Lake State Park

Boyd Lake State Park is a Colorado state park at 3720 N. County Road 11-C. Colorado Parks and Wildlife describes it this way:

  • A water-recreation haven in northern Colorado
  • A lake at the westernmost edge of the plains, by the foot of Longs Peak
  • A daily vehicle pass is sold at the entrance

Before you buy

What to Check on Any Loveland Address

Four things that change from one side of town to the other. Each takes a few minutes to look up and is worth doing before you write an offer.

Five Hidden Costs Before Moving to Northern Colorado

12:37 · Costs

  1. Which county is it in?

    Loveland is in Larimer County, and the City’s own quick facts say so. Look a parcel up in the Larimer County property search.

  2. Which schools go with the address?

    Thompson School District covers about 98 percent of the city by area and Poudre School District about 2 percent. Thompson says its attendance maps are general guidance, so use its School Locator with the street address for a precise answer.

  3. Is it in the floodplain?

    The City’s Flood Management page lists the current Big Thompson River floodplain maps and newly adopted Lake Loveland floodplain maps. It also points to the state’s hazard mapping site, where an address can be entered for a flood risk report, and names city engineers to call about a property.

  4. Is there a metro district or special district?

    A metro district is a special district with taxing authority that pays for infrastructure. The City of Loveland adopted Title 20 of its municipal code in 2024 to oversee them and has a page for prospective homebuyers. The property record or tax bill names any district on an address.

Quick answers

Loveland Questions Buyers Ask

What county is Loveland in?

Loveland is in Larimer County, according to the City’s own quick facts.

What school district is Loveland in?

Mostly Thompson School District, with a small part in Poudre School District. Thompson’s School Locator gives the exact answer for an address.

What is a metro district, and will my house be in one?

The City describes a metro district as a special district with taxing authority that pays for infrastructure and services. Whether an address is in one shows on the property record or tax bill, and the City’s metro district page has a section for homebuyers.

How do I check flood risk on a Loveland address?

The City publishes Big Thompson River and Lake Loveland floodplain maps and links a state tool where an address can be entered for a flood risk report.
