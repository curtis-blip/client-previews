Get Your Bearings in Lafayette

Mary Miller platted Lafayette in February 1888, setting aside 150 acres of her farm after she found coal there, and she named the town for her late husband, Lafayette Miller. Voters approved incorporation on April 2, 1889, and the whole city sits in Boulder County today.

Stop 1

Old Town and the History Museum

Start where Mary Miller’s town began. The city describes Old Town as stretches of Baseline Road, South Public Road, East Simpson Street and North Harrison Avenue, and the Bob L. Burger Recreation Center, which opened in 1990, is at 111 W. Baseline Road. The Lafayette History Museum is on Simpson Street:

  • Museum at 108 E. Simpson St., in the 1890s Lewis Home, a coal miner’s cottage on the National Register of Historic Places
  • Sits on the town’s original main street, across from the old City Hall
  • Called the Lafayette Miners Museum from 1976 until 2022
  • Miller’s street names came from her own places and people: Geneseo for her birthplace, Cleveland for the president

Stop 2

Waneka Lake Park

Waneka Lake Park is at 1600 Caria Drive. The city describes it as a recreational and wildlife refuge, and lists:

  • A 1.2 mile fitness trail
  • Paddle boat and canoe rentals from the boathouse in summer
  • Dogs allowed on leash

Stop 3

City Trails and the Coal Creek Trail

The city lists about 20 miles of trails that connect neighborhoods, business areas and neighboring communities, with segments of the Coal Creek and Rock Creek regional trails among them. Boulder County’s Coal Creek Trail has a trailhead on Public Road:

  • About 20 miles of city trails
  • The county’s Coal Creek Trail is 14 miles, soft surface, from 66th Street in Superior to Erie
  • Public Road trailhead in Lafayette

Before you buy

What to Check on Any Lafayette Address

Four things that change from one side of town to the other. Each takes a few minutes to look up and is worth doing before you write an offer.

Five Hidden Costs Before Moving to Northern Colorado

12:37 · Costs

  1. Which county is it in?

    All of Lafayette is in Boulder County, so the Boulder County assessor holds the record. Look an address up in the Boulder County property search.

  2. Which schools go with the address?

    The whole city is in Boulder Valley School District. The BVSD school finder shows the neighborhood schools by address, and the district says new students are guaranteed a seat at their neighborhood attendance area school.

  3. Is the address in a floodplain?

    The city’s zoning page points to FEMA’s Map Service Center for floodplain information, and Boulder County publishes floodplain maps for 21 mapped drainages. The county says its rules cover unincorporated land, so ask the City of Lafayette which rules apply to the address.

  4. Who provides water and sewer?

    The City of Lafayette runs its own water and wastewater service, and says its source water comes from snowmelt in the Rocky Mountains. Ask the city to confirm service for the address.

Quick answers

Lafayette Questions Buyers Ask

Which county is Lafayette in?

Boulder County, entirely. The Census county layer puts the whole city there.

Which school district serves Lafayette?

Boulder Valley School District serves the city. Use the district’s school finder to see the neighborhood schools for a specific address.

How did Lafayette get its name?

Mary Miller platted the town in 1888 and named it for her late husband, Lafayette Miller, who died in 1878.

Where can I walk or paddle in Lafayette?

Waneka Lake Park has a 1.2 mile fitness trail and summer paddle boat and canoe rentals from the boathouse. The city also lists about 20 miles of trails, including segments of the Coal Creek and Rock Creek regional trails.
