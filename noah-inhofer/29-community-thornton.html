Get Your Bearings in Thornton

Thornton began in 1952, when developer Sam Hoffman bought 400 acres of the old Eppinger family farm north of Denver to build an affordable, self-sustaining community. The first three homes were finished in 1953, and the city is named for Governor Dan Thornton. Today it is a home rule city, almost all of it in Adams County.

Stop 1

Dorothy Boulevard: Fire Museum and Arts Center

Start in the original Thornton. The fire station at 9451 Dorothy Boulevard went up in 1958 as a combined fire station and city building, and it now holds the Ken Freiberg Fire Museum. The Thornton Arts & Culture Center is a short way down the same street. A few facts from the city:

  • Station and city building paid for by $130,000 in bonds voters approved in November 1957
  • Ken Freiberg organized the volunteer fire department in 1954, and the museum is named for him
  • Arts & Culture Center at 9209 Dorothy Boulevard, home of the OZ Gallery
  • OZ Gallery open Tuesday to Friday, 12 to 5 p.m., and second Saturdays, 10 a.m. to 2 p.m.

Stop 2

Thornton Community Center

The Community Center is at 2211 Eppinger Boulevard. The city lists what is inside:

  • Gymnasium
  • Indoor walking and jogging track
  • Dance studio
  • Teen space
  • Skatepark

Stop 3

Carpenter Park

Carpenter Park is at 3498 E. 112th Avenue, directly behind the Carpenter Recreation Center, and the city keeps it open daily from 6 a.m. to 11 p.m. The city lists these amenities:

  • Carpenter Park Lake and trails
  • Harley Brown Amphitheater, seating for up to 500
  • Skatepark, tennis courts and playgrounds
  • Paddle boats and canoes in season; private boats are not allowed on the lake

Stop 4

Trail Winds Recreation Center

At the north end of the tour, the Trail Winds Recreation Center is at 13495 Holly Street. The city’s list of what it offers:

  • Indoor pool with a lazy river and a water slide
  • Climbing wall
  • Indoor track
  • Active Adult Wing

Before you buy

What to Check on Any Thornton Address

Four things that change from one side of town to the other. Each takes a few minutes to look up and is worth doing before you write an offer.

Five Hidden Costs Before Moving to Northern Colorado

12:37 · Costs

  1. Which county is it in?

    Nearly all of Thornton is in Adams County, and the Census shows small slivers in Weld and Broomfield counties. Look an address up in the Adams County real property search to see the parcel and its county.

  2. Which schools go with the address?

    Four districts cover parts of the city: Adams 12 Five Star, School District 27J, Mapleton and Adams 14. The largest share is Adams 12, and its boundary maps show each neighborhood school’s area. The other districts have their own lookups. Use the street address and confirm the campus with the district before you write an offer.

  3. Is it in a floodplain?

    Adams County Floodplain Management explains how to find out whether a property is in a floodplain and links an interactive FEMA map. FEMA’s Map Service Center is the federal lookup. Check the map for any address you are serious about.

  4. Who provides water, and is there a special district?

    The city runs Thornton Water for its customers, and new residents start service through Utility Billing at the City of Thornton. Some developments pay for public improvements through special districts, so check the state’s special district map for the address. Gas and electric come from Xcel Energy or United Power, depending on the address.

Thornton Questions Buyers Ask

What county is Thornton in?

Almost all of Thornton is in Adams County. The Census also shows small slivers in Weld and Broomfield counties, so look up the parcel on the county assessor’s site.

Which school district serves Thornton?

Four districts cover parts of the city: Adams 12 Five Star Schools, School District 27J, Mapleton and Adams 14. Which one serves a home depends on its address, so confirm it with the district.

How did Thornton start?

Developer Sam Hoffman bought 400 acres in 1952 to build an affordable, self-sustaining community. The first three homes were completed in 1953, and the city is named for Governor Dan Thornton.

How do I start water service in Thornton?

Contact Thornton Utility Billing at 303-538-7370. Gas and electric come from Xcel Energy or United Power, depending on the address.
