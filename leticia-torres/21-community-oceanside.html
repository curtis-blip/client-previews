Neighborhoods

Oceanside Neighborhoods

Oceanside splits into four quarters. Here is what you will find in each one.

Northwest Oceanside

San Luis Rey Valley and Ivey Ranch

  • San Luis Rey Valley

    Home to Mission San Luis Rey, founded in 1798. The City says the valley grew fast in the 1960s and 70s.

  • Ivey Ranch

    Around Ivey Ranch Park, a nonprofit ranch park at 110 Rancho del Oro Drive.

Northeast Oceanside

Morro Hills and Guajome

  • Morro Hills

    A master-planned area where several hundred single-family homes were built in the 2000s, the City's last large new-home project.

  • South Morro Hills

    The City's one remaining farming area, about 3,440 acres, with 2,620 acres in crops.

  • Guajome

    Guajome Regional Park, a county park at 3000 Guajome Lake Road with trails, fishing and 33 campsites.

Southwest Oceanside

Downtown, Capistrano, Loma Alta and Fire Mountain

  • Downtown

    Centered on Mission Avenue and Coast Highway, near the pier, the harbor and the Oceanside Transit Center.

  • Capistrano

    East of Interstate 5 near the harbor, built up since the 1950s.

  • Loma Alta

    A hillside neighborhood east of Interstate 5, built up since the 1950s and named for Loma Alta Creek nearby.

  • Fire Mountain

    A hillside neighborhood just east of the coastal area, built up since the 1950s.

Southeast Oceanside

Rancho Del Oro, Ocean Hills, Mira Costa and El Corazon

  • Rancho Del Oro

    Farmland turned into homes, shopping and business parks in the 1980s and 90s, with a SPRINTER station at 3513 Oceanside Boulevard.

  • Ocean Hills

    One of the large master-planned areas, with Rancho Del Oro and Morro Hills, where the City's growth has happened since the 1980s.

  • Mira Costa

    Home to MiraCosta College's Oceanside campus at 1 Barnard Drive.

  • El Corazon

    A 450-acre open space in the middle of the city that the City is developing as a regional recreation area.

Living Here

What It's Like to Live Here

The places people in Oceanside go on a weekend.

  • Oceanside Pier

    A wooden pier about a quarter of a mile long, rebuilt in 1987.

  • Oceanside Harbor

    The small craft harbor opened in 1963, with Harbor Beach beside it.

  • Mission San Luis Rey

    The 1798 mission on Mission Avenue, a National Historic Landmark with a museum.

  • Oceanside Museum of Art

    The art museum at 704 Pier View Way, downtown.

  • California Surf Museum

    Surf history at 312 Pier View Way, open daily 10 to 4.

  • Buena Vista Audubon Nature Center

    A nature center at 2202 S. Coast Highway with a trail around Buena Vista Lagoon.

  • Guajome Regional Park

    A county park at 3000 Guajome Lake Road with picnic lawns, trails and camping.

  • Sunset Market

    A food and music street fair on Pier View Way every Thursday from 5 to 9 p.m.

Schools

Which School Goes With Your Address

Four school districts serve Oceanside, and the address decides which one. Confirm with the district before you buy.

  • Oceanside Unified

    Serves most of the city, kindergarten to grade 12. School locator.

  • Vista Unified

    Serves a large part of eastern Oceanside, kindergarten to grade 12. District site.

  • Bonsall Unified

    Serves part of the city, kindergarten to grade 12. District site.

  • Carlsbad Unified

    Serves a small part of the city, kindergarten to grade 12. Look up an address.

Before You Buy

Before You Make an Offer

Check these on any home before you write an offer.

  1. 01

    Coastal Zone

    The City's Coastal Zone runs from the inland side of Coast Highway to the ocean, plus land near the San Luis Rey River, Loma Alta Creek and Buena Vista Lagoon. Some projects there need a coastal permit. The City's Local Coastal Program page.

  2. 02

    Short-⁠Term Rentals

    New non-hosted short-term rentals are prohibited outside the Coastal Zone, and permits renew every year. The City's rental rules.

  3. 03

    Flood Maps

    Near the coast, the San Luis Rey River and the lagoons, read FEMA's flood maps for the address.

  4. 04

    Water and School Districts

    The City sells water to most homes, but Vista Irrigation District and Rainbow Municipal Water District serve parts of Oceanside. Four school districts serve the city. Both depend on the address.
