What it takes to sell for what the home is genuinely worth, in a state where the sale prices are not public.

The price is set before the first showing.

Almost every difficult listing traces back to the same thing: it launched at a number that could not be defended, sat, and then chased the market down. The homes that sell well are priced against real closed data on day one and presented properly from the first photograph.

We sold 88 homes in the last twelve months, about $25.5M in volume, at an average price of $351,000. Below is exactly how we run a listing.

The Process

How we run a listing

Price against real closed data

In a non-disclosure state this is the whole ball game. We pull actual closed prices from the NTREIS MLS, not portal estimates, and we show you the comparable sales so you can see the reasoning rather than take a number on faith.

Decide what is worth doing first

Not every improvement returns its cost. We walk the house and tell you plainly which repairs will show up in the offer and which ones will not. Frequently the honest answer is paint, light fixtures, landscaping and nothing else.

Prepare and photograph properly

Staging, decluttering and professional photography, plus video and aerial where the property justifies it. Buyers filter on the first image. On acreage and lake property the aerial is not optional.

Launch across the MLS and the portals

The listing goes live on NTREIS and syndicates out from there. We also work our own buyer pipeline and the agents we deal with regularly in these markets, which is where a meaningful share of our sales originate.

Manage showings and feedback

Showings get scheduled, confirmed and followed up. You get the feedback as it comes in, including the feedback you would rather not hear, because that is what tells us whether the price or the presentation needs to move.

Negotiate the whole offer

Price, option period, financing terms, closing date, what conveys, repair obligations. A slightly lower offer with clean terms often nets more than a higher one that falls apart in week three.

Work the option period and appraisal

The buyer inspects, and repair requests come back. We advise on what is reasonable to concede and what is not. If the appraisal comes in low, we have the closed comparables ready to challenge it.

Close

Title work, final walkthrough, funding. We handle the coordination and stay reachable through it.

What sellers ask us

We pull the actual closed figures from the NTREIS MLS, which licensed agents can access and the public cannot. Then we adjust for the things a model cannot see: condition, updates, lot, orientation, whether the comparable backed onto a road.

This is the single clearest argument against pricing from an online estimate in this state. The portals are modelling a number they have never observed.

Usually less than you think. Paint, lighting, landscaping and decluttering reliably return more than they cost. Kitchens and bathrooms often do not return their full cost, particularly in the price bands where most of our business sits.

We will walk the house and give you a short, specific list. If the honest answer is that a repair will not pay for itself, we will tell you that.

Commission is negotiable and is agreed in writing before we list. Beyond that, expect title policy, survey if one is needed, any agreed repairs and prorated taxes at closing. We give you a written net sheet before you sign, so you know what you walk away with rather than guessing.
