Homes for Sale in Whitney, Texas

A Hill County town three miles east of a 23,500 acre lake. How this market actually breaks down, what to check before you buy into it, and every listing on the market right now.

Hill County, Texas

The town is three miles from the water

Whitney sits in Hill County, about three miles east of the lake that carries its name. That short gap is the first thing to understand about buying here, because it splits the market cleanly in two.

In town you are buying a small Texas town, laid out in 1876 around the cotton railroad. Out toward the water you are buying into a US Army Corps of Engineers reservoir with roughly 225 miles of shoreline, and a completely different set of rules, costs and questions comes with it.

Plenty of people arrive looking at one half of that and buy in the other. It is worth walking both before you decide which one you are actually shopping for.

Three different purchases

Where people actually look

"Whitney" covers three markets that share a name and almost nothing else. They price differently, they inspect differently, and they suit completely different people.

01

White Bluff

A gated community of roughly 3,450 acres holding about four miles of Lake Whitney shoreline, with two golf courses, a marina, lodging and its own restaurants and pools. It works like a small resort town sitting inside Hill County. It also carries a property owners association, dues and rules, so buying here means buying the amenities as deliberately as you buy the house.

02

The waterfront and the access lots

Lake Whitney is a flood control reservoir the Corps of Engineers built on the Brazos River, and the Corps still controls the shoreline. That one fact shapes everything on this side of the market: what you can build, what you can put in the water, and how far the water sits from the back door in a given season. Lake access and true waterfront are not the same purchase, and the difference is worth understanding before you tour.

03

In town, and the land around it

Whitney itself is small in the way an 1876 railroad town is small, with a compact grid and square lots. Drive a few minutes out and those lots give way to acreage, then to working land. A good number of buyers start out set on a lake house and end up here instead, once they have walked both.

What you are looking at

What the housing stock actually is

Listing photos flatten everything into the same house. On the ground, Whitney is three distinct kinds of building, and they inspect and insure very differently.

01

The original town

Older frame and masonry homes on square lots inside the first street grid, running from the railroad and cotton years forward. Expect what older Texas houses come with: earlier wiring, rooms added over time, and foundations that deserve a close look.

02

The lake era build-out

The Brazos was impounded in 1951 and the area grew around the new lake. A large share of what went up near the water was built as weekend property rather than year-round housing, and it was built to that standard. Whether a house was meant to be lived in every day is a real question here, not a technicality.

03

New building and acreage

Newer construction on larger parcels outside the city limits, plus the continuing build-out inside White Bluff. Different questions apply out here: water source, septic, who maintains the road, and how far the utilities actually run.

Michael's take

"I spent years in construction before I ever sold a house, so I look at these places the way a builder does. On anything near the lake the first thing I want to know is whether it was built to be lived in year round or built for weekends, because that one answer changes the inspection, the insurance and the offer. You cannot tell from the photos."

The fixed points

What the area is built around

Four things that anchor this market, none of which are going anywhere.

Lake Whitney

About 23,500 acres of impounded Brazos River with roughly 225 miles of shoreline, operated by the Corps of Engineers for flood control, hydroelectric power and water supply.

Corps lake information

Lake Whitney State Park

775 acres on the east shore, leased from the Army by Texas Parks and Wildlife since 1954. Camping, day use and public water access without owning a foot of shoreline.

Texas Parks and Wildlife

Whitney Dam

A 166 foot concrete and earthfill dam the Corps closed on the Brazos in 1951, built for flood control and power. It is the reason the lake exists, and the reason most of this market exists.

White Bluff

3,450 acres and about four miles of shoreline, with two golf courses, a marina, lodging and a full amenity list, operating as a gated community on the lake.

White Bluff Resort
Schools

The district serving Whitney

The town is served by Whitney Independent School District, which runs four campuses: Whitney Elementary for pre-kindergarten through second grade, Whitney Intermediate for third through fifth, Whitney Middle School and Whitney High School.

Attendance boundaries change over time, and an address outside the city limits is not automatically inside the district. Confirm the assignment for a specific property with the district directly before you write an offer.

Before you write an offer

What to check before you buy here

None of this is unique to Whitney, but all of it comes up here more than it does in a city, and it is easier to check early than to unwind later.

Who controls the shoreline

Lake Whitney is a Corps of Engineers reservoir, so the land between a lake property and the water is very often federal. Docks, retaining walls and shoreline vegetation are permitted through the Corps rather than the county. Ask what is permitted, what is grandfathered, and what transfers with the sale.

Where the water line actually sits

The lake is operated for flood control first. Levels move, sometimes considerably. See a property in more than one season if you can, and at minimum ask what the view and the shoreline look like at both ends of the range.

Water and wastewater

Inside the city limits you are likely on municipal service. Outside it, expect a well or a rural water supply and a septic system. Have the septic inspected and the well tested as their own line items, separately from the home inspection.

The road to the door

Ask who maintains it. A county road, a private easement and an association street are three different answers, with three very different long-term costs and three different winter stories.

Association dues, and what they buy

White Bluff and several lake developments carry property owners associations. Get the current dues, the transfer fees, the amenity rules and the governing documents in writing before your option period runs out, not after.

Insurance, before you fall in love

Older lake-era construction, distance from a fire station and proximity to water all move the premium. Price the insurance early. On some properties out here it is the number that decides the deal.

Joanna's take

"My side of this is what a house can become. A cabin from the lake's early years is not a bad house, it is an unfinished one, and I would rather show someone what it could be than talk them out of it. The ones that disappoint are the ones nobody looked at closely while there was still time to walk away."

Common questions

Whitney, answered

Is Whitney actually on Lake Whitney?

Not quite. The city sits about three miles east of the lake. Most of what people call lake property is outside the city limits and closer to the shoreline, which usually means a different situation for utilities, roads and associations than an address in town.

Which school district serves Whitney?

Whitney Independent School District, with four campuses in town: an elementary, an intermediate, a middle school and a high school. Boundaries change over time and addresses outside the city limits are not automatically included, so confirm a specific property with the district.

Do I need a permit to build a dock?

Very likely, and from the US Army Corps of Engineers rather than the county, because the Corps controls the Lake Whitney shoreline. If a dock is already there, confirm what it is permitted for and whether that permit transfers to you.

Is White Bluff a separate town?

No. It is a gated community of roughly 3,450 acres in Hill County holding about four miles of Lake Whitney shoreline, with two golf courses, a marina and lodging. It has its own association, dues and rules, but it is not a municipality and it is not a separate school district.

How far is Whitney from Waco, Fort Worth and I-35?

Roughly 30 miles to Waco and about 65 miles to Fort Worth. I-35 is about 12 miles east at Hillsboro, the Hill County seat, by way of State Highway 22.

Can I buy acreage near Whitney?

Yes. Once you are outside the city limits, lots give way to acreage and then to working land. Those purchases turn on water, septic, road access and fencing far more than on square footage. Our farm and ranch page covers what changes when you buy land instead of a lot.
