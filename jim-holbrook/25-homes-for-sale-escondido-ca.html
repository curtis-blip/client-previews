Escondido at a glance

A valley town from 1888

Escondido is the inland city at the junction of State Route 78 and Interstate 15, about 31 miles by road north of downtown San Diego. It became a city on October 8, 1888, and counted more than 150,000 people at the 2020 Census.

Escondido is Spanish for hidden, and the city sits in a valley. Jim covers it as part of North County San Diego: State Route 78 runs west from here through San Marcos and Vista to Oceanside, and the SPRINTER rail line follows the same corridor. Inside the city limits are the town site laid out in 1886, a historic district of older houses and City lakes in the hills. Just outside them are unincorporated county communities and the City of San Diego's San Pasqual Valley.

01

A town laid out in 1886

Escondido grew on the Rancho Rincon del Diablo, a Mexican land grant made to Juan Bautista Alvarado in 1843. In 1886 the Escondido Land and Town Company bought the rancho, laid out the town site and divided the valley into small farms for grapes and citrus. It also offered the San Diego Central Railroad a bonus to build a line in from the coast. The city incorporated on October 8, 1888.

Grape Day Park, at 321 North Broadway, is a landmark on the City's local historic register, and City Hall is on the same street at 201 North Broadway. The Escondido Public Library is on South Kalmia Street, and its Pioneer Room holds the city's local history collection.

02

Old Escondido's historic homes

The City created the Old Escondido Historic District in 1989. Its roughly 900 homes, from small Craftsman bungalows to Victorians, were built from the mid-1880s on, between 4th and 13th avenues and between Escondido Boulevard and Chestnut Street. Much of the neighborhood's design, its street trees and its sandstone curbs and walls, dates from 1914 to 1920.

Owning a house here comes with rules. Exterior changes, from paint and windows to fences, need a certificate of appropriateness from the City, whether or not the house is on the historic register. Some houses also carry a Mills Act contract with conditions of its own.

03

The lakes and Daley Ranch

Escondido runs its own water system. The joint Escondido-Vista canal carries water from Lake Henshaw to Lake Wohlford, the City stores water in Dixon Lake, and the Escondido-Vista Water Treatment Plant treats it. Rincon del Diablo Municipal Water District serves parts of the city too, so which one serves a house depends on the address.

Both lakes are City parks with fishing, and Dixon Lake has a campground. North of it, the City bought Daley Ranch in 1996: 3,201 acres of oak woodland, sage scrub, chaparral and grassland, managed as a conservation area with more than 25 miles of trails for hiking, biking and riding.

04

Past the city limits

The city limits stop short of several nearby communities. Harmony Grove, Hidden Meadows, Elfin Forest and Del Dios are unincorporated San Diego County, and the Census Bureau counts each as a place of its own. The County, not the City, decides what can be built there.

South of the city, the San Pasqual Valley belongs to the City of San Diego, whose Public Utilities Department owns most of its 14,000 acres. A 1964 plan set the valley aside as an agricultural and open space preserve. San Pasqual Battlefield State Historic Park, on State Route 78, marks the battle fought there on December 6, 1846.

What Jim would check in Escondido

  • City or county Harmony Grove, Hidden Meadows, Elfin Forest and Del Dios are outside the city limits, so county zoning and permits apply there. Ask Jim which side of the line a house is on.
  • Historic rules In Old Escondido, or for a house on the City's historic lists, exterior work needs City review first, and a Mills Act contract carries its own terms (the City's historic preservation page).
  • Mello-Roos Newer neighborhoods such as The Villages, on the former Escondido Country Club, and Eureka Ranch sit inside City Community Facilities Districts. The special tax is on the property tax bill.
  • Flood and fire maps Look the address up on FEMA's flood maps and on CAL FIRE's fire hazard maps, which can affect insurance and building rules, especially in the hills.

Questions

Escondido FAQ

Are Harmony Grove and Hidden Meadows part of Escondido?

No. Harmony Grove, Hidden Meadows, Elfin Forest and Del Dios are unincorporated San Diego County, outside the city limits, so county zoning and permits apply there. Ask Jim which side of the line a house is on.

Which school districts serve Escondido?

The City lists three: Escondido Union School District, Escondido Union High School District and San Pasqual Union School District. Confirm the schools for a specific address with the district.

What comes with a house in the Old Escondido Historic District?

The City created the district in 1989. Owners need a certificate of appropriateness before exterior changes such as paint, windows or fences, whether or not the house is on the historic register.

How far is Escondido from San Diego and Temecula?

By road from downtown, downtown San Diego and San Diego International are each about 31 miles south on Interstate 15. Old Town Temecula is about 28 miles north, and Carlsbad about 20 miles west on State Route 78.
