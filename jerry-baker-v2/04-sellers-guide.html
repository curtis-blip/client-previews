The process

Eight steps from first conversation to closing

01

Walk the property together

Jerry comes out and sees what you actually own, including the access, the water situation and anything a buyer's inspector is going to find. Pricing from a desk is how mountain listings end up wrong in both directions.

02

Price it from real comparables

Land comparables in Gilpin County are thin, and the ones that exist need adjusting for acreage, slope, access and permits. This is the step another firm told one of Jerry's clients was impossible. It is not impossible, it is work.

03

Assemble the paperwork buyers will ask for

Well permit, septic records, plat, recorded access, mineral rights position, any covenants. Having these ready shortens your time on market more than any amount of staging, because it removes the reasons a buyer hesitates.

04

Photograph and map it properly

Raw land photographs badly from the ground. What sells it is aerial context showing the parcel in its setting, the boundary drawn on the image, and honest pictures of the road in. Buyers who feel misled walk away at the walkthrough.

05

List and syndicate

The MLS feeds the portals, and the portals are where out of state buyers find Gilpin County. Several of Jerry's closings started with someone searching from another state who had never been here.

06

Show it, and vet the buyers

One repeat client put it plainly: Jerry weeded out several potential buyers who came in too low or had questionable financing and would have wasted his time, then found the one that fit. Land deals fall apart at financing more often than houses do.

07

Negotiate

Jerry holds the Master Certified Negotiation Expert designation. On thin comparables and an emotional asset, which describes most inherited mountain ground, the negotiation is where the price is actually set.

08

Close, and stay on the paperwork

The reviews are consistent about this stage. He keeps on top of the closing documents and tells you where things stand without being asked. That is the whole job in the last two weeks.

Marketing

How mountain ground actually gets sold

Different from a suburban listing, because the buyer pool is smaller, further away, and asking different questions.

Aerial and boundary imagery

Context from above with the parcel line drawn on it. This is the single highest impact thing on a land listing.

The diligence pack up front

Permits, plat and access published with the listing instead of held back. Fewer questions, fewer walkaways.

Reaching the Front Range

Most buyers for this ground live in Denver and Boulder, not in Gilpin County. Syndication and search matter more than a sign.

A sign that gets called

One buyer found the land she wanted by spotting Jerry's sign on the side of it and calling. In rural country the sign still works.

Choosing an agent

A land specialist versus a general residential agent

This is not a knock on residential agents. It is that mountain land is a different product, and the gap shows up in specific places.

A general residential agent

  • Prices from price per acre, because that is the available shortcut
  • May not know a household well permit is unavailable until the buyer's lender asks
  • Photographs land the way they photograph a house
  • Sometimes declines the listing outright when access is difficult

Black Hawk Real Estate

  • Adjusts comparables for access, slope, water and permits before quoting a number
  • Checks the permit position at the start, so it is not a surprise under contract
  • Markets with aerial context, drawn boundaries and honest road photos
  • Takes the difficult ones. The jeep road parcel up Russell Gulch sold at the number, on schedule
