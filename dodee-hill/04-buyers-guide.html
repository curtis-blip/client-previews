Buyer's Guide

First-time home buyer in Texas, your steady hand on the Coastal Bend

Seven steps from pre-approval to keys, written for buyers in Corpus Christi and the Gulf.

Whether you're a first time home buyer in Texas, a military family PCS-ing to NAS Corpus Christi, or moving up the Coastal Bend coast, the path is the same: clarity at every step.

Dodee leads with listening. Every home gets the same honest lens: what it costs to own on the Coastal Bend, what windstorm and flood insurance run for that ZIP, what comparable homes in Southside Corpus Christi, Flour Bluff, Portland, and North Padre actually closed at, and where the tradeoffs live. No pressure, no rushed decisions, no glossing over a sagging foundation or an aging AC. You get full transparency on the numbers and the gut-check, so the home you choose holds up for the next decade, not just the next showing.

How to buy a home in Texas, the steady-hand version

The home buying process in Texas, written for the Corpus Christi and Coastal Bend reality.

01 Step 01

Pre-approval, lenders, and a real budget

Pre-qualification is a soft estimate over the phone. Pre-approval means a lender pulled your credit, reviewed your income, and put a real number behind your offer. In a Corpus Christi multiple-offer situation, only the second one carries weight. Texas first time home buyer programs (TSAHC, TDHCA My First Texas Home, mortgage credit certificates), FHA at 3.5 percent down, conventional from 3 to 5 percent, and VA at zero down for active-duty and veterans are all on the table for the right buyer.

Expect to pull 60 days of bank statements, two recent pay stubs, two years of tax returns, and a clean credit pull. Dodee introduces you to local lenders who know NAS-CC VA files, Coastal Bend insurance quirks, and Nueces County title companies. You pick. No kickbacks, no pressure on which one.

02 Step 02

Define needs vs. wants for the Coastal Bend

Before you scroll a single listing, the conversation should be about life, not square footage. Are you commuting to NAS Corpus Christi off Ocean Drive, or to NAS Kingsville? Are the kids zoned to Flour Bluff ISD, or is Calallen the priority? Do you want a Southside cul-de-sac with mature live oaks, a Portland lot across the bay, or a beach house on North Padre? How close to the water is too close once windstorm and flood premiums are in the mix?

Dodee walks through the real Coastal Bend tradeoffs: hurricane evacuation zones, Tier 1 versus Tier 2 windstorm areas, school zones, HOA personality, drive times to the bases, and which streets get standing water after a regular Gulf rain. The bedroom count is the easy part. Everything around it is what you'll actually live with.

03 Step 03

Tour homes, in person and virtual for relocators

Staging is designed to make you fall in love. Dodee's job on a tour is also to help you fall a little out of love, on purpose, so you see the house clearly. Roof age. AC date stamp. Salt-air corrosion at the AC coils, fasteners, and exterior fixtures. Foundation walk. Drainage at the back of the lot. Window seals on a coastal home. What's behind the staged furniture, what's under the new carpet.

For military families PCS-ing in or out-of-state buyers planning a move, Dodee runs full FaceTime walkthroughs, captures detailed video, and pulls flood and windstorm history before you book a flight. If a home is a contender after the virtual tour, the in-person trip is an inspection trip, not a discovery one.

04 Step 04

Make the offer, the Texas way

Texas runs on TREC contracts, not a freelance template. The levers that win houses without writing a bigger check are the option period (your paid right to walk for any reason, usually 5 to 10 days), earnest money (typically 1 percent of price held by a Coastal Bend title company), the financing contingency, the appraisal posture, and the closing date.

Price is the headline. Terms are what actually win houses in a competitive Southside or Padre Island market. Dodee walks you through each lever, when to use an escalation, when an appraisal gap is smart, and when to hold your number. The goal is to win on terms you still feel good about thirty days later when the inspection report lands.

05 Step 05

Inspections, the coastal version

A standard general inspection is the floor in Corpus Christi, not the ceiling. The Coastal Bend punch list adds foundation (post-tension slab cracks and movement), roof (age and storm history matter for insurance), AC and ductwork, salt-air corrosion at exterior metal, and a windstorm certificate of compliance (the WPI-8) on properties built or altered in Tier 1 or Tier 2 wind zones. Without a current WPI-8, windstorm coverage gets ugly fast.

Septic and well are real on rural and acreage properties around Odem, Robstown, and outside Kingsville. Dodee has a Coastal Bend inspector roster and tells you which findings warrant a credit, which warrant a repair, and which are simply the cost of owning a home near salt water.

06 Step 06

Appraisal and survey

The lender orders the appraisal once you're past inspections. If it comes in low, the path forward is a price renegotiation, a buyer-side appraisal gap, a seller concession, or a clean exit. Dodee has sat at this table enough to know which one fits your situation.

The survey is the second piece. In Texas, an existing seller-provided survey with a T-47 affidavit is often acceptable to the title company. If it isn't, a new survey runs a few hundred dollars and confirms the lot lines, easements, and any encroachments before you close. On waterfront and acreage properties, this matters more than buyers expect.

07 Step 07

Final walkthrough and closing

The day before or morning of closing, Dodee walks the home with you one more time. Negotiated repairs done. Systems still working. Nothing pulled out of the house that was supposed to stay. Then to the title company. In Texas, closings happen at title, not at an attorney's office. You'll wire funds in advance, sign a stack, review the settlement statement, and walk out with keys. The appointment usually takes about an hour.

The work doesn't stop at handoff. Windstorm and homeowner insurance binders confirmed, hurricane prep tips for new coastal owners, vendor intros (HVAC for salt air, roofers, painters, irrigation), and a follow-up call after the move-in. Most of Dodee's business comes from past clients and the people they send. That's by design, and it starts after closing day.

Frequently asked questions

Honest answers about buying a house in Corpus Christi, Nueces County, and across the Coastal Bend.

It depends on the loan, not the city. Conventional loans in Texas start around 3 to 5 percent down for qualified buyers, FHA can go as low as 3.5 percent, and VA loans can go to zero down for eligible service members and veterans. Texas first time home buyer programs through TSAHC and TDHCA can layer on down-payment and closing-cost assistance for buyers who qualify. Dodee's lender intros give you exact numbers for your situation in the first conversation.

Timing the market matters less than buying a home that fits your life and your budget. Corpus Christi and the broader Coastal Bend are steady, owner-occupied markets, not boom-and-bust speculation, and waiting for a perfect moment usually costs more in lost equity than a slightly higher rate would have. If your income is stable, your savings cover three to six months of payments, and you plan to stay at least five years, the math usually works in Nueces County.

From first call to closing is typically 60 to 90 days. Pre-approval takes one to two weeks, the actual home search runs anywhere from a weekend to a few months depending on inventory and how specific your criteria are, and once you're under contract you're looking at 30 to 45 days to close. Cash buyers can move faster. First time buyers using TSAHC or TDHCA assistance sometimes need an extra two weeks for paperwork.

You can technically work with the listing agent, but it's almost never the right move. The listing agent has a fiduciary duty to the seller, which means their job is to get the highest price and the cleanest terms for the other side. Having your own agent costs the buyer nothing in most Texas transactions, and it means you have someone whose entire job is to advocate for you on price, terms, inspections, and repairs.

It happens, especially on well-priced homes in Southside Corpus Christi, Flour Bluff, Portland, and North Padre. The next move depends on why you lost. If it was price, Dodee will tell you whether it's worth circling back if the deal falls through. If it was terms, she'll tighten your next offer. Most buyers don't win the first house they bid on, and that's not a failure, it's part of staying disciplined. The right house is sometimes the third or fourth offer, not the first.

Yes, military relocation is a core part of Dodee's practice. VA loan workflow, COE coordination, BAH planning against real Coastal Bend carrying costs, virtual tours and FaceTime walkthroughs for buyers stuck on orders elsewhere, and neighborhood guidance based on commute to NAS-CC or NAS Kingsville, schools, and resale if you rotate out. Buying a house in Corpus Christi on PCS orders has a rhythm, and Dodee knows it.

Buyer agent commissions in Texas are negotiable and have changed under recent NAR settlement rules. In most transactions the seller still offers a buyer-side commission as part of the listing, which means the buyer pays nothing out of pocket beyond their down payment and closing costs. When that's not the case, Dodee walks you through the options upfront, in writing, before you sign anything. No surprises at the closing table.
