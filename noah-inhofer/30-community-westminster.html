Get Your Bearings in Westminster

Westminster started as a farming settlement called DeSpain Junction, was renamed Harris, and incorporated in 1911. The city says it spans 34 square miles in two counties. The Bowles House, built in 1881, the year the railroad came, is one of the few buildings left from the original downtown, and the city runs it as a museum.

The Westminster tour

Westminster From City Park to McKay Lake

Pick a stop and the map follows along: Noah’s video from the spot where he has filmed it, and the homes for sale around it.

Map data: U.S. Census Bureau. Choose a numbered pin or a stop above. The dotted loop is an order to see them in, not a road.

Stop 1

City Park

City Park is at 10455 Sheridan Boulevard. What the city lists there:

  • Access to the Big Dry Creek regional trail
  • A paved loop walking path and the Armed Forces Tribute Garden
  • A fitness center, a skateboard park and Christopher Fields

Stop 2

Big Dry Creek Trail

The trail is the city’s long green line across town. The city’s facts on it:

  • Nearly 12 miles within Westminster, from Standley Lake Regional Park to Interstate 25 and on into Thornton
  • A National Recreation Trail since 2003
  • Crosses under most streets through underpasses

Stop 3

Standley Lake Regional Park and Wildlife Refuge

Standley Lake is at 100th Avenue and Simms Street in unincorporated Jefferson County, just outside the city limits. Westminster is one of the owners of its water, which is why the rules are strict:

  • Westminster, Thornton, Northglenn and the Farmers Reservoir and Irrigation Company own the water
  • It is the main drinking water supply for the three cities, so no swimming or wading
  • Lake access runs May 1 to September 30 with a daily paddle pass or annual permit

Stop 4

McKay Lake Open Space

McKay Lake is the north-side stop, at 2000 West 144th Avenue. The city’s notes on it:

  • Built in 1905 for irrigation, and later part of a hunting club
  • Bought by Westminster in nine parcels from 1997 to 2012
  • A 1.8-mile trail around the lake and a pier for fishing or birding

Before you buy

What to Check on Any Westminster Address

Four things that change from one side of town to the other. Each takes a few minutes to look up and is worth doing before you write an offer.

Five Hidden Costs Before Moving to Northern Colorado

12:37 · Costs

  1. Which county is it in?

    Westminster spans two counties, Adams and Jefferson, with about half the city in each by area. The county sets the assessor and the tax bill, so look the address up with the Adams County Assessor or the Jefferson County Assessor, whichever holds the parcel.

  2. Which schools go with the address?

    Three districts serve the city: Jeffco Public Schools, Adams 12 Five Star Schools and Westminster Public Schools. The right one depends on the address, and each has a finder, including the Westminster Public Schools finder. Confirm the campus with the district before you write an offer.

  3. Is it in the floodplain?

    The city’s floodplain management page points buyers to FEMA’s National Flood Hazard Layer viewer and the Mile High Flood District’s information hub. A new Little Dry Creek flood hazard study is under way.

  4. Who provides the water and sewer?

    The City of Westminster bills water and sewer to its utility customers and takes water and sewer issues at 303-658-2500.

Quick answers

What Buyers Ask About Here

What county is Westminster in?

Two: Adams and Jefferson. About half the city is in each by area, so the county depends on the address, and the assessor you check does too.

Which school district serves Westminster?

Jeffco Public Schools, Adams 12 Five Star Schools and Westminster Public Schools all serve parts of the city. Use the district finder for the address and confirm with the district.

How do I check a Westminster home for flood risk?

Search the address in FEMA’s National Flood Hazard Layer viewer or the Mile High Flood District hub. The city says newer homes are not built in a floodplain, but older homes near a waterway may be in one.

Is Standley Lake inside Westminster?

No. The park is at 100th Avenue and Simms Street in unincorporated Jefferson County, although Westminster is one of the owners of the lake’s water. No swimming or wading is allowed because it supplies drinking water.
