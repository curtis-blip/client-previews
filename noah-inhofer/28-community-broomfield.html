Get Your Bearings in Broomfield

Broomfield is a city and a county at once. The city dates its founding to 1877 and its incorporation to 1961, and on Nov. 15, 2001 it became the City and County of Broomfield, so one government handles the services and the property records.

The Broomfield tour

Broomfield From the Farmhouse to Anthem Park

Pick a stop and the map follows along: Noah’s video from the spot where he has filmed it, and the homes for sale around it.

Map data: U.S. Census Bureau. Choose a numbered pin or a stop above. The dotted loop is an order to see them in, not a road.

Stop 1

Brunner Farmhouse

Start at 640 Main Street, on the northeast corner of Midway Boulevard and Main Street. The Brunner Farmhouse is a historic farmhouse with gardens, and the city keeps a designated open space around it that it calls the Field.

  • Farmhouse and gardens at 640 Main Street
  • On the northeast corner of Midway Boulevard and Main Street
  • Surrounded by an open space called the Field

Stop 2

Broomfield Depot Museum

The depot is the city’s railroad story. The city’s own history of it:

  • Built by the railroad in 1909, when about 160 people lived in Broomfield
  • Closed in 1967 and became a museum in 1982
  • Moved in 1976 from 120th and Wadsworth to Zang Spur Park
  • Free to visit, and open most Saturdays

Stop 3

Broomfield County Commons

Out on Sheridan Boulevard, at 13200, the Commons is where the city keeps its big recreation ground. What the city lists there:

  • The Paul Derda Recreation Center
  • A park and sports complex with 15 playing fields
  • The Big Loop Trail, a 2.2-mile loop past reservoirs and wetlands
  • Open space and the County Commons cemetery

Stop 4

Anthem Community Park

The north-side stop is Anthem Community Park, on the northeast corner of Sheridan Parkway and Lowell Boulevard. The city lists a large shelter, restrooms, a barbecue grill, soccer fields and a tennis court.

  • At 15663 Sheridan Parkway
  • Large shelter, restrooms and a barbecue grill
  • Soccer fields and a tennis court

Before you buy

What to Check on Any Broomfield Address

Four things that change from one side of town to the other. Each takes a few minutes to look up and is worth doing before you write an offer.

Five Hidden Costs Before Moving to Northern Colorado

12:37 · Costs

  1. Which county is it in?

    Broomfield is its own county, a consolidated city and county since Nov. 15, 2001, so the Broomfield Assessor holds the property record. Records from before 2001 may sit with the legacy county. Look an address up with the parcel, floodplain and tax inquiry.

  2. Which schools go with the address?

    Six districts overlap the city, so the district depends on the address: Adams 12 Five Star, Boulder Valley, Jeffco, St. Vrain Valley, Brighton and Fort Lupton. The city’s schools page lists them and links each district’s site. Confirm the campus with the district before you write an offer.

  3. Is it in the floodplain?

    The city’s floodplain search shows floodplains by street address or parcel ID. The city is also seeking a FEMA map revision along the City Park Channel and South Tributary, so check any address you are serious about.

  4. Who provides the water and sewer?

    The City and County of Broomfield does, through its Water Utilities and Services Department, which provides water, wastewater and stormwater services.

Quick answers

Broomfield Questions Buyers Ask

Is Broomfield a city or a county?

Both. It became the City and County of Broomfield on Nov. 15, 2001, so one government handles the services and the property records.

Which school district is Broomfield in?

Six districts overlap the city: Adams 12 Five Star, Boulder Valley, Jeffco, St. Vrain Valley, Brighton and Fort Lupton. The one for a home depends on its address, so use the district’s finder and confirm with the district.

How do I check a Broomfield address for flood risk?

Use the city’s floodplain search by street address or parcel ID. The city is also working with FEMA on a map revision along the City Park Channel and South Tributary.

What is there to see in Broomfield?

The Depot Museum, a 1909 railroad depot that is free to visit most Saturdays, and the Brunner Farmhouse at 640 Main Street. Broomfield County Commons has the Paul Derda Recreation Center and the 2.2-mile Big Loop Trail.
