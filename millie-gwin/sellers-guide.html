The Smart Seller's Guide to Selling Your Titusville Home

From pricing strategy to closing day, a clear roadmap for getting your home sold on the Space Coast.

Seller's Guide

10 Steps to a Successful Home Sale

Selling a home in Titusville and the greater Brevard County market requires more than sticking a sign in the yard. The right preparation, pricing, and marketing strategy can mean the difference between a property that lingers and one that attracts strong offers quickly. This guide breaks down each phase so you know exactly what to expect.

01

Evaluate Your Timing

Timing plays a larger role in your sale outcome than most sellers realize. On the Space Coast, spring and early summer consistently see the highest buyer activity. Families relocating for school enrollment, snowbirds looking to lock in their next winter home, and military families transferring to Patrick Space Force Base all tend to shop during these months.

That said, the best time to sell is when it aligns with your personal goals. If you need to relocate for work in October, waiting until March does not make sense. A well-priced, well-marketed home can sell in any season. The key is adjusting your expectations and strategy based on when you list.

02

Know Your Home's Value

Online estimates from Zillow, Redfin, and Realtor.com can vary by tens of thousands of dollars, and none of them account for the condition of your home, recent upgrades, or hyperlocal market shifts. A professional Comparative Market Analysis (CMA) looks at actual closed sales of similar properties in your area within the last few months.

A CMA also factors in active competition. If three similar homes in your neighborhood are already listed, that changes your pricing strategy. Get a realistic picture before you commit to a number. Request a free home valuation here and find out where your property stands in the current market.

03

Choose the Right Agent

Not all agents approach a listing the same way. Some will take beautiful photos and wait for the MLS to do the work. Others will build a full marketing campaign around your property, with professional staging guidance, targeted advertising, and proactive outreach to buyer's agents in the area.

When interviewing agents, ask specific questions: How many homes have you sold in Titusville this year? What is your average days-on-market? How do you handle pricing if the home does not get offers in the first two weeks? A local agent who knows Brevard County's buyer pool, inspection norms, and negotiation patterns will outperform a generalist every time.

04

Prepare Your Home

First impressions happen fast, and in Florida, they start with your curb appeal. Pressure wash the driveway and walkways, trim back any overgrown landscaping, and make sure the exterior paint or stucco is clean. Inside, declutter aggressively. Buyers need to see themselves in the space, not your family photos and collectibles.

Florida-specific preparation matters too. Make sure your air conditioning system is running efficiently (buyers will notice if a house feels warm during a showing). Check for any signs of moisture, mildew, or water staining, especially around windows and in bathrooms. These are red flags that Florida buyers watch for. Even small repairs, like replacing worn caulk or fixing a slow drain, signal that the home has been well maintained.

05

Price It Right

Overpricing is the most common mistake sellers make, and it is the most costly. A home priced too high sits on the market, accumulates days-on-market that make buyers suspicious, and eventually requires a price reduction that could have been avoided entirely.

In Titusville, the current median home price sits around $280,000, with average days on market between 76 and 85 days. Homes priced competitively from day one consistently sell faster and closer to asking price. Your agent should present a pricing strategy backed by real data from the Brevard County Property Appraiser and recent MLS activity, not gut feelings.

06

Market Your Property

Great marketing is what separates a listing that gets attention from one that gets scrolled past. Professional photography is non-negotiable. Buyers form their opinion of a home within seconds of seeing the first photo online, and smartphone pictures taken in poor lighting will cost you showings.

Beyond the MLS listing, your property should be promoted through targeted social media advertising, email campaigns to local buyer's agents, virtual tours for out-of-state buyers (common in the Space Coast market), and strategic placement on top real estate platforms. Millie builds a custom marketing plan for every listing, reaching buyers where they are actually searching, not just where every other agent posts.

07

Manage Showings

Once your home is listed, keeping it in showing condition is one of the hardest parts of the process. Develop a quick routine: wipe down counters, make the beds, open blinds for natural light, and keep floors clean. The easier it is to maintain, the less stressful the showing period becomes.

Flexibility matters. Buyers often want to see homes on short notice, and the seller who says "yes" to a same-day showing has an advantage over the one who delays. After each showing, your agent should follow up with the buyer's agent for feedback. If multiple visitors mention the same concern, that is actionable information you can use to adjust your approach.

08

Review Offers and Negotiate

When offers come in, resist the temptation to focus only on the price. The strongest offer is the one most likely to close smoothly. Evaluate each offer's financing type (cash and conventional are typically stronger than FHA), contingencies, earnest money deposit amount, and proposed closing timeline.

If an offer comes in below your asking price, your agent will help you craft a counter-offer that keeps the negotiation alive without giving away your leverage. In a multiple-offer scenario, you may have the option to ask all buyers for their highest and best. Your agent's negotiation experience is critical here, and this is where having someone who knows the Brevard County market deeply pays dividends.

09

Navigate Inspections and Appraisals

After going under contract, the buyer will schedule inspections. In Florida, this typically includes a general home inspection, a wind mitigation report, a 4-point inspection, and a termite/WDO inspection. The results may generate a repair request. Your agent will help you evaluate which requests are reasonable, which are negotiable, and which you can push back on.

The appraisal is equally important. If the buyer is financing the purchase, their lender will send an appraiser to confirm the home's value supports the loan amount. If the appraisal comes in below the contract price, you have options: reduce the price, ask the buyer to cover the gap, meet in the middle, or cancel the contract. Having a well-documented CMA from the beginning strengthens your position in any appraisal dispute.

10

Close the Sale

Closing as a seller in Florida takes place at a title company. You will sign the deed, authorize the transfer, and settle any prorated property taxes, HOA dues, or outstanding utility balances. The title company handles the disbursement of funds, and your net proceeds are typically wired to your bank within one to two business days.

If you have owned and lived in the home for at least two of the last five years, you may qualify for the IRS home sale capital gains exclusion, which allows you to exclude up to $250,000 in profit ($500,000 for married couples filing jointly) from capital gains tax. Consult with a tax professional to understand how this applies to your situation. Once closing is complete and the deed is recorded with Brevard County, the sale is official.
