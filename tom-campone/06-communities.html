A day in the life

Mornings start with a run on the Lakefront Trail and coffee before the walk to the Loop. Afternoons are spent at the Museum Campus or wandering Grant Park, and evenings mean dinner on Wabash or a quick train to anywhere in the city. This is the rare Chicago neighborhood where you can live without a car and never feel like you are missing out.

What you'll find

The South Loop is defined by its condos and high-rise towers, from converted Printer's Row lofts to full-amenity glass buildings along the lakefront, alongside a growing stock of townhomes and a handful of single-family homes. It draws first-time buyers, downsizers, and investors in equal measure.

A day in the life

A morning pastry on 18th Street, an afternoon at a gallery opening, and an evening of tacos and live music. Pilsen runs on its art and its history, where Mexican-American heritage shows up in the murals on nearly every block, the food, and a calendar of festivals that pull the whole city south. It is one of Chicago's most distinct and creative corners.

What you'll find

Pilsen's housing is its character: greystones, brick two-flats and three-flats, vintage worker cottages, and converted loft buildings, many with original detail intact. It appeals to buyers who want architecture and culture over polish, and to investors drawn to its multi-unit stock.

A day in the life

A walk through Palmisano Park's quarry trails, a bratwurst before a White Sox game, and dinner at a corner spot where the owner knows your name. Bridgeport is one of Chicago's oldest neighborhoods, proudly residential, tight-knit, and increasingly home to artists and young families drawn by its character and its prices.

What you'll find

Bridgeport is a single-family and two-flat neighborhood at heart, with brick bungalows, historic frame cottages, and classic two-flats lining quiet, tree-shaded blocks. It remains one of the better values close to downtown, which makes it a favorite for both owner-occupants and investors.

Finding your fit

The right neighborhood, not just the right home

A great home in the wrong neighborhood rarely stays a great home for long. Before Tom shows you a single listing, he wants to understand how you actually live: your commute, whether you want a car, the noise and pace you can handle, and what you want your weekends to look like.

That conversation is where his 20 years on the near south side pay off. Tom can tell you which South Loop buildings hold their value, where Pilsen's character is changing block by block, and why a Bridgeport two-flat might stretch your budget further than you expected. The goal is a place that still fits you in five years, not just on closing day.
