Buyer's Guide

Buying a home in North County San Diego, the right way.

Eight steps from first call to keys in hand.

Whether you're a first time home buyer in San Diego, relocating to the coast, or stepping up into Del Mar or La Jolla, the path is the same: clarity at every step.

Jerre brings a courtroom-trained eye for documents and contracts, two decades of North County market memory across Carlsbad, Encinitas, Solana Beach, Del Mar, La Jolla, and the inland valleys, and a no-pressure pace that protects you from the rushed decisions that hurt buyers most. Every tradeoff gets named out loud, every clause gets read carefully, and you always know where you stand before the next move.

01 Step 01

Define what daily life actually looks like

Bedrooms and bathrooms are the easy part. Before we open a single listing, we talk through what the next ten years of mornings and evenings need to feel like: school commute, beach proximity, walkability to coffee or trails, lot size for pets or projects, view orientation, and how you actually use a kitchen on a Tuesday night.

The gap between what people search for and what they end up loving is huge in North County. A buyer who thinks they want Carlsbad Village often lands in Aviara. Someone set on Del Mar discovers Solana Beach has the same morning fog and a friendlier price band. We name those tradeoffs early so the search stays honest.

02 Step 02

Understand your budget like an investor

Pre-qualification is a quick check. Pre-approval is real underwriting. The difference matters when you're competing for a coastal home, because listing agents read pre-approval letters like reviewers, not formalities. We get you the version that actually wins offers.

Then we go past the lender's number and look at what fits your life: monthly carry including HOA, Mello-Roos in newer master-planned communities, insurance in fire-zone overlays, and the price ranges by city that shape what's realistic. Carlsbad, Encinitas, Carmel Valley, and Escondido each move at their own tempo, and your budget reads differently in each one.

03 Step 03

Get pre-approved with a lender who knows San Diego

Local lenders close faster on coastal properties. They've seen the HOAs, they understand Mello-Roos disclosures, they know how Pacific Beach condo associations review buyer packages. A national call-center lender often misses these details and the deal slows or breaks at appraisal.

Jerre keeps a short list of lenders with strong North County track records. You'll get two or three intros, talk to humans, compare real rates and terms, and pick the one whose process feels right. No pressure, no kickback relationships, just lenders who close on time.

04 Step 04

Search with intention, not just the MLS feed

Auto-feeds are useful for the obvious matches. The better homes usually need curation: pocket listings from agents Jerre has worked with for years, off-market intros from past clients getting ready to sell, and neighborhood-specific intel that doesn't show up in Zillow's data layer.

Carlsbad has six distinct submarkets. Encinitas changes character every few blocks. Del Mar splits north and south of the racetrack with different price logic. La Jolla's view premiums depend on which side of Mt. Soledad you're on. We curate listings against your actual criteria, not against a 2-mile radius.

05 Step 05

Tour with a critical eye

Staging is designed to sell. Touring is designed to buy. We walk through homes looking past the styled vignettes and asking the questions that matter: where does water go in a January rain, how does the view change at 4pm in summer, what's the actual noise floor from the freeway or the train.

For any home that's seriously in the running, plan a second walkthrough at a different time of day. Morning fog burns off differently in Encinitas than in Carmel Valley. Traffic patterns reshape commutes after 3pm. Jerre will flag the things to recheck and meet you back at the house before you write.

06 Step 06

Make a strategic offer in a competitive market

Winning isn't always about the highest number. In multiple-offer scenarios across Carlsbad and Encinitas, we use escalation clauses, cleaner contingency timelines, flexible close dates, and rent-back terms that let sellers move at their own pace. Sometimes a thoughtful offer at list beats a higher offer with messy terms.

Jerre also reads the listing agent. How they communicate, how they price, how they describe past offers, all of it tells you what the seller actually cares about. We build the offer around that, not around a generic template, and we never push you to overpay just to win.

07 Step 07

Inspections, appraisal, and renegotiation

Every coastal home turns up something on inspection. Sea air ages roofs, decks, and HVAC faster than inland. Older homes in Leucadia or La Jolla hide quirks behind fresh paint. Our job is to separate normal wear from real problems, then decide what's worth pushing on and what's part of buying near the ocean.

Jerre's courtroom training pays off here. She reads the inspection report, the disclosures, and the contract clauses with the same precision she brought to legal documents, and she knows when to ask for credit, repair, or price reduction. If the appraisal comes in low, we have a plan ready before the call ends.

08 Step 08

Closing and the days after

Closing day is paperwork, wire confirmations, and a key handoff. We walk you through what to expect at the title company, what each signature means, and how funds move so nothing surprises you. Jerre is there in person when she can be, on the phone when she can't.

The relationship doesn't end at the closing table. Past clients call Jerre years later for contractor referrals, rental conversion advice, neighbor disputes, and the next purchase. Many of her current clients are repeat buyers from the early 2000s. That continuity is what twenty years in one market actually looks like.

Frequently Asked Questions

Honest, North-County-specific answers to the questions Jerre hears every week.

Conventional loans typically start at 5 percent down, FHA at 3.5 percent, and VA at zero down for qualified buyers. Most North County buyers we work with put 10 to 20 percent down on coastal properties to stay competitive in multi-offer situations. On a median-priced Encinitas home around the mid-$1Ms, that range matters a lot, so we walk through your options against your actual savings before you fall for a listing.

The honest answer is that timing depends on your situation, not the headlines. North County coastal inventory has stayed tight for years, so waiting for a big drop has historically punished buyers more than it has rewarded them. If your job, savings, and life plans are stable, the better question is whether the right home for the next ten years is on the market right now. Jerre will tell you straight if she thinks you should wait.

From the first conversation to keys in hand, most buyers take two to four months. Active touring and offer-writing usually runs four to eight weeks, and escrow itself runs 21 to 35 days depending on financing. Cash buyers can close in two weeks. Buyers relocating from out of state often take longer because we plan the trip windows carefully so you're not flying back and forth chasing showings.

You can work with the listing agent directly, but understand what you're giving up. The listing agent represents the seller and their job is to get the highest price and best terms for that seller. A dedicated buyer's agent represents you, negotiates on your side, and reads contracts with your interests in mind. With recent commission changes, buyers and agents now sign a written agreement upfront that spells out exactly what services and compensation look like.

Losing an offer is part of buying in coastal North County, especially in Carlsbad, Encinitas, and Cardiff. We debrief after every loss: what the winning offer looked like, what we'd do differently, and whether the loss was actually a gift in disguise. Jerre also tracks back-up positions, because deals fall through more often than buyers realize, and the second-place offer can quietly become the winner two weeks later.

Buyer commission is now negotiated upfront in a written agreement before we tour homes together, per the 2024 NAR settlement rules. Jerre walks every buyer through the agreement plainly: what services are included, what the rate is, and how seller-paid concessions can offset the cost in many transactions. There are no surprise fees and you'll see the math clearly before you sign anything.
