Fallbrook at a glance

A farm town in the hills

Fallbrook sits in the hills of northern San Diego County, west of Interstate 15 and about 14 miles by road from Old Town Temecula. It is not a city: the County of San Diego governs it, and the Census Bureau counts it as a census-designated place.

The village around Main Avenue is the old town center. Beyond it, the County's plan for Fallbrook keeps lots large and encourages houses that share their land with groves. Camp Pendleton and the Naval Weapons Station lie just west of downtown, and the Santa Margarita River runs through the hills to the north on its way from Temecula to the ocean.

01

Named for a Pennsylvania farm

The Reche brothers left Fall Brook, Pennsylvania, and started a farm here in 1869. They planted fruit trees and grapes, later kept bees, and called the ranch Fall Brook. A post office opened in 1878, and in 1890 a railroad line linked Fallbrook to the coast.

The first farms dry-farmed wheat and olives. Once water systems reached the area, avocados spread across the hills, and the County's plan for Fallbrook records that the town became known as the "Avocado Capital" of the world. The original stretch of downtown is designated Historic Fallbrook, and the Fallbrook Library on South Mission Road opened in 1913 as the first branch of the San Diego County library system.

02

Fallbrook has no city hall

Fallbrook is unincorporated San Diego County. Land use follows the County's General Plan and its Fallbrook Community Plan, which the Fallbrook Community Planning Group prepared with County staff, and building permits come from the County.

The plan draws a village boundary around the town center and places apartments and townhouses inside it. Outside it, lots start at half an acre in the semi-rural areas and an acre in the rural lands, and the plan encourages country estates that pair a house with light agriculture, especially groves. County Public Works maintains the public roads; many others are private, some with road maintenance agreements and some without.

03

Live Oak Park and the river

Live Oak County Park on Reche Road is a 27-acre grove of old oaks. In 1919 the Chamber of Commerce of Northern San Diego County voted to make Reche Grove a park, and it was dedicated as Live Oak Park on July 17, 1920. The County runs it today.

North of town, the County's Santa Margarita County Preserve on De Luz Road protects 221 acres of river corridor, with two miles of trails for hikers, riders and mountain bikers and a staging area for horses. The Fallbrook Land Conservancy owns other preserves around the community, and part of that land is open to the public.

04

Water from across the line

Two districts serve Fallbrook. Fallbrook Public Utility District left the San Diego County Water Authority on January 1, 2024, after approval by the Local Agency Formation Commission and a vote of the district's voters. It now buys imported water from Eastern Municipal Water District in Riverside County, treated at the Lake Skinner plant near Temecula. Rainbow Municipal Water District, which serves parts of Fallbrook, is a member of Eastern Municipal too. Which one serves a house depends on the address.

FPUD also draws local water from the Santa Margarita River, a project built to supply more than 30 percent of its demand. The river begins in Temecula, at the confluence of Murrieta and Temecula creeks, and runs through Temecula Gorge in San Diego State's Santa Margarita Ecological Reserve, which sits on the county line between Temecula and Fallbrook. From there it crosses Camp Pendleton to the ocean.

What Jim would check in Fallbrook

  • County rules Zoning and permits come from the County of San Diego under the Fallbrook Community Plan. Ask how the plan treats a parcel before you count on dividing it or building more on it.
  • Private roads Many Fallbrook roads are private. Ask whether a road maintenance agreement covers the road to the house, and who pays for repairs.
  • Water and septic FPUD or Rainbow, by address. Ask whether the house is on sewer or a septic system: the County's plan limits sewer for new subdivisions.
  • Flood and fire maps Look the address up on FEMA's flood maps and on CAL FIRE's fire hazard maps, which can affect insurance and building rules.

Questions

Fallbrook FAQ

Is Fallbrook a city?

No. Fallbrook is unincorporated San Diego County, and the Census Bureau counts it as a census-designated place. The County's General Plan and its Fallbrook Community Plan govern land use, and County Planning and Development Services handles building permits.

Which school districts serve Fallbrook?

Fallbrook Union Elementary School District runs the elementary schools and Potter Junior High, and Fallbrook Union High School District runs the high schools. The elementary district's online locator confirms the schools for an address.

Where does Fallbrook's water come from?

Fallbrook Public Utility District serves the Fallbrook area, and Rainbow Municipal Water District serves parts of it. Both buy imported water through Eastern Municipal Water District in Riverside County, and FPUD also draws local water from the Santa Margarita River.

How far is Fallbrook from Temecula and San Diego?

By road from Main Avenue, Old Town Temecula is about 14 miles by East Mission Road and Interstate 15. Downtown San Diego is about 55 miles, and the pier at Oceanside about 20.
