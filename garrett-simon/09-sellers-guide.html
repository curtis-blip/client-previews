Selling a Home in Telluride From a Distance

Eight steps from the first price conversation to the closing, for owners who spend most of the year somewhere else.

For owners who live elsewhere

Selling a home in Telluride while you are away

If Telluride is your second home, you may spend only a few days here during the whole sale. The rest happens by phone, email and electronic signature, with Garrett and Jessica at the property for you.

The steps below run in the order a sale does, from the first price conversation to the wire at closing. Each one says what Garrett and Jessica handle on the ground and what you decide from home.

Start with an instant estimate

How Garrett markets a listing

What moves this market

01

Step 1 of 8

Start with the price conversation

Get a quick read from the instant estimate, then ask Garrett for a listing price. He walks the property, with you or with whoever looks after it, and builds a full market analysis from the closest recent sales and what is for sale now. In a market this small, the few sales that truly match yours matter more than any average.

02

Step 2 of 8

Choose when to list

Telluride sells in every season, and each property has a best one. A ski-in/ski-out condo shows well under snow; a mesa home with a view, in summer or fall. If the property is in a rental program, look at the booking calendar before you pick a date: buyers will want to see it, and renters will be in it.

03

Step 3 of 8

Gather what buyers will ask for

Buyers ask for documents early, and the Colorado contract sets dates for them. For a condo: the HOA declaration, rules, budget, minutes and insurance. For any rental: the booking history, the management statements and your short-term rental license. For a mesa property: the well permit, the septic records and any road agreement. Jessica keeps the list and chases what is missing.

04

Step 4 of 8

Prepare the property from a distance

Garrett tells you what is worth fixing before the photographs and what to leave alone. Jessica lines up the cleaners, painters and trades and meets them at the property, so you are not flying in for a repair. Decide early what furniture and equipment stays with the sale; it changes how the property is shown and priced.

05

Step 5 of 8

Plan the photographs for the right light

For most buyers the photographs are the first visit, and sometimes the only one before they fly in. Schedule them for the season that shows the property best, on a clear day, with the property clean, the snow cleared and personal things put away. Video and a floor plan help a buyer who is deciding from two time zones away.

06

Step 6 of 8

Put it in front of buyers here and elsewhere

The listing goes into the local MLS, where brokers across the valley see it, and through Telluride Properties to the Forbes Global Properties network. Garrett puts the property's real strengths in front: for one Mountain Village condo with no parking, that was its rental history.

How Garrett markets a listing

07

Step 7 of 8

Show it while you are away

You do not need to be here for showings. Garrett or Jessica opens the property, works around renters with your management company, and tells you what buyers said. Leave clear notes on the heat, the lights and the snow, and lock away anything valuable.

08

Step 8 of 8

Offers, inspection and closing from wherever you are

Garrett reviews every offer with you, the terms as well as the price, and negotiates the inspection requests that follow. You sign along the way electronically; ask the title company early how you will sign the closing documents from home. Confirm any wire instructions for your proceeds by phone, never from an email.

Questions

What sellers ask

Do I need to be in Telluride to sell?

No. Garrett and Jessica handle the walkthrough, the trades, the showings and the inspection on the ground. You review and sign documents electronically, and you can ask the title company about signing the closing documents where you live.

What documents should I gather for a condo?

The HOA declaration, bylaws, rules, budget, recent minutes and insurance certificate, plus the rental history if it has been rented and your short-term rental license. The Colorado contract sets a date for the HOA documents, so have them ready before you list. The state's HOA Information and Resource Center has more on association records.

Can I keep renting it while it is listed?

Yes, if showings and inspections can be fit around the bookings. Settle it with Garrett and your management company before you list, and tell buyers which bookings after closing come with the sale.

How do you price a property with few comparable sales?

Garrett starts at the property: the view, the light, the access, the condition, the HOA and the rental history. He compares it with the closest recent sales and what is for sale now, and adjusts for the differences. The instant estimate is a starting point, not a listing price.

What do I have to disclose?

Colorado has a state-approved seller's property disclosure, with versions for homes and for land. Garrett goes through it with you; for a legal question, ask a real estate attorney. If the property has a septic system, keep the latest report from a certified septic inspection: the county suggests owners keep one for a sale.

What happens to the furniture and the ski gear?

Whatever you and the buyer agree. Decide early what stays, list it in the contract, and price the property with that in mind.
