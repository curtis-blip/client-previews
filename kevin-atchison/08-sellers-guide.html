Selling a house
in this corridor.

I price against what actually closed nearby, not against what the neighbours are asking. Those are different numbers, and only one of them sells a house.

01 Before you list

The first price is the only one that gets a full audience

A house listed correctly gets its best offers in the first two weeks, because that is when every buyer with a saved search sees it for the first time. A house listed high gets those same buyers, once, at the wrong number, and they do not come back when it is reduced.

In a market this thin that matters more than it does in a city. There may only be nine buyers for your house in a given month. Reaching all nine on day one is the entire strategy, and everything below serves it. If you want a number before we talk, start with an instant estimate and I will build the full analysis from there.

02 How selling works

Eight steps, in the order they happen

Step 01

Walk the house before we price it

An instant estimate does not know your roof, your water rights or what the house two streets over really closed at. I walk it, take my own notes, and then build the number from comparables I can defend line by line.

Step 02

Price against what closed, not what is asking

List prices tell you what sellers hope for. Closed sales tell you what buyers paid. In a thin market one odd sale skews the whole set, so I will show you which comparables I am using and which I am throwing out, and why.

Step 03

Fix the cheap things that cost you offers

I will give you a short list and a contractor list to go with it. It is usually paint, a drain, a fence panel and a deep clean. I am not going to tell you to remodel a kitchen to sell a house in Silt.

Step 04

Get the paperwork ready before it is asked for

Well permit, septic records, the shared well agreement if there is one, and any HOA or metro district documents. Having these ready on day one keeps a buyer's inspection period from becoming a renegotiation.

Step 05

Photograph it properly and launch on a Thursday

Full photography before anything goes live, never phone pictures. It launches Thursday so it is fresh for the weekend, hits every saved search on the Aspen‑Glenwood MLS at once, and syndicates out to the portals from there.

Step 06

Show it and tell you the truth about feedback

You will hear what people actually said, including the parts you will not enjoy. Three showings and no offers means something specific, and it is nearly always the price or one fixable thing.

Step 07

Negotiate the whole offer, not just the number

Financing type, inspection terms, closing date and what stays with the house. A slightly lower offer with clean terms often nets you more than a higher one that falls apart at the appraisal.

Step 08

Close, and know the figures in advance

I send you the closing figures the day they arrive rather than the morning of, so if something is wrong there is time to fix it. Then keys, and you are done.

I will tell you if now is the wrong time to sell.

It sometimes is. You are better off hearing that from me than finding out across ninety days on the market.

04 Questions sellers ask

The ones worth answering before we meet

What is my house worth?

Start with the instant estimate, which gives you a range in about a minute. Then I will walk the house and build a full market analysis, because an algorithm cannot see a new roof, a shared well or the fact that the comparable down the street had a failing leach field.

How long will it take to sell?

It depends far more on the price than on the market. Priced correctly, most houses on this corridor go under contract inside a month. Priced ten percent high, the same house can sit a season and then sell for less than it would have on day one.

What does it cost to sell?

My commission is agreed in writing before we list and it is negotiable. On top of that expect title and closing fees, any agreed concessions, and prorated taxes. I will give you a written net sheet before you sign anything, so you know what lands in your account.

Do I have to pay the buyer's agent?

Not automatically, not since the rules changed. You can choose to offer compensation and many sellers still do, because it widens the buyer pool. It is a strategic decision with a real effect on how many people can afford to write on your house, and we will make it deliberately rather than by default.

Should I sell before I buy?

If you are staying in the valley, usually we run both together rather than in sequence. The timing is the whole problem and it is solvable, but only if it is planned from the start. The buying process covers the other half.

Will you tell me not to sell?

Yes, if that is the answer. If your number is not achievable right now, or you would be better waiting a season, I would rather say so and keep the relationship than take a listing I cannot deliver on.
