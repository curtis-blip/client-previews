Selling a home in Las Vegas

A real valuation, an honest list of what is worth fixing, and five steps that start with the number. In English, Nepali, Hindi and Urdu.

How selling goes

Five steps, and the one that matters most is the first

Almost everything that goes wrong with a sale in this valley traces back to the asking price. Price it correctly and the rest of the process is administration. Price it optimistically and you spend two months discovering that, then reduce anyway, from a weaker position.

Before anything else

Start with a real valuation

Not an automated estimate pulled from a national average. A figure for your address, based on what has closed nearby, what is sitting unsold, and the condition of your property as it stands today.

It costs nothing and it does not commit you to listing.

What it is actually worth

I price off what has closed near you in the last few months and what is sitting unsold, not off an automated estimate. Then I tell you the number I think it will take, which is sometimes not the number you were hoping for.

02

Getting it ready

A short, honest list of what is worth doing and what is not. In this market paint, light and the front of the house pay back. Most of the rest does not, and I will say so rather than sell you work.

03

Photography and the listing

Professional photography, then the listing built properly: the MLS, the syndication, and the description written for the buyer rather than for the algorithm.

04

Offers and inspection

Offers, counters, and the inspection period. This is where deals fall apart, usually over something on a roof or in a report that nobody prepared for. I would rather find it early than argue about it in week four.

05

Closing

Appraisal, final walkthrough, signing, funding and recording. If you are selling a rental, I can handle the tenant side of the handover too, because that is the other half of what I do.

The first two weeks decide the price.

A listing gets its largest audience the week it appears. Everything after that is a smaller room. That is the whole reason I would rather argue with you about the number before it goes live than after.

Questions sellers ask

Before you list

What is my house actually worth?

The honest answer is a range, not a figure, and it depends on condition and timing as much as on square footage. I will give you the range and the reasoning behind it, based on what has closed near you recently. Automated estimates pull from national averages and routinely miss by a wide margin in this valley.

What should I fix before listing?

Usually less than people expect. Paint, lighting, landscaping at the front, and anything that will show up in an inspection anyway. Kitchens and bathrooms rarely return what they cost if you are renovating purely to sell. I will walk it with you and give you a short list rather than a long one.

How long will it take?

That depends on price, condition and the month, so anyone giving you a number before seeing the property is guessing. What I can tell you at the first visit is roughly where your home sits against what is currently on the market, and what would have to be true for it to move quickly.

Can I sell with a tenant still in it?

Yes, and it is common with investment property. Whether you should depends on the lease, the rent against market, and whether the likely buyer is an investor or an owner-occupier. Because I manage rentals as well, I can read the lease and tell you which of those you are actually selling into.

What do you charge?

Commission is agreed in writing before the property is listed, and it is negotiable. Ask any agent for the full picture rather than a single percentage: what is included, what is billed separately, and what happens if the property does not sell.

Do you handle the buying side too if I am moving within the valley?

Yes, and most local moves are both at once. It is easier to time a sale and a purchase when one person is holding both calendars. The buying process is set out on the Buyer's Guide.
