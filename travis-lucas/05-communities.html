Apache Junction

Under the Superstitions, where the East Valley ends

Apache Junction sits where the valley floor runs out and the Superstitions start. It is four markets wearing one name: the older in-town grid, the newer subdivisions south along the highway, the acreage north and east toward the mountains, and the 55-plus resort communities that fill up every winter.

  • Superstition Mountain views from most of the east side
  • Large 55-plus resort communities, Road Haven among them
  • Acreage and horse property north and east of town
  • Pinal County taxes, and utility districts that vary street by street

Mesa

The valley floor, and the deepest inventory on this side

Mesa is the biggest city in the East Valley and it has the inventory to match. Whatever you are looking for, some version of it exists here: 1970s ranches near downtown, golf communities on the east side, new build out toward the county line.

  • Widest price range of any market I work
  • Established golf and lake communities on the east side
  • Light rail and freeway access into Phoenix
  • Mature landscaping, which the newer suburbs will not have for years
Homes for sale in Mesa

San Tan Valley

Newer streets, and more land for the money

San Tan Valley is where buyers go when the lot matters. It is newer than most of the East Valley, still filling in, and the same money buys noticeably more house and more yard than it does thirty minutes west.

  • More square footage per dollar than Mesa or Gilbert
  • Newer construction, most of it built after 2000
  • Unincorporated, so check the district before you write
  • San Tan Mountain Regional Park on the doorstep
Homes for sale in San Tan Valley

Queen Creek

Farm town turned master-planned, and still growing

Queen Creek went from farmland to one of the fastest-growing towns in the state, and it kept more of its character than most places that grow that fast. Olive mill, working farms, and master-planned neighborhoods sitting next to each other.

  • Among the fastest-growing towns in Arizona
  • Master-planned communities with real amenities
  • Agritourism core: the olive mill, the farms, the market
  • New schools, built for the growth rather than retrofitted
Homes for sale in Queen Creek

Gold Canyon

At the base of the Superstitions, quieter than the rest

Gold Canyon sits right against the Superstitions, east of Apache Junction, and it is the quietest market I work. Golf, desert, and a lot of people who came for the view and stayed.

  • Directly beneath the Superstition Mountains
  • Two established golf communities
  • Unincorporated Pinal County
  • Strong seasonal and 55-plus ownership
Homes for sale in Gold Canyon

Gilbert

Family-oriented, and priced accordingly

Gilbert is the most polished of the East Valley suburbs, with the schools and the downtown to match, and prices that reflect both. Inventory moves quickly here.

  • Highly rated school district, verify boundaries with the district
  • Heritage District downtown, walkable and busy
  • Largely built out, so most inventory is resale
  • Shortest commute to the Chandler employment corridor
Homes for sale in Gilbert

Chandler

Where the jobs are, and the housing that follows

Chandler is the East Valley's employment centre, with the semiconductor corridor and everything that grew around it. Housing runs from 1980s ranches to new infill.

  • Major employment corridor, Intel among others
  • Wide range of stock, 1980s through new infill
  • Downtown Chandler restaurant and event scene
  • Direct access to the 101 and 202 loops
Homes for sale in Chandler

Florence

Historic town, low prices, and room to grow

Florence is the value end of the corridor: a genuine historic town centre, prices well below the rest of the East Valley, and a lot of land between here and everything else.

  • Lowest entry prices of the eight markets
  • National Register historic district downtown
  • Long commute to Phoenix employment, plan for it
  • Large parcels and acreage available
