Living in Glenwood Springs, CO

The north end of the corridor, where the Roaring Fork meets the Colorado. The most expensive town I work in and the one most people want first, which is exactly why the towns below it are worth understanding.

Meet Kevin

00 Orientation

Glenwood Springs is supply-constrained, and that is structural

Glenwood sits where two rivers meet inside a narrow valley, which is what makes it beautiful and also what makes it expensive. There is very little flat ground left and no realistic way to make more, so the price is not a sentiment that will correct. It is a constraint. What you get for it is a genuinely walkable town with a hospital, a train station, the hot springs and a direct run up the Roaring Fork toward Aspen. Most buyers I work with start here and end up deciding between paying for it or driving to it, and both are reasonable answers.

01 Before you offer

What I would check for you

Glenwood is the most expensive town I work in, so the checks matter more here in absolute dollars than anywhere else on the corridor.

What you are paying for supply

The valley is narrow and there is very little flat ground left. That constraint is structural and it is not going away, which is worth understanding before you decide the price is irrational.

Access in winter

A house up Four Mile or on Red Mountain is a different daily proposition in February. Drive it in bad weather, not in September.

Who maintains the road

City street, county road or private easement. On the canyon roads this is a real annual cost and it is worth knowing the number.

Wildfire insurance

This is where I see it bite hardest on the corridor. Price the insurance while you are under contract, because on some properties it changes what you can afford.

Condo and townhome documents

A large share of what is affordable in town is attached. Reserves and assessment history tell you more than the monthly dues do.

Short-term rental rules

If part of your plan is renting it out, confirm what is permitted for that specific address before you write. Rules differ inside and outside the city.

Getting around

I-70 through Glenwood Canyon, Highway 82 up the Roaring Fork toward Aspen, and an Amtrak stop in town. RFTA runs buses up valley.

The reason most people have heard of the town. The pool has been running since the 1880s and it is still the anchor of the place.
