Selling a House in Arizona

Eight steps from the first price conversation to your proceeds, and what the Arizona contract asks of a seller along the way.

How it works

Selling a House in Arizona, Step by Step

Selling a house in Arizona starts with a price grounded in recent sales and ends when the deed is recorded and your proceeds arrive. Between the two come the preparation, the listing, showings, offers and escrow, and each step is below.

Rebecca Durfey has been licensed in Arizona since 2002. She studies the market daily, and you have personal and direct communication with her from the first visit to closing.

Step 1 of 8

Choose Your Agent and Sign the Listing Agreement

Your listing agreement sets the price you start at, how long the listing runs, what Rebecca does for you and how she is paid. Commission is negotiable and set in the listing agreement, and Rebecca goes through every line with you before you sign.

From then on you deal with Rebecca directly, and her licensed assistant, Sherri Dastrup, works alongside her.

02

Step 2 of 8

Price It on Recent Sales

Rebecca prepares a market analysis of homes like yours that sold nearby, alongside what is for sale now and what failed to sell. The price shapes the first impression online and the showings that follow.

Before you list, ask for an estimate of your net proceeds after the loan payoff and closing costs, so every offer can be read against it.

03

Step 3 of 8

Get the House and Yard Ready

Fix what a buyer's inspector would find, and touch up worn paint. Outside, rake and refresh the gravel, trim desert plants back from walls and walkways, check that the drip irrigation runs and keep the pool water clear. Inside, clear the counters and closets so rooms read larger.

Ask Rebecca which repairs are worth making before you list and which are better left to the buyer's inspection.

04

Step 4 of 8

Stage the Rooms That Matter Most

Staging the whole house is rarely needed. Focus on the living room, the kitchen and the primary bedroom. Empty rooms can look smaller than they are, so a few well-sized pieces of furniture help buyers judge the space.

Moving out before it sells? Leaving a few pieces behind, or renting them, keeps the rooms furnished.

Rebecca doesn't just get your listing and stop interacting with you, she provides weekly updates about your home and the market and she is very responsive if you have questions for her!

05

Step 5 of 8

List It on the MLS

Rebecca lists your home on ARMLS, the Arizona Regional Multiple Listing Service, which puts it in front of buyers' agents across the Valley and on the websites that draw their listings from the MLS.

Before it goes live, agree on the photos, the description and the showing instructions.

06

Step 6 of 8

Showings

Buyers' agents book showings through the listing. Leave during showings so buyers can talk freely, take pets with you if you can, and leave the lights on and the blinds open.

Send any questions about the house to Rebecca, so every buyer hears the same answers.

07

Step 7 of 8

Negotiate and Accept an Offer

Rebecca reviews each offer with you: the price, the earnest money, the buyer's loan, the closing date and any requests. You can accept, counter or decline.

With more than one offer, she lays out your options before you choose.

08

Step 8 of 8

Escrow, Closing and Your Proceeds

Once you are under contract, you deliver the Seller's Property Disclosure Statement (SPDS) and answer the buyer's requests in the Buyer's Inspection Notice and Seller's Response. Your contract sets each deadline, and Rebecca tracks them with you.

You sign the closing documents with the escrow officer. After the deed is recorded, escrow pays off your loan and sends your proceeds by check or wire. Confirm wiring instructions by phone, never by email alone.

Questions

Seller Questions, Answered

How is a listing agent paid in Arizona?

Commission is negotiable and set in your listing agreement. No board, association or MLS sets it.

When do I get my proceeds?

After the deed is recorded with the county and escrow confirms it. You can ask for a check or a wire to your bank account; confirm wiring instructions by phone with the escrow officer, never by email alone.

How are property taxes handled at closing?

Arizona property taxes are billed in two halves, due October 1 and March 1 (A.R.S. 42-18052). The escrow officer prorates them at closing, so you pay your share up to the closing date.

Do I have to fill out a disclosure statement?

Most sellers complete the Seller's Property Disclosure Statement (SPDS). Even without it, a seller's duty to disclose what they know remains, as the Arizona Buyer Advisory explains. For legal questions about a disclosure, ask an Arizona attorney.

When should I cancel my homeowners insurance?

Keep your policy in force until the sale closes and the house is no longer yours, then cancel it. Ask your insurer about any refund of prepaid premium.

Why does escrow ask me for tax information?

The closing agent reports most real estate sales to the IRS on Form 1099-S, so escrow collects your details for it. Whether you owe tax on the sale is a question for your tax adviser; IRS Publication 523 covers selling a home.
