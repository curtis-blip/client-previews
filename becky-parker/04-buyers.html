Your First-Time Home Buyer Guide for Georgia.

From Athens to McDonough, eighteen years of walking buyers through every step of the process, plain English only.

Where We Start

First Time Home Buyer Georgia: The Honest Version.

Buying your first home in Georgia means navigating mortgage pre-approval, inspection contingencies, escrow timelines, and closing fees you've probably never heard of. Becky has been doing this since 2006, mostly with first-time buyers in Athens, Monroe, and McDonough. She picks up the phone, she reads the contract with you line by line, and she tells you the truth about a house before you write the offer. No hand-waving, no jargon.

The Process

Eight Steps From Pre-Approval to Keys.

Step 01

Getting Pre-Approved for a Georgia Mortgage.

Before you tour a single home, you need a pre-approval letter from a Georgia-licensed lender. The lender will pull your credit, verify your income, and lock in a debt-to-income ratio that tells us your real ceiling. Shop two or three lenders. Rate and fees vary more than people think.

Step 02

Defining Your Search.

We sit down with your pre-approval number and build a real budget: principal, interest, taxes, insurance, HOA if any. Then we line up the must-haves, the deal-breakers, and the school districts that matter. Commute math from Athens, Monroe, or McDonough into Atlanta is part of this conversation, not an afterthought.

Step 03

Touring Homes.

Showings in Northeast Georgia mostly run by appointment through ShowingTime. We tour in batches of three or four, with thirty minutes between, and Becky walks each house with you. Look at the roof line, the HVAC age, water stains on ceilings. Cosmetics like paint and carpet are noise. Bones are signal.

Step 04

Making the Offer.

An offer in Georgia is a binding contract once accepted. We set the price based on actual recent comps in that neighborhood, not the asking, and we attach earnest money (typically one percent of price). In multiple-offer situations we may use an escalation clause, capped at a number you've already approved.

Step 05

Under Contract.

Once the seller accepts, Georgia gives you a Due Diligence period (typically seven to fourteen days) where you can walk away for any reason and keep your earnest money. Your financing contingency and appraisal contingency run alongside it. In Georgia, the closing attorney handles title search and document prep, not a separate title company.

Step 06

Inspection and Appraisal.

We hire a licensed Georgia home inspector (Becky will recommend three, you pick). The inspection report becomes the basis for your repair request, due back to the seller before Due Diligence ends. Meanwhile your lender orders an appraisal. If the appraised value comes in low, we re-negotiate or you walk, contract intact.

Step 07

Final Walk-Through and Closing Prep.

Seventy-two hours before closing we walk the home one final time to confirm repairs are done and the property is in the agreed condition. Your closing attorney sends wire instructions. Verify those instructions by phone with the attorney's office directly, never trust an email. Wire fraud in Georgia real estate is real and irreversible.

Step 08

Closing Day.

Georgia is an attorney-state, meaning a licensed Georgia real estate attorney runs the closing table. You'll sign roughly forty pages: deed, mortgage note, closing disclosure, title affidavit. Becky sits next to you. Keys change hands once the deed is recorded with the county, usually the same afternoon.

Common Questions

Buyer FAQs.

How much do I need for a down payment in Georgia?

It depends on the loan. Conventional loans start at three percent down for qualified buyers, FHA at three and a half percent, VA at zero down for eligible veterans. The Georgia Dream program also offers down-payment assistance up to ten thousand dollars for first-time buyers who meet income limits. Becky will walk you through which fits your situation.

What credit score do I need to buy a home in Georgia?

FHA loans accept scores as low as 580 with three and a half percent down, and 500 with ten percent down. Conventional loans typically want 620 or higher, with the best rates at 740 plus. If your score sits in the low 600s, we have time to clean it up before pre-approval. A 20-point bump can move your interest rate noticeably.

Why does Georgia require an attorney at closing?

Georgia is one of twenty-two attorney-states, meaning state law requires a licensed Georgia real estate attorney to handle the closing, the title search, and the deed recording. The attorney represents the transaction itself, not buyer or seller specifically. You'll meet the attorney at closing; their fee is part of your closing costs and shows up on the Closing Disclosure.

What is earnest money, and is it refundable?

Earnest money is your good-faith deposit on the home, typically one percent of the purchase price, held in escrow by the closing attorney. It's refundable if you walk away during your Due Diligence period or if a contingency (financing, appraisal, inspection) fails. Once Due Diligence ends and you waive contingencies, the earnest money is at risk if you back out.

How long does the typical home purchase take?

From pre-approval to closing day, plan on forty-five to sixty days once you're under contract. The pre-approval and search portion is up to you (some buyers move in two weekends, some take six months). Once we have a signed contract, the timeline is mostly driven by your lender's underwriting speed and the closing attorney's calendar.

What's the difference between pre-qualified and pre-approved?

Pre-qualified means a lender did a quick conversation and gave you a rough number based on what you told them. Pre-approved means the lender pulled your credit, verified income with tax returns and pay stubs, and issued a letter committing to lend up to a specific amount. In Northeast Georgia, sellers expect pre-approval letters with offers. Pre-qualified will not get you to the front of the line.
