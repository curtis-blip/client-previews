Living in Whitewater

Ranch country where Highway 141 leaves Highway 50

Whitewater is a rural community on U.S. Highway 50, eight miles south of Grand Junction by the county plan's count, where State Highway 141 turns off toward Unaweep Canyon. It has no town government: Mesa County plans and governs it as one of six rural communities in the county.

The Whitewater Community Plan, written in 2007 and amended in 2011, covers about 18.5 square miles from Whitewater Hill south to Siminoe Road, with the Gunnison River on the west. Its historic core, about one square mile, is surrounded by BLM land, and the plan describes the area as ranching land and open BLM land, low density and rural.

01

On Highway 50, eight miles south of the city

U.S. Highway 50 runs straight through Whitewater, which the county's plan describes as bisected by the highway, with Grand Junction eight miles north and Montrose 50 miles south. The plan area ends at Whitewater Hill, where Orchard Mesa begins.

Whitewater has no town hall. Mesa County governs it under the Whitewater Community Plan, which in 2007 updated the 1999 Whitewater and Kannah Creek Plan and was amended in 2011.

02

The turn for Unaweep Canyon

State Highway 141 branches off US 50 at Whitewater, the plan's access point to the Unaweep-Tabeguache Scenic and Historic Byway. CDOT lists the byway at 133 miles on Highways 141 and 145, about three hours of driving, and the plan follows it to the historic mining camp of Placerville in San Miguel County.

Unaweep Canyon cuts through the Uncompahgre Plateau down to Precambrian rock, CDOT notes. The canyon saw a copper boom around the turn of the 20th century, and uranium ore from nearby Uravan was processed for the first atomic bombs. CDOT names the Hanging Flume, the Driggs Mansion and the Dominguez and Escalante Expedition marker among the byway's stops.

03

Ranch land with BLM all around

The county's plan describes the Whitewater area as ranching land and open BLM land, low density and rural, with the Gunnison River on its west edge. Its historic core of about one square mile is surrounded by BLM lands.

So a parcel here may back onto public land or a working ranch. Ask Dave where a property's lines fall and what sits beside it before you make an offer.

04

Dominguez-Escalante, south along the Gunnison

The BLM's Dominguez-Escalante National Conservation Area takes in the Dominguez Canyon Wilderness, recreation on the Gunnison River and a network of off-highway vehicle trails, with Big Dominguez and Potholes campgrounds for overnight stays.

For the east side, the BLM sends visitors south on US 50 past Whitewater to Bridgeport Road and the Bridgeport Bridge. For the north side and Dominguez Campground, turn onto Highway 141 at Whitewater and continue to the Tabeguache Trail turnoff.

05

Where Whitewater students go to school

The Whitewater plan says there are no public schools in the planning area. Students ride the public school bus to schools outside it.

Confirm the district and the campus for any address with District 51's boundary information before you settle on a home.

Questions

Whitewater FAQ

Is Whitewater a town?

No. Whitewater is an unincorporated rural community, one of six in Mesa County, and the county governs it under the Whitewater Community Plan, written in 2007 and amended in 2011.

Are there schools in Whitewater?

Not in the planning area, the county's plan says. Students ride the public school bus to schools outside it. Confirm the district and school for any address with District 51's boundary information.

Where does the Unaweep-Tabeguache Byway start?

At Whitewater, where State Highway 141 leaves US 50. CDOT lists the byway at 133 miles on Highways 141 and 145, about three hours of driving, and the county's plan follows it to the old mining camp of Placerville in San Miguel County.

How far is Whitewater from Grand Junction?

About 9 miles by road to downtown Grand Junction, roughly 15 minutes without traffic by OpenStreetMap routing. Orchard Mesa is about 6 miles north.
