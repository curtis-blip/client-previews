Buyer Education

Anna Maria Island
Rental Rules

What buyers need to know — because the rules are not island‑wide, and the differences change everything.

Cindy Quinn, P.A.
Broker Associate · CLHMS · 25+ Years on Anna Maria Island

The City of Anna Maria, Holmes Beach, Bradenton Beach, and the City of Bradenton each regulate vacation rentals differently. Buyers should never assume a property is short‑term‑rental friendly just because it is near the beach or on the island. Here is what you need to know, municipality by municipality.

City of Anna Maria

Anna Maria

Parcel-specific due diligence, not one-size-fits-all

In the City of Anna Maria vacation rental system, the clearest public‑facing rules focus on annual registration, inspections, occupancy, parking, and operational compliance. Owners are required to maintain a current Florida DBPR transient public lodging license, a Florida Department of Revenue registration, and a Manatee County tourist tax account, and the city requires supporting documentation to be submitted annually.

Key nuance — The ordinance expressly says the chapter is not intended to prohibit vacation rentals or regulate the duration or frequency of vacation rentals, and instead is aimed at life‑safety and neighborhood‑compatibility concerns. That is why Anna Maria properties should be treated as parcel‑specific due diligence deals, not "all the same" opportunities.

Operationally, Anna Maria is strict. Occupancy is limited to the lesser of two persons per bedroom and eight total occupants including day guests. The same ordinance requires one off‑street parking space per bedroom, prohibits vacation‑rental parking in the right‑of‑way, and sets quiet hours from 10:00 p.m. to 8:00 a.m.

City of Holmes Beach

Holmes Beach

Where zoning changes everything, street by street

If you want the clearest example of how zoning changes rental strategy, Holmes Beach is it. The city's official Vacation Rental Certificate page states that zoning districts determine your minimum rental period — and that distinction can dramatically affect revenue assumptions from one street to the next.

Zoning District Minimum Rental Period Classification
R-1 & R-1AA 30-day minimum Monthly
R-2, R-3 & R-4 7-day minimum Weekly
A-1 Daily rentals allowed Daily

Holmes Beach also requires a Vacation Rental Certificate and a city inspection process. Inspectors check for smoke detectors, CO detection where required, a working fire extinguisher, visible address numbers, pool alarms, and FEMA‑related ground‑level compliance in elevated homes.

Investor insight — Holmes Beach is one of the most attractive markets on Anna Maria Island, but it is also one of the easiest places to misread if you do not verify the exact zoning district first. A home in a weekly zone and a home in a 30‑day zone can produce very different numbers, even if they are close together.

City of Bradenton Beach

Bradenton Beach

Licensing first, then parcel-level zoning confirmation

The current Bradenton Beach short term rental regulations make clear that any unit rented more than three times per year for periods of less than 30 days or one calendar month, whichever is less, must be registered with the State of Florida and must obtain a city Transient Public Lodging Establishment license. That is the clearest city‑level starting point for buyers.

The zoning analysis in Bradenton Beach is more nuanced than many buyers realize. The city's Land Development Code shows that hotel, motel, and time‑sharing uses are permitted or specially permitted in parts of the C‑1 Limited Commercial, C‑2 General Commercial, and Mixed Use Bridge Commercial Overlay framework. That supports the idea that Bradenton Beach can be more tourism‑oriented than some surrounding residential areas, but it still does not mean every parcel has the same short‑term rental rights.

Safe takeaway — Bradenton Beach can be favorable for short‑term rental use in the right zoning context, but buyers should confirm the parcel's zoning, use category, and city licensing path before relying on projected rental income.

City of Bradenton

Bradenton

Clear operating framework with published occupancy standards

The City of Bradenton vacation rental program applies to properties rented more than three times per year for periods of less than 30 days. The city requires a Certificate of Registration for each short‑term vacation rental, along with a DBPR license, a Florida Department of Revenue registration, a Manatee County tourist tax account, and a City of Bradenton local business tax receipt.

Bradenton's rules are especially useful for buyers because the city publishes a clear operating framework. The registration guidance brochure requires a designated responsible party who is available 24/7 and able to respond to complaints within one hour.

The city publishes a specific maximum occupancy standard: the lesser of two persons per bedroom plus two additional persons, one person per 150 square feet of temperature‑controlled living space, or 12 persons total.

Parking note — Bradenton ties parking to permitted occupancy. One off‑street parking space is required for every four persons of permitted occupancy, and some older properties may end up with lower practical occupancy if parking is constrained.

Unincorporated Area

Manatee County

Site-specific answers only — no simple citywide rules here

For mainland properties outside city limits, buyers should not rely on city summaries. In unincorporated Manatee County, zoning and land‑use questions are handled through the county's Comprehensive Plan, Land Development Code, and Code of Ordinances. That makes the answer more site‑specific and less suited to a simple citywide rule of thumb.

Tax reminder — The county states that rentals of six months or less are subject to tourist tax, and it also states that it does not have collection agreements with platforms like Airbnb and Vrbo — so owners remain responsible for compliance.

Common Pitfalls

What Buyers Miss Most

Assuming Island‑Wide Rules

The biggest mistake buyers make is assuming that Anna Maria Island rental rules are the same in Anna Maria, Holmes Beach, and Bradenton Beach. They are not. Holmes Beach publishes the clearest zoning‑based minimum rental periods, Anna Maria publishes the clearest operational controls, and Bradenton Beach is best understood through licensing plus parcel‑specific zoning review.

Forgetting Private Restrictions

Even if city rules allow short‑term rentals, HOA rules, condo documents, deed restrictions, flood‑compliance issues, and parking limitations can still change whether a property works for your intended use. Holmes Beach's inspection guidance specifically flags ground‑level living‑space issues in elevated homes.

Before You Buy

Your Pre‑Purchase Checklist

Before you buy a vacation rental here, make sure you have confirmed each of the following.

  1. The exact municipality the property sits in
  2. The exact zoning district for that parcel
  3. The applicable minimum rental period
  4. Required city registration and inspection steps
  5. The property's DBPR, DOR, and Manatee tourist‑tax compliance path
  6. Actual occupancy and parking limits
  7. Any HOA, condo, or deed restrictions
  8. Any floodplain or FEMA‑related layout issues before you underwrite projected income
