Specie Mesa, Colorado

Homes for Sale on Specie Mesa, CO

Ranch parcels on a high mesa west of Placerville, and what changes from one side of Specie Creek to the other.

Specie Mesa at a glance

One mesa, two sets of districts

Specie Mesa rises southwest of Placerville, just west of Wilson Mesa, at about 9,160 to 9,600 feet. Specie Creek runs north across it to the San Miguel River, and the creek is where the mesa divides.

East of the creek, Specie Mesa Ranch and Specie Wilderness are in the Telluride Fire Protection District and mostly in Telluride School District R-1. West of it, the county maps the Peninsula subdivisions, Top of the World and Great American, where its layers show no fire protection district and Norwood School District R-2J, and where the Telluride district has proposed an inclusion. Downtown Telluride is about 25 road miles away, by Beef Trail Road and CO-145 through Placerville and Sawpit.

01

Specie Mesa Ranch and Specie Wilderness

East of Specie Creek, the county maps two ranch subdivisions, both zoned Forestry/Agriculture at its sample points and both in the Telluride Fire Protection District.

  • Specie Mesa RanchAbout 3,270 acres in 21 county parcel records, from about 5 to 478 acres with a median near 108. It borders BLM land and two conservation easements, of about 623 and 934 acres, held by the San Miguel Conservation Foundation, a Telluride land trust. A grid of sample points puts it about 97 percent in Telluride R-1 and 3 percent in Norwood R-2J.
  • Specie WildernessSeven parcels of about 40 to 110 acres, some 508 acres in all, at the south end of the mesa. It borders National Forest and sits at about 9,400 feet, in Telluride R-1 at a sample point.

02

West of Specie Creek

West of the creek the picture changes. The county maps six subdivisions there, and at the center of each, its fire layer shows no fire protection district and its school layer shows Norwood School District R-2J.

  • The Peninsula subdivisionsFour county-mapped subdivisions share the name: Peninsula, Peninsula Park, Peninsula Pines and Peninsula Point.
  • Top of the WorldA subdivision west of the creek that the Telluride fire district names in its inclusion proposal.
  • Great AmericanAlso named in the proposal, as an HOA the district says it already responds to.

All six lie inside the county's outline of a proposed 2025 addition to the Telluride Fire Protection District. The district's inclusion proposal would add about 130 square miles in all, and it is not final, so ask where it stands. The district's Station 3 is in Placerville, at 410 Front Street.

03

Beef Trail Road and the river

County Road M44, Beef Trail Road, leaves CO-145 near the mouth of Specie Creek, west of Placerville, and climbs onto the mesa. The county maintains it, but for its first 5.3 miles the winter plowing is private, and for about 10 miles farther west it is not plowed at all.

From Specie Mesa Ranch it is about 25 road miles to downtown Telluride, 24 to Telluride Regional Airport and 60 to Montrose Regional, up CO-145 past Placerville and Sawpit. Below the mesa, Recreation.gov lists the Specie Creek Boat Launch on the San Miguel River.

What Garrett would check on Specie Mesa

  • Fire districtTelluride Fire east of Specie Creek; west of it, no district is mapped and the inclusion is not final. Ask where it stands, and ask your insurer what it means for a policy.
  • School districtMostly Telluride School District R-1 east of the creek and Norwood School District R-2J to the west, and about 3 percent of Specie Mesa Ranch falls in R-2J too. Confirm the district for the parcel.
  • Water and septicThe EPA lists no community water system on the mesa, which proves nothing either way, so ask where a parcel's water comes from; a well's state permit file states what it may be used for. Septic systems under 2,000 gallons a day are permitted by the county's Environmental Health program.
  • Winter accessBeef Trail Road is privately plowed for about 5.3 miles and not plowed beyond. Ask who plows to the parcel and who pays, and drive it in winter before you buy.
  • Easements and zoningConservation easements and BLM land border Specie Mesa Ranch, and National Forest borders Specie Wilderness, so read the recorded easements. Zoning reads Forestry/Agriculture at the county's sample points; confirm a parcel with the Zone District Finder.

Questions

Specie Mesa FAQ

Which fire district covers Specie Mesa?

East of Specie Creek, the Telluride Fire Protection District. West of it, the county maps no district at the Peninsula, Top of the World and Great American subdivisions; the Telluride district has proposed including them and says it already responds there. Ask where the inclusion stands.

Which school district serves Specie Mesa?

Mostly Telluride School District R-1 east of Specie Creek, though about 3 percent of Specie Mesa Ranch falls in Norwood R-2J, and Norwood R-2J for the west-side subdivisions. Confirm any parcel with the districts.

How do you get to Specie Mesa in winter?

By Beef Trail Road, County Road M44, from CO-145. The county maintains it, but the winter plowing is private for about 5.3 miles, and the road is not plowed for about 10 miles beyond that.

How big are the parcels on Specie Mesa?

County records show 21 parcels in Specie Mesa Ranch, from about 5 to 478 acres with a median near 108, and seven in Specie Wilderness, from about 40 to 110 acres. Records can include common or open-space tracts.
