Your Complete Guide to Buying a Home in Brevard County

Every step of the process, from your first phone call to the moment you get the keys.

Buyer's Guide

10 Steps to Owning Your Space Coast Home

Buying a home on Florida's Space Coast is one of the most exciting decisions you will ever make. Whether you are drawn to the waterfront living in Merritt Island, the small-town character of Titusville, or the growing communities near Lake Nona, this guide walks you through every phase so you can move forward with clarity and confidence.

01

Define Your Goals

Before you start browsing listings, take time to think about what you actually need from your next home. Are you looking for a single-family house with a yard, or does a low-maintenance condo fit your lifestyle better? How far are you willing to commute? Do you need to be close to certain schools, a hospital, or the beach?

Brevard County stretches over 70 miles of coastline, and each community has its own personality. A buyer relocating from out of state will have very different priorities than someone moving across town. Getting clear on your non-negotiables early saves you weeks of looking at homes that were never going to work.

02

Understand Your Budget

Your maximum mortgage approval is not the same as what you should spend. A lender might approve you for $400,000, but your monthly comfort zone might be closer to $320,000 once you factor in everything else.

In Brevard County, property taxes average roughly 1% of assessed value. Florida homeowner's insurance has climbed significantly in recent years, so budget accordingly. If you are buying in a community with an HOA, those fees can range from $100 to $500+ per month depending on the amenities. Run the full numbers before you fall in love with a property.

03

Get Pre-Approved

There is a real difference between pre-qualification and pre-approval. Pre-qualification is a quick estimate based on what you tell the lender. Pre-approval involves a full review of your income, credit, and assets, and it results in a conditional commitment letter.

Sellers on the Space Coast take pre-approved buyers far more seriously, especially in competitive price ranges. Many listing agents will not even entertain an offer without one. If you are deciding between a national online lender and a local mortgage company, consider that local lenders often close faster and are easier to reach when questions come up mid-transaction.

04

Find the Right Agent

The right agent does more than open doors. A local Space Coast agent understands which neighborhoods flood during heavy storms, which communities have the strongest resale values, and which inspection issues are common in Florida construction (think: humidity damage, aging roofs, and older HVAC systems).

Your agent should also know how to navigate Florida's unique contract structure, including the "as-is" residential contract that many sellers prefer. Ask how many transactions they have closed in Brevard County, how they communicate, and what their strategy looks like when multiple offers are on the table.

05

Tour Homes and Neighborhoods

Photos only tell part of the story. Visit properties at different times of day to get a feel for traffic, noise, and how the neighborhood actually lives. A street that feels peaceful on a Tuesday morning might be backed up with school traffic at 3 PM.

In Brevard County, always check FEMA flood zone maps before making an offer. Flood insurance can add hundreds per month to your costs if the property sits in a high-risk zone. Also evaluate commute routes to Patrick Space Force Base, Kennedy Space Center, or wherever your workday takes you. The Indian River and Banana River crossings can create bottlenecks during peak hours.

06

Make a Strong Offer

Crafting a competitive offer is part strategy, part timing. Your agent will research comparable sales, analyze how long the property has been listed, and assess the seller's likely motivation. From there, you will decide on your offer price, earnest money deposit (typically 1-3% in Florida), and contingencies.

Florida uses standardized real estate contracts (FAR/BAR), and the terms you choose, including your inspection period, financing contingency timeline, and preferred closing date, all matter to the seller. In a multiple-offer situation, flexibility on closing date or a larger earnest money deposit can set your offer apart without simply throwing more money at the price.

07

Navigate Inspections

Florida inspections go beyond the standard home inspection. Expect to schedule a wind mitigation inspection (which can significantly reduce your insurance premium), a 4-point inspection covering roof, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC, and a termite/WDO (wood-destroying organism) inspection, which is practically mandatory in the Sunshine State.

Roof condition is one of the biggest factors in Florida real estate. Insurance companies have become increasingly strict about roof age, and many will not write a policy on a roof older than 15-20 years. Your inspector should document the roof's condition, remaining life expectancy, and any signs of storm damage. Everything that comes back in the inspection report becomes a negotiation tool.

08

Secure Your Financing

Once your offer is accepted, your lender will begin the formal underwriting process. This is when you will lock your interest rate and finalize your loan type. Conventional, FHA, and VA loans are all common in Brevard County, and the right choice depends on your down payment, credit profile, and whether you have military service.

If you are a first-time buyer, look into the Florida Housing Finance Corporation programs and the Brevard County Housing Finance Authority, which offers up to $15,000 in down payment and closing cost assistance for qualified buyers (minimum 640 FICO, maximum purchase price of $444,000). These programs can make a meaningful difference in how much cash you need at closing.

09

Close on Your New Home

Closing day in Florida typically takes place at a title company rather than an attorney's office (Florida is a "title state"). Your title company will handle the deed transfer, disburse funds, and ensure all liens are cleared. You will receive a closing disclosure at least three business days before the closing date, giving you time to review all the numbers.

Before closing, you will do a final walkthrough to confirm that the property is in the agreed-upon condition and that any repairs from inspection negotiations have been completed. Bring a valid photo ID and a cashier's check or wire transfer confirmation for your closing costs. Once everything is signed, the deed records with the Brevard County Clerk of Courts, and the keys are yours.

10

Move In and Enjoy

Congratulations. You are officially a Space Coast homeowner. The first few weeks will be a mix of excitement and logistics. Update your address with the post office, your bank, your insurance providers, and the DMV. Set up your utilities (Florida Power & Light, City of Titusville Water, and your preferred internet provider) as early as possible.

Take time to explore your new neighborhood. Introduce yourself to your neighbors. Check out the local farmers markets, the parks along the Indian River, and the unique rhythm of life that comes with living near the ocean and the space program. You picked the Space Coast for a reason. Now go enjoy it.
