Selling a House in Georgia

Eight steps from pricing to the closing table, with the taxes and paperwork Georgia puts on a seller.

Before You List

How Selling a House in Georgia Works

Selling a house in Georgia runs from a price to a contract to a closing that a licensed Georgia attorney handles.

These eight steps follow a Forsyth County sale in order, from the first number to the last signature.

Taxes and fees named here carry their source and their year, and they change.

This guide explains the process. It is not legal or tax advice. For questions about your contract, your deed or your taxes, ask a Georgia real estate attorney or a tax professional.

01

Step 1 of 8

Price the home

A price starts with what similar homes in your part of Forsyth County have sold for. Rhonda holds a certification in Data Science in Real Estate from MIT and uses market data to help sellers position a home from day one. Start with what your home is worth to see an estimate.

02

Step 2 of 8

Get the house ready to show

Fix what you already know is wrong, clear out clutter and tidy the yard and the porch. Buyers see the outside first.

03

Step 3 of 8

Complete the disclosure statement

The Georgia Real Estate Commission's guidance says sellers should complete a disclosure statement and must tell buyers about hidden defects they know of. Real estate brokers must also disclose material facts. Complete the statement, keep your repair records with it and ask your attorney what your contract requires.

04

Step 4 of 8

List the house and show it

Once the house is priced and ready, it goes on the market with photos and a description. Buyers and their agents book showings, and you decide on offers as they come in.

05

Step 5 of 8

Review offers and sign a contract

Compare each offer's price, closing date and conditions, not only the number at the top. The contract sets the inspection period, so read that clause closely and have your attorney review the contract before you sign.

06

Step 6 of 8

Handle the inspection and the appraisal

The buyer inspects the house during the period in the contract. What you agree to repair, and by when, follows the contract's wording. The buyer's lender completes its own review, including an appraisal.

07

Step 7 of 8

Plan for the transfer tax

The seller is liable for Georgia's real estate transfer tax, though contracts often shift it to the buyer. It is paid before the deed records. The seller taxes section has the rate and the return.

08

Step 8 of 8

Close with a Georgia attorney

Only a licensed Georgia attorney may close the sale or prepare the deed. If you close after January 1, email the closing statement to Tax@forsythco.com, and the buyer reapplies for the homestead exemption. The property tax guide explains the exemption.

Transfer Tax

Taxes a Georgia Seller May Owe at Closing

Georgia's real estate transfer tax is $1 for the first $1,000 of the sale price plus 10 cents for each added $100, as of 2026.

The seller is liable for it, though contracts often shift it to the buyer. The Georgia Department of Revenue transfer tax page has the current rule. On a Forsyth County sale, read your contract to see who pays.

The tax is paid before the deed records, and the PT-61 return is filed online with the Georgia Superior Court Clerks' Cooperative Authority (GSCCCA). Your attorney handles the closing, so ask which parts they file for you.

Questions

Quick Answers About Selling in Georgia

Do I have to complete a seller's disclosure form in Georgia?

No Georgia statute requiring a particular form was found while this guide was written. The Georgia Real Estate Commission's InfoBase says sellers should complete a disclosure statement and must tell buyers about hidden defects they know of. Ask your attorney what your contract requires.

Do I need an attorney to sell my house in Georgia?

Only a licensed Georgia attorney may close the sale or prepare the deed, so an attorney is part of every Georgia closing.

What happens to my homestead exemption when I sell?

The buyer applies for their own exemption. If you close after January 1, email the closing statement to Tax@forsythco.com.

I live outside Georgia. Does that change what I owe at closing?

It can. For nonresident sellers, Georgia requires 3% withholding on Form G2-RP, as of 2026. See the Georgia Department of Revenue Form G2-RP page, and ask your attorney or a tax professional how it applies to your sale.
