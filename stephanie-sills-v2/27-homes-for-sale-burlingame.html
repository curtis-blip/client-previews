Living Here

Living in Burlingame

Burlingame calls itself the City of Trees: more than 18,000 public trees shade its streets, and it has protected them since 1908, the year it became a city.

The land took its name in 1868 from Anson Burlingame, the American diplomat to China, and the town grew quickly after the 1906 earthquake, when hundreds of lots sold to people building new homes. Easton, next door, joined in 1910. Today Burlingame runs from the bay to the hills west of El Camino Real, with two downtowns on Burlingame Avenue and Broadway, Caltrain in the middle of town and SFO just to the north.

Burlingame Neighborhoods and Kinds of Homes

Most of the city's houses sit on tree-lined streets laid out in the first decades of the 1900s. Easton Addition was the town of Easton until it joined Burlingame in 1910; Burlingame Park and Lyon-Hoag are an easy walk from downtown; Mills Estates rises toward the canyon and the hills.

More than half of Burlingame's households rent, so beside the single-family streets you'll find apartment, condo and townhome buildings. The tall eucalyptus rows along El Camino Real are older than the city itself.

02

Burlingame Avenue, Broadway and the Station

Burlingame Avenue is the main downtown, with shops and restaurants between El Camino Real and California Drive. Broadway, about a mile north, is the second, with its own string of shops and places to eat.

Burlingame station still uses its 1894 depot, a Mission Revival building roofed with old tiles from Mission San Antonio de Padua and the San Mateo asistencia, and listed on the National Register of Historic Places. Broadway station runs on weekends only, and the city has chosen a plan that raises the tracks at Broadway without a station there.

03

Parks, the Canyon and the Bay

Washington Park is the city's big green: lawns, picnic groves and courts under the trees. Up the hill, Mills Canyon Wildlife Area keeps a wooded canyon of trails inside the city limits, and on the bay side the Shorebird Sanctuary and Bayside Fields sit along the shoreline.

The main library is on Primrose Road downtown, with a smaller branch on Easton Drive.

What to Check Before You Buy in Burlingame

  • Design reviewNew houses, most second-story additions and some single-story additions go to the Planning Commission for design review. Build the time into any remodel plan.
  • Protected treesPrivate trees 44 inches around or larger, measured 54 inches above the ground, are protected. Removing or heavily pruning one takes a city permit, and a replacement is often required.
  • Near BroadwayCaltrain plans to raise the tracks at Broadway, and construction could begin as soon as late 2028. Buying near California Drive and Broadway? Read the project page.
  • Flood zonesLow-lying blocks near the creeks and the bay can sit in a flood zone. Look the parcel up on FEMA's flood maps.
  • Your new tax billProperty tax resets to the price you pay, and San Mateo County sends a supplemental bill for the rest of the fiscal year.

Questions

Burlingame Real Estate Questions

Is Burlingame expensive to live in?

What you spend depends most on the home and the street. Beyond the price, plan for property tax based on what you pay and, for condos and townhomes, association dues. For current prices, see the searches above or Stephanie's market reports.

Is Burlingame or San Mateo nicer?

They suit different buyers. Burlingame is smaller, with two compact downtowns, one public high school and SFO next door. San Mateo is about three times the size, with more neighborhoods, a bigger downtown and three Caltrain stations. Stephanie works in both and can walk you through each.

Which school district serves Burlingame?

Burlingame School District runs the public schools from kindergarten through eighth grade: six elementary schools and Burlingame Intermediate. Burlingame High School is part of the San Mateo Union High School District. Boundaries can change, so confirm any address with the districts.

How far is Burlingame from San Francisco?

Downtown San Francisco is about 17 miles north on US-101, and SFO sits just past the city's northern edge. Caltrain from Burlingame station runs to San Francisco and San Jose, and BART starts at Millbrae, one town north.
