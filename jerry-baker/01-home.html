How I work

Two different problems up here

Both turn on the same thing: whether your broker knows what a mountain property does when nobody has a clean comparable for it.

Buying

Buying up here asks different questions

A mountain purchase turns on things a Denver contract never mentions. Whether the well is permitted. Whether the road gets plowed. Whether a lender will finance raw land at nine thousand feet. I answer those before you write an offer, not after.

  • Well permits, water rights and septic condition
  • Winter access, county plowing and easements
  • Wildfire mitigation and what insurers now require
  • Lenders who actually close on cabins and land

Selling

Scarce comparables are the whole problem

Another firm told the Beavers there were no usable comparables in Gilpin County. I built a full comparable analysis over that weekend. Pricing land and cabins is not pulling three neighbours off a subdivision street, and a listing that does not explain access, water and utilities will sit.

  • Hand built comparables where the data is thin
  • Listing copy that answers access and utility questions up front
  • Marketing aimed at Denver buyers, who are most of them

Where I work

Eight towns, and they do not trade alike

Gilpin County, Clear Creek and the Jefferson County foothills. What a property is worth here depends on which side of a pass it sits on, so start with the town.

Nederland

A trading post since 1874, now the way into Indian Peaks and Rocky Mountain National Park. Barker Meadow Reservoir sits at the edge of town, and the recreation access is why most buyers look here first.

Homes in Nederland

Rollinsville

Established 1871, a small cluster of homes and a few businesses on Highway 119 between Nederland and Black Hawk. The road runs all the way up to Rollins Pass at the summit of the Front Range.

Homes in Rollinsville

Golden

A Gold Rush town from 1859, now home to Colorado School of Mines and Coors, fifteen miles west of Denver. The most liquid market I work in, and the one that behaves most like the metro.
