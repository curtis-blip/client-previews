Get Your Bearings in Arvada

Arvada was named and platted decades before it incorporated: the formal notice of the new town, population 100, was posted on December 1, 1870, and the city’s Articles of Incorporation date from 1904. Its historic downtown, Olde Town, is on the National Register of Historic Places.

Arvada From Olde Town to Two Ponds

Pick a stop and the map follows along: Noah’s video from the spot where he has filmed it, and the homes for sale around it.

Map data: U.S. Census Bureau. Choose a numbered pin or a stop above. The dotted loop is an order to see them in, not a road.

Stop 1

Olde Town Arvada

Start downtown. The city calls Olde Town historic, and the Arvada Historical Society keeps its archive at the McIlvoy House, 7307 Grandview Avenue. A few facts from the city:

  • Listed on the National Register of Historic Places
  • One of three Arvada stops on RTD’s G Line
  • The formal notice of the town, population 100, was posted December 1, 1870

Stop 2

Arvada Center for the Arts and Humanities

The Arvada Center is at 6901 Wadsworth Boulevard. What it offers, from its own site:

  • Theatre, galleries and classes
  • 2.5 miles north of the I-70 and Wadsworth exit
  • Free parking on site

Stop 3

Majestic View Nature Center

The city runs this nature center at 7030 Garrison Street. The city’s list of what is there:

  • Nearly 2 miles of nature trails through wetland and prairie
  • A paved Interpretive Trail and a mulch trail to the Oberon Lake overlook
  • Exhibits, a kids’ activity area, classrooms and public programs

Stop 4

Two Ponds National Wildlife Refuge

The refuge at 9210 West 80th Avenue is run by the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and is free to visit.

  • 3 miles of crushed gravel and dirt trails through mixed prairie grasses
  • Free nature programs, guided hikes and pollinator programs

Before you buy

What to Check on Any Arvada Address

Four things that change from one side of town to the other. Each takes a few minutes to look up and is worth doing before you write an offer.

Five Hidden Costs Before Moving to Northern Colorado

12:37 · Costs

  1. Which county is it in?

    Arvada is a two-county city, Jefferson and Adams. The city’s property page links both county assessors, so use the one for the parcel, starting with the Jefferson County property records search.

  2. Which schools go with the address?

    Jeffco Public Schools covers nearly all of the city, and a small part is in Westminster Public Schools. Jeffco’s School Finder, on its enrollment page, shows neighborhood schools by address. Confirm the campus with the district before you write an offer.

  3. Is it in a floodplain?

    The city’s Floodplain Management page says a floodplain development permit may be needed for a property in a floodplain. It links an interactive 100 year floodplain map, FEMA and non-FEMA, and FEMA’s Map Service Center.

  4. Who provides water and sewer, and is there a special district?

    Most addresses are served by the City of Arvada, but a few areas inside the city are served by other water and sewer districts, such as Ralston Valley, Clear Creek Sanitation and North Table Mountain Water. The city’s PDF maps show which, and the state’s special district map covers metro and other districts.

Quick answers

Arvada Questions Buyers Ask

What county is Arvada in?

Arvada spans two counties, Jefferson and Adams. Check the parcel with the right county assessor; the city’s property page links both.

Which school district serves Arvada?

Jefferson County Public Schools covers nearly all of Arvada, and a small part is in Westminster Public Schools. The address decides, and Jeffco’s School Finder shows neighborhood schools.

Is Arvada’s water provided by the city?

For most addresses, yes. The city says a few areas inside its boundaries are served by other water and sewer districts, so confirm the provider for the address.

What is Olde Town Arvada?

It is the city’s historic downtown, listed on the National Register of Historic Places, with one of three Arvada stops on RTD’s G Line. The town was first noticed on paper on December 1, 1870.
