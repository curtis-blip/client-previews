The actual specialty

Mining claims, jeep road parcels, and lots nobody else will list

Read Jerry's reviews and a pattern shows up fast. Vacant land in Black Hawk. An off grid mining claim. Three and a third acres up Russell Gulch reachable only by an unmaintained jeep road. A steep lot on South Boulder Creek that needed one specific buyer who wanted the fishing more than the building envelope.

Those are not easy transactions and most agents decline them. They involve well permits, septic feasibility, legal access, mineral rights, and the occasional conversation with the Forest Service. Jerry has been doing them for sixteen years.

Ask Black Hawk Real Estate

The questions that actually come up when somebody is looking at high country ground for the first time.

Two different questions. An existing well needs a permit on file with the Colorado Division of Water Resources, and the permit type controls what you are allowed to use the water for. On raw ground you are asking whether a household use permit is available for that parcel, which depends on the aquifer and the basin. Jerry checks this before you write an offer, not after.
A road existing on the ground is not the same as a road you have the right to use. Some Gilpin County parcels are reached by easements across neighboring land, some by county roads that are not maintained in winter, and a few by routes that were never recorded at all. This is the single most common reason a mountain lot is cheap.
A patented mining claim is private land, granted out of the federal estate, and it can generally be built on subject to county zoning. An unpatented claim is a right to minerals on land the federal government still owns, and it is not a building site. The two get advertised in similar language and they are not remotely the same purchase.
