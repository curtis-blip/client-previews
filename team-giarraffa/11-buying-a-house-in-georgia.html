Buyer's Guide

Buying a House in Georgia

Eight steps from your first lender call to closing day, with examples from Forsyth County.

Start Here

How a Georgia Home Purchase Works

Buying a house in Georgia follows a set order, and a licensed Georgia attorney handles the closing.

The eight steps here run in the order a Forsyth County purchase takes. Louis and Rhonda Giarraffa work from the Century 21 Results office in Cumming, so the examples come from Forsyth County.

This guide explains the process. It is not legal advice. For questions about your contract, your deed or your closing, ask a Georgia real estate attorney.

01

Step 1 of 8

Decide where to look and what to spend

Pick the parts of Forsyth County you want to see and a monthly payment you are comfortable with. Count property tax in that payment: the Forsyth County property tax guide shows who sets each part of the bill.

02

Step 2 of 8

Get preapproved with a lender

A lender looks at your income, credit and savings and tells you how much you can borrow. Ask if you qualify for help with the down payment: the first-time buyer programs guide covers Georgia Dream, Peach Plus and Peach Advantage.

03

Step 3 of 8

Search for homes

Search Forsyth County homes on Home Search, then ask Louis or Rhonda to set up a tour. Homes on the water raise dock and permit questions, and the Lake Lanier buyer's guide on their lake site covers both.

04

Step 4 of 8

Tour the short list

See the homes you like in person. Look at the lot, the roof and the street, and ask how old the major systems are. Take photos and notes so you can compare the houses afterward.

05

Step 5 of 8

Make an offer and sign the contract

Your offer becomes a contract when both sides sign. Earnest money is not required for a Georgia contract to be binding. When a contract calls for it, the contract says who holds it. Whether to ask for it, and how much, is a policy decision the broker makes with the seller's wishes in mind, according to the Georgia Real Estate Commission. Have your attorney read the contract before you sign.

06

Step 6 of 8

Inspect the house during the due diligence period

Your contract sets how long you have to inspect the house, and that window ends on a date. Hire an inspector as soon as the contract is signed. The due diligence section explains where the period comes from.

07

Step 7 of 8

Finish the loan and the final checks

Your lender completes its review, including the appraisal. Ask for an itemized list of what you will owe at closing; the closing costs section names the usual charges. Walk through the house once more before you sign.

08

Step 8 of 8

Close with a Georgia attorney and collect the keys

Only a licensed Georgia attorney may close the sale or prepare the deed, so your closing runs through an attorney. You sign the closing papers, and the keys are yours. If the home is in Forsyth County, apply for the homestead exemption once you move in; the property tax guide lists the dates.

At the Closing Table

Closing Costs in Georgia

Closing costs on a Georgia purchase come from a short list of charges, and the amounts depend on your loan and your contract.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau lists what comes with a mortgage in its guide to mortgage costs:

  • Lender origination charges
  • Discount points
  • The appraisal
  • Title insurance (an owner's policy is optional)
  • Recording and government fees
  • Prepaid interest
  • The first year of homeowner's insurance
  • Initial escrow deposits

Two Georgia taxes also come up at a closing. The intangible recording tax is $1.50 for each $500, or fraction of $500, of the loan note, up to a cap of $25,000. The lender pays it and may pass it on to you. The Georgia Department of Revenue intangible recording tax page has the current rule.

The real estate transfer tax is $1 for the first $1,000 of the sale price plus 10 cents for each added $100. The seller is liable for it, though contracts often shift it to the buyer. See the Georgia Department of Revenue transfer tax page. Both rates are as of 2026 and can change.

On a Forsyth County purchase, ask your lender which of these charges apply to your loan, and ask your attorney to go through the closing statement with you before you sign.

Inspection Window

The Due Diligence Period

The due diligence period is the time your contract gives you to inspect the house. Its length comes from the contract you sign.

The inspection clause gives you the right to inspect for a specified time. Read the number of days and the end date before you sign, because the period has an end date.

On a Forsyth County purchase, use that time to hire a home inspector and read the report. What you can do if the inspection finds a problem, and by when, depends on the wording of your contract. Your attorney can explain it.

Questions

Quick Answers About Buying in Georgia

Do I need an attorney to buy a house in Georgia?

Yes, an attorney is part of the closing. Only a licensed Georgia attorney may close a sale or prepare the deed. An attorney can also answer legal questions about your contract, which this guide cannot.

Do I have to pay earnest money in Georgia?

Earnest money is not required for a binding contract. If your contract includes it, the contract says who holds the money, and the amount is a broker's policy decision that takes the seller's wishes into account. The Georgia Real Estate Commission FAQ has more.

Does Georgia require a seller's disclosure form?

We found no Georgia statute that requires a particular form. The Georgia Real Estate Commission's InfoBase says sellers should complete a disclosure statement and must tell buyers about hidden defects they know of, and that brokers must disclose material facts. Ask for the statement early and have your attorney read it.

Can a Georgia program help with my down payment?

Some can. Georgia Dream, run by the Georgia Department of Community Affairs, offers down payment help through participating lenders, and its limits change. The first-time buyer programs page has the details.
