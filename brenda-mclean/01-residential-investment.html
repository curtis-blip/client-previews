Single-Family Rentals

Two to four units can spread rent across more than one lease. Attention goes to unit condition, utility setup, current leases and how a lender will treat the property.

Metro Atlanta Counties for Residential Investors

Metro Atlanta gives residential investors a wide choice of locations and home styles, and each county moves at its own pace. Cobb

Marietta, Smyrna, Kennesaw and Acworth anchor Cobb County. Home styles and HOA rules shift from one neighborhood to the next, so each address gets its own review.

Woodstock, Holly Springs and Canton line the I-575 corridor. Comparing subdivision rules and upkeep costs helps sort one Cherokee County home from another.

DeKalb

DeKalb County takes in Decatur, Tucker, Dunwoody and Stone Mountain. Its cities can set their own local rules, so rental requirements are checked address by address.

Lawrenceville, Duluth, Snellville and Suwanee are among the Gwinnett County cities to compare. Community dues and leasing rules are part of every review.

Frequently Asked Questions

Explore these quick answers to common questions about buying residential investment property in Metro Atlanta. If you do not see your question here, send Brenda a message for a clear, simple reply that fits your situation.

What types of residential property can I invest in?

Common choices include single-family rentals, townhomes, condos, duplexes to fourplexes and homes that need renovation. The right fit depends on your budget, how hands-on you want to be and how long you plan to hold the property.

How do I compare one rental property with another?

Start with realistic numbers: likely rent, property taxes, insurance, HOA dues, repairs and the time between tenants. Laying those out side by side shows what each property asks of you. Rents and costs are estimates, and no investment return is guaranteed.

Can I rent out a townhome or condo?

It depends on the community. Many HOAs limit or cap leasing, so read the covenants and rules before you go under contract, and confirm with the association how they apply to that unit.

How much money do I need to buy an investment property?

It depends on the price, the property type and the loan. Lenders often set different down payment, rate and reserve requirements for investment property than for a home you live in, so speak with a lender early. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau explains how mortgages work.

Should I manage a rental myself or hire a property manager?

Either can work. Managing it yourself means handling showings, repairs and leases, while a manager charges a fee and takes on the daily tasks. Both must follow fair housing law and Georgia's landlord and tenant rules, which the Georgia Landlord-Tenant Handbook explains.

How is rental income taxed?

Rental income and expenses have their own federal rules, which IRS Publication 527 covers. A tax professional can tell you how they apply to your situation.
