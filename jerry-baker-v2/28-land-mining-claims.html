The specialty

Mining claims for sale in Colorado, and the land around them

Patented claims, gulch acreage, off grid parcels and mountain lots across Gilpin County, from the broker who has been closing them since 2010.

Searching for mining claims for sale in Colorado turns up a lot of listings and very little explanation. The most important thing to understand before you spend money is that two properties advertised in almost identical language can be completely different purchases.

Gilpin County was one of the first places in Colorado where gold was found, in 1859, and the ground above Black Hawk and Central City has been divided, patented, abandoned and re-recorded ever since. Jerry Baker has sold parcels out of that history for sixteen years, including an off grid claim, gulch acreage reachable only by jeep road, and creek lots that suited one buyer and nobody else.

The distinction that matters most

Patented and unpatented claims are not the same purchase

Get this one wrong and you have bought something you cannot build on.

Patented claim

Private land. At some point the federal government issued a patent transferring full title out of the public estate, usually more than a century ago. You own the surface, and often the minerals with it.

  • Can generally be built on, subject to county zoning and septic feasibility
  • Financeable and insurable like other real property
  • Boundaries follow the original survey, not section lines, so plats look unusual

Unpatented claim

Not private land. It is a right to extract minerals on ground the federal government still owns, maintained by annual filings. Buying one buys that right, nothing more.

  • Not a building site. A residence is not an authorised use
  • Lapses if the annual maintenance filings are not kept current
  • Conventional lenders and title companies will generally not treat it as real estate

Status is checkable. The BLM land and mineral records system holds patent and claim filings, and the Gilpin County Assessor will show whether a parcel is on the tax roll as private property. Jerry checks both before an offer goes in.

Before you buy

Four questions to answer on any claim or parcel

Is it patented?

Establish this first, from the BLM record and the county tax roll rather than from the listing description. Everything else depends on the answer.

Can you legally reach it?

Recorded access, an easement across a neighbour, or a route nobody ever wrote down. Old claim ground has the worst access records of anything up here.

Is there water available?

An existing well needs a permit on file, and a new one needs a permit to be available in that basin. A parcel without either has a hard ceiling on what it can become.

Who owns the minerals?

In mining country the minerals were often severed from the surface a century ago. Owning the ground does not always mean owning what is under it. Read the title exceptions.
